Learning objectives

  • Explain the roles of OR, Orco and GCaMP in the AeroSense sensing chain.
  • Predict what happens when one required component is removed.
  • Recognize why containment and biosafety matter for a living sensor.

Build a sensing pathway

AeroSense’s sensing design places insect OR/Orco proteins in HEK293 cells and uses GCaMP to report calcium changes as fluorescence.

Follow the information from molecular recognition to optical measurement.

Measurement

Recognition happens at the receptor. Measurement happens after a reporter converts that event into a signal.

Parts contains the construct maps; Experiments provides the laboratory methods.

Three molecules, three jobs

OR recognizes odorant. Orco partners with insect ORs so the receptor can function as a ligand-gated ion channel in this heterologous plan. GCaMP reports a calcium change as fluorescence.

mCherry, when used, is an expression marker — a way to see that a construct is present — not the odor-to-light conversion. A photodiode reads light outside the cell. An AI classifier, if used, sits even later, in computation.

Published insect-receptor studies informed the sensing architecture.

Build the sensor

Assemble the biological sensing chain and place the detector and classifier in their layers. Select a part, then a zone, or drag with a pointer.

HEK293, OR, Orco, and GCaMP belong in the cell. The photodiode belongs in readout, outside the cell. The AI classifier belongs in computation. mCherry can ride in the cell as an expression reporter; it is not required for the odor-to-fluorescence conversion.

Conceptual assembly — not experimental AeroSense data and not a plasmid map.

Check your understanding

What is the biological sensing element in AeroSense’s design?

  • Living HEK293 cells that express insect OR/Orco proteins and a calcium reporter.
  • A photodiode that binds odorant molecules in place of a receptor.
  • An AI classifier that is itself the living sensor.
Answer and explanation

Living HEK293 cells that express insect OR/Orco proteins and a calcium reporter.

The design combines HEK293 cells, insect OR/Orco proteins and a GCaMP reporter. Optical readout and computation follow the biological sensing step.

What role does GCaMP play in this sensing chain?

  • It binds odorants instead of the olfactory receptor.
  • It reports a calcium change as fluorescence, which can then be measured optically.
  • It legally certifies food as safe once it glows.
Answer and explanation

It reports a calcium change as fluorescence, which can then be measured optically.

GCaMP is a genetically encoded calcium indicator. In this design it converts a Ca²⁺ change that can follow receptor activation into a fluorescence readout. It does not bind the odorant in place of OR, and a glow is not a food certificate.

Why is containment necessary for a living odor sensor?

  • Engineered living cells should stay physically isolated from food, users, and the open environment.
  • Containment is optional if the cells look healthy under a microscope.
  • Containment exists so the AI can smell the room from outside the laboratory.
Answer and explanation

Engineered living cells should stay physically isolated from food, users, and the open environment.

HEK293 cells engineered to express heterologous receptors are living genetically modified material. They belong behind a sealed cartridge or other containment concept, with responsible handling and waste. They should not contact the food being screened.
