Safety & Security

Safety by Design. Evidence by Verification.

AeroSense treats biological containment, chemical handling, hardware protection, measurement integrity, responsible interpretation, and deployment boundaries as parts of the same safety system. A control that appears in a schematic is not, by itself, a verified result.

Current deployment boundary · iGEM 2026

Laboratory contained-use only

During the 2026 iGEM project, the team does not deploy live engineered-cell cartridges to food facilities or other non-laboratory environments. Current work is a laboratory prototype. A future conceptual cartridge, and any later commercial deployment, are separate layers. Future cartridge deployment requires additional containment validation, leak/failure testing, transport and disposal planning, regulatory determination, a defined user workflow, and real-world validation. A sealed concept is not an approved product.

This is the documented project scope on this wiki. It is not a published institutional authorization letter, and it is not a field-certified device claim. Hardware, IHP, and this notice use HEK293T; Experiments and the notebook currently name HEK293. The host line is not frozen here.

Our safety case

Safety follows the same chain as the instrument: Sense → Read → Decode → Act. This is the functional system architecture, not the team workstream hierarchy. Full Hazard → Possible consequence → Control → Verification → Status rows live only in the Risk register; each layer below is a compact index into those IDs. A control that appears in a schematic is not, by itself, a verified result.

  1. Hazard What can go wrong in biology, chemistry, hardware, data, or use.
  2. Possible consequence Who or what could be harmed, including a misleading measurement.
  3. Control Design, administrative, or interpretive barrier specified in team documents.
  4. Verification A test, inspection, or dated record — not a schematic by itself.
  5. Decision Continue, redesign, restrict, or stop until evidence exists.

Select a layer for a compact summary and register links. Without scripting, every layer summary remains below.

The four-node selector is an enhancement. Sense, Read, Decode, and Act summaries remain in the page source in order; full hazard rows are in the Risk register.

Safe to build

Safe construction of AeroSense depends on biological containment, safer organism and part choices, chemical exposure control, equipment practice, and waste decontamination. Ordinary laboratory controls are marked Implemented where team documents describe them in use. Formal White List, Check-In, and authorization records remain a separate 2026 rules check.

  • Organism and part choices Laboratory K-12 cloning, a human-origin culture host, chemically synthesised receptor sequences, and standard selection markers. Final status must be checked against the current 2026 iGEM Safety and Security rules and official team submissions.
  • Chemical exposure Concentrated VOC, agonist, and solvent stocks are the higher exposure risk. Fume-hood stock work is in use. Exact identities remain a planned / candidate panel.
  • Equipment practice Autoclave and sharps handling follow host-lab practice. Transfection method (electroporation versus lipid reagents) is not frozen.
  • Waste decontamination Autoclave and chemical-waste routes are documented laboratory practice. Contractor pickup records are not published.

Safe to measure

An instrument can be biologically contained and still be unsafe if it produces a technically misleading result.

This is AeroSense’s Read-layer safety contribution: power, light, containment at the hardware interface, and a high-impedance analog front end are safety controls, not only engineering details. A protection feature in a datasheet or schematic is designed protection. A dated measurement is tested evidence. They are not the same.

High-impedance measurement integrity

The transimpedance amplifier uses a very high feedback resistance (LMP7721-class, RF = 100 MΩ as a starting point). At that impedance, picoampere-scale leakage looks like photocurrent. Moisture, contamination, flux residue, and unguarded leakage can therefore become a false biological signal — which is why they are safety controls, not only analog hygiene.

  • Moisture Surface films create leakage paths that the TIA will amplify.
  • Contamination Board dirt or sample splash can inject current that is not fluorescence.
  • Flux residue Ionic residue on a high-Z node is a designed-in false-current source until cleaned and verified.
  • PCB leakage Specified short high-impedance traces. No leakage figure is published. Dark measurement is pending.
  • Guard design Specified guarding is both a measurement-quality and a safety barrier. Presence in layout is not a dark-current result.
  • Saturation TIA or ADC clipping can look like a peak or a null. A saturation flag is specified so a clipped sample is invalid, not classified.

Dark calibration and saturation/QC detection are specified with guarding and cleanliness. Hardware validation-ladder dark and dynamic-range tests remain Planned / pending evidence.

Safe to interpret

Decode and Act are safety. A cautious electrical design can still cause harm if a number is worded as biology, food clearance, or a legal certificate. Until sufficient real-world validation exists, AeroSense is described as a proposed early-screening / risk-indication architecture, not as a standalone confirmatory food-safety diagnostic.

Measurement safety principle If AeroSense cannot establish that a measurement is technically trustworthy, the system should not convert it into a confident biological conclusion.

Did the reader report a change above its own display threshold?

Does not mean the measurement is valid. Does not mean a food-safety condition is confirmed.

Question 02 · empty square

Was the measurement technically valid?

Did dark baseline, calibration, saturation, and SNR checks pass?

Does not mean a pathogen, mycotoxin, or legal limit was identified. A valid number can still be the wrong question.

Question 03 · dashed square

Has a food-safety condition been confirmed?

Has an independent confirmatory method established a food-safety fact?

AeroSense, as documented here, does not answer Q3. A screening band can only guide follow-up. This remains a proposed early-screening / risk-indication architecture.

Decision tree

Walk the YES path downward. Every NO is a stop: do not skip ahead to a food-safety sentence.

  1. Q1 · Signal detected?

    If yes → Continue to validity. A detection is only Q1.

    If no — stop Not a detection. That is still not a food-safety clearance and not a “safe to eat” statement.

  2. Q2 · Measurement technically valid?

    Passed calibration, dark baseline, saturation, and SNR checks?

    If yes → The number is technically usable as a risk indication input. It is not Q3.

    If no — stop Invalid. Apply the measurement safety principle: do not convert this sample into a confident biological conclusion.

  3. Risk indication

    Early-screening language only: a pattern that may warrant attention. Not a mycotoxin concentration, not a legal certificate, not a clinical result.

    Next → Ask Q3 separately. Do not let this box absorb Q3.

  4. Q3 · Food-safety condition confirmed?

    Would require → Independent confirmatory laboratory testing and validation this wiki does not claim.

    On this project — not confirmed Retest and recommend confirmatory testing. High-risk or invalid outputs should not be worded as reassurance.

False negative and false positive

If this architecture were used for food-risk indication, the two failure directions harm different people. Neither rate is published. Both are why Q1–Q3 must stay separate.

False negative

A missed real risk, including an invalid measurement displayed as “clear.” The consequence is inappropriate reassurance — the most serious communication failure for an early-warning concept.

False positive

A noisy or non-specific signal treated as a confirmed hazard. The consequence is unnecessary isolation, retesting, or disposal of food or produce.

Safety barriers in the output

Specified so the interface cannot collapse to a single class label. Implementation and biological validation of these barriers remain pending.

  • Calibration status A number without a current calibration is not Q2-valid.
  • Dark baseline No stable dark gate → no confident biological conclusion.
  • SNR Low signal-to-noise stays in the record; it is not silently classified.
  • Saturation A clipped channel is invalid, not a peak and not a null result.
  • Confidence Screening language should carry uncertainty, not a silent “safe.”
  • Invalid flag The principle, as a field: do not emit biology from a failed Q2.
  • Retest criteria A screening band can require another run. It cannot finish Q3.
  • Confirmatory testing High-risk or invalid outputs should recommend laboratory confirmation.

Evidence-claim integrity

Survey answers, interviews, literature, and this project’s own assays are different kinds of evidence. They are not interchangeable, and they do not become a food-safety confirmation by being placed on the same page.

  • Reported to us Survey responses and interview comments. Named quotations or identifiable professional comments are published only where explicit permission for attribution has been obtained.
  • Inferred by the team Design choices and regulatory hypotheses, including Taiwan device-class notes. Inference is not a legal determination and not an experimental result.
  • Literature Published receptor, VOC, or toxicology sources. Literature is not an AeroSense measurement.
  • Experimentally validated Results from this project’s own assays. Current data remain preliminary. A proposed early-screening / risk-indication architecture is not field validation.

Biosecurity and dual use

AeroSense currently has low but non-zero dual-use relevance. Its modular receptor architecture is useful precisely because sensing targets can change; therefore expansion to broader pathogen-associated VOC detection or other sensitive applications should trigger renewed review. The assessment is proportional: common laboratory chassis, insect olfactory receptor genes, VOC research stocks, and a reconfigurable sensing architecture in contained use — not a weaponization narrative.

Intended capability
Contained heterologous expression of insect OR/Orco with a fluorescence reporter, plus a weak-fluorescence reader, aimed at plant/fungal VOC risk indication. Assay VOCs are research reagents in a laboratory, not a release pathway.
Potential alternative use
The same modular idea — swap the receptor, keep the readout — could, in principle, be pointed at other volatiles, including pathogen-associated markers discussed in literature and IHP exploration (for example indole as an E. coli marker). Plasmid stocks, engineered mammalian lines, and concentrated VOC stocks are dual-use-relevant materials in the ordinary laboratory sense. This page does not provide a how-to for uncontained or harmful deployment.
Current misuse relevance
For the documented iGEM 2026 scope — laboratory contained-use, no extra-lab live-cell cartridge, no confirmatory diagnostic claim — present misuse potential is assessed as low but non-zero. That assessment is not a security clearance and not a claim that a pathway is absent.
Controls / boundaries
Specified contained-use; extra-lab live cells Restricted; institutional access control for cells and plasmids as host-lab practice (SOP not cited here); public language that refuses field, clinical, and food-certificate readiness; documentation that emphasizes methods for contained research rather than actionable misuse detail. Access-control logs are not published.
Trigger for re-review
Renew dual-use review before: expanding into broader pathogen-associated VOC detection as an intended product; general-purpose receptor engineering offered as a swap-in library for arbitrary targets; extra-laboratory living-cell cartridges; or any documentation that would function as a deployment how-to. Exploring additional VOC targets in literature does not authorize those applications and does not make dual-use vanish.

Re-review is the control for growth. Low present relevance is compatible with a real trigger later. Do not wait for a dramatic scenario. A scope change is enough.

AeroSafe — a safety-case framework for living biosensors

AeroSafe is a framework this team developed from AeroSense’s own laboratory-prototype work. It is not an established community standard, not an iGEM Safety Committee method, and not a claim that other teams must adopt it. It is a reusable way to keep engineered cells, a future cartridge, an optical/electronic reader, computational interpretation, and a real-world decision workflow from collapsing into a single “safe / unsafe” sentence. iGEM · Safety and Security Policies ↗ (opens in a new tab)

Core structure

Every material hazard on this page is asked the same six questions. Status is never inferred from a schematic. Deployment is a separate decision from containment design.

  1. Hazard What can go wrong in biology, chemistry, optics, electronics, data, or use.
  2. Possible consequence Who or what could be harmed — including a person who trusts a misleading number.
  3. Control The barrier specified in design or practice. Naming a part is not the same as showing it works.
  4. Verification A test, inspection, or dated record. Datasheets and drawings stay in this box until a result exists.
  5. Status Verified, Implemented, Planned, or Restricted — text, shape, and color together. Never color alone.
  6. Deployment decision Continue in the lab, redesign, restrict, or stop. A sealed-cartridge concept is not permission to leave the laboratory.

Why a living biosensor needs this chain

A food-risk indicator that uses live engineered cells can fail in the cartridge, the reader, the software, or the decision workflow — the worked Hazard → Consequence → Control → Verification example for this project is the Risk register, not a second parallel narrative.
