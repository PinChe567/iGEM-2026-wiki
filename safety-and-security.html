Safety & Security

Risk-aware design from cells to deployment

This page records biosafety, biosecurity, chemical, optical, electrical, environmental, and data-security reasoning for AeroSense. It does not claim institutional approval or proven containment without team-supplied records.

Evidence-ready inventory · many fields awaiting team records
Important: This Wiki summary does not replace institutional standard operating procedures (SOPs), chemical Safety Data Sheets (SDS), or the official iGEM Safety Forms. Operators must follow current lab SOPs and the forms submitted through the iGEM teams portal.

Safety at a glance

Project scope
Living-cell olfactory receptor assays in HEK293T cells; cloning in E. coli DH5α; fly-inspired computational analysis; portable optical readout prototype (research use).
Contained-use status
Institutional contained-use / biosafety authorization status not documented on this wiki

Safety by design

Design choices appear only when they exist in the current proposal/architecture. Untested controls stay Planned.

  • Closed / chambered gas handling (planned): proposal describes multiported gas chambers and tubing rather than open-bench VOC spraying. Status: Planned — not validated on this wiki.
  • Sterile filtration on inlet (planned): 0.22 µm PTFE filter in the gas path. Status: Planned — particulate control intent only; not a VOC hazard control proof.
  • Physical isolation of optics vs heat (planned): proposal discusses keeping waste heat away from the optical/cell compartment. Status: Planned.
  • Leak prevention / seals (planned): PDMS / chamber sealing concepts. Status: Planned — leak-test evidence missing.
  • Shutdown / interlock logic: Not documented as implemented
  • Access control to device or materials: Lab access rules pending institutional confirmation

AeroSense is not described as “biosafe” on the basis of design intent alone. Related engineering iterations belong on Engineering; hardware detail on Hardware.

Biosecurity and dual-use reflection

AeroSense uses common laboratory chassis and insect olfactory receptor genes for sensing research. The dual-use discussion stays proportionate to that scope.

  • Misuse of biological materials: engineered HEK293T lines and plasmid stocks should remain under institutional access control; do not distribute outside approved channels.
  • Unauthorized access to design files: sequences, hardware files, and acquisition firmware should use team-controlled repositories with least-privilege access once hosted.
  • Harmful VOC scenarios: assay VOCs are research reagents, not a public release pathway. Open environmental spraying or weaponization-style deployment is outside project scope and must not be enabled by documentation.
  • Responsible publication: wiki and Registry pages should emphasize contained-use methods, omit actionable misuse detail, and avoid claiming field or clinical readiness without evidence.
