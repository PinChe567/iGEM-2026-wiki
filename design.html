Design

Biological Design

Why biological sensing was designed this way — not how it was tested.

AeroSense combines a Drosophila melanogaster tuning odorant receptor (OR) with the co-receptor Orco and the genetically encoded calcium indicator GCaMP6f in a mammalian host, so VOC recognition can be read as fluorescence. This report explains the Sense-layer plasmid architecture and the proposed path from odorant recognition to a calcium-dependent fluorescent readout. The construct records are linked to Parts; the experimental workflow and observations are documented in Experiments and Results. The design rationale and iteration history are recorded in Engineering · Wet Lab.

Abstract

AeroSense is designed to convert an odorant-receptor response into a calcium-dependent fluorescence signal in HEK293 cells. The selected architecture comprises nine tuning-receptor plasmids and a shared GCaMP6f–(GGGGS)3–DmOrco reporter plasmid on pcDNA3.1(+). The receptor cassette produces a separate IRES-dependent mCherry marker; the reporter cassette encodes one fusion protein. Common expression and assay contexts are intended to make receptor outputs comparable. This report presents the design rationale and sequence-check record, and distinguishes them from cellular expression, localization and ligand-response measurements. The principal unresolved question is whether coupling the reporter to Orco preserves channel function while providing a useful optical response.

Introduction · what a comparable sensing panel needs

The Sense layer has to produce a comparable receptor-response vector, not a one-off fluorescence photograph. Those constraints, not sequence decoration, set the architecture.

  • Tuning receptor Each sensing cell needs one Drosophila OR that supplies ligand specificity. Nine ORs are used so the array can span chemical classes without collapsing every odor onto a single receptor.
  • Orco channel support Insect ORs are not GPCRs. The OR–Orco complex is a heteromeric ligand-gated ion channel [1], so Orco must be present as functional channel support in the same cell as the tuning receptor.
  • Optical conversion Channel opening must become an optical signal. GCaMP6f is the designed genetically encoded reporter of cytosolic Ca2+ [2].
  • Nine-receptor comparability Downstream decoding needs a vector, not nine incompatible assays. Receptor identity may change; chassis, backbone, reporter architecture, and assay logic should not.
  • Minimize unnecessary differences Only the OR open reading frame is intended to change across sensing plasmids. Promoter context, IRES marker, poly(A), and selection markers stay shared.
  • Standardized assay output The designed product of a well is a normalized, comparable receptor-response — not an ad hoc microscope snapshot.

Ten plasmids implement that contract: nine OR sensing modules and one GCaMP6f–Orco reporting module, all on pcDNA3.1(+).

Sensing mechanism

Insect ORs are not GPCRs. The OR–Orco complex is a heteromeric ligand-gated ion channel [1] Sato et al., 2008 — full entry in References. , so no additional signal-transduction cascade is required in a heterologous host. When a VOC binds the tuning OR, the channel opens and extracellular Ca²⁺ enters the cell. GCaMP6f is a published genetically encoded indicator of cytosolic Ca²⁺ [2] Chen et al., 2013 — full entry in References. , so the intended optical output of this architecture is a fluorescence rise. This provides a rationale for using HEK293 as a heterologous host. Successful readout still depends on receptor expression, membrane localization, channel function and reporter performance in our constructs.

  1. VOC
  2. tuning OR + Orco
  3. ligand-gated channel
  4. extracellular Ca2+ influx
  5. GCaMP6f fluorescence
  6. receptor-response vector

  1. 1. VOC recognition

    The tuning OR determines ligand specificity. Orco is the shared co-receptor in every sensing cell.

  2. 2. OR/Orco channel opening

    Ligand binding opens the heteromeric cation channel. No GPCR second-messenger pathway is required.

  3. 3. Ca²⁺ influx

    Extracellular Ca²⁺ enters through the open channel into the cytosol.

  4. 4. GCaMP6f fluorescence

    Cytosolic GCaMP6f is the intended optical reporter of that Ca²⁺ rise. This is the designed Sense-layer output, not a measured AeroSense trace.

Receptor panel and codon optimisation

Nine tuning receptors were selected to span distinct chemical classes of plant and pathogen VOCs. The roster and the Zboray overlap are shown in Receptor panel.

All nine ORFs are codon-optimised for human expression (CTG constitutes ~92% of leucine codons; GC3 ≈ 0.83). Native Drosophila codon usage is a documented cause of poor OR expression in mammalian cells, and codon optimisation has been shown to rescue stable expression of otherwise non-functional insect ORs in HEK293 [4] Roberts et al., 2021 — full entry in References. .

What mCherry does and does not report

Because mCherry is translated from the same transcript but as a separate protein, red fluorescence above matched background supports expression of the bicistronic cassette. It does not report receptor folding or trafficking to the plasma membrane. Receptor function requires a controlled response assay: VUAA1 tests the Orco-dependent pathway, while a reference ligand tests the tuning receptor. Red fluorescence alone does not establish cell viability or receptor function. See Assay design for comparability.

Why comparability is designed in

Exact volumes and timings belong on Experiments. This list is the design contract.

  • Same mammalian chassis All team wet-lab experiments use HEK293. The same cell background is retained across receptor comparisons; passage, density and imaging conditions remain assay-level variables to record.
  • Same backbone All ten plasmids use pcDNA3.1(+).
  • Same promoter context where applicable CMV enhancer/promoter and BGH poly(A) are shared.
  • Same reporter architecture One GCaMP6f–(GGGGS)3–DmOrco ORF pairs with every sensing plasmid.
  • Same assay geometry Designed for a matched multiwell calcium-response format, not receptor-specific microscope recipes.
  • Same stimulus / control framework Vehicle, Orco agonist, and genetic negatives are defined as interpretation rules, not as completed measurements on this page.
  • Same normalization method Responses are designed to be comparable after a shared VUAA1-referenced normalization on Experiments.
  • Same downstream data schema The intended product is a receptor-response vector that can be compared across the nine receptors after the shared normalization.

Reporter topology

The current fusion architecture replaced an earlier bicistronic reporter design; see Engineering · Wet Lab for the redesign evidence.

N-terminal GCaMP6f — why topology matters

N-terminal placement of GCaMP6f is the central orientation decision in Module 2. It follows published receptor topology; it is not a claim that AeroSense has re-determined that topology.

Insect ORs and Orco adopt an inverted membrane topology relative to vertebrate GPCRs: the N-terminus is intracellular and the C-terminus extracellular [6] Benton et al., 2006 — full entry in References. , confirmed structurally by cryo-EM of Orco [7] Butterwick et al., 2018 — full entry in References. .

GCaMP6f must reside in the cytosol to sense the Ca²⁺ entering through the channel. Fusing it to Orco’s C-terminus would place the sensor in the extracellular space, where high extracellular Ca²⁺ would compromise its use as a reporter of the intended cytosolic transient.

Placing GCaMP6f N-terminally follows that topology, and it has a second, expected design consequence:

  • Literature-supported requirement: the sensor faces the cytoplasm.
  • Design hypothesis / expected benefit: tethering may place the sensor near channel-associated Ca²⁺ entry and improve local reporting; the fusion could also impair folding or channel function. That comparison is a rationale, not an AeroSense measurement on this page.

(GGGGS)3 linker

The (GGGGS)3 linker was chosen to give GCaMP6f enough conformational freedom to undergo its Ca²⁺-driven change while keeping it close to the channel. Glycine is used for backbone flexibility and serine to keep the linker hydrophilic. Its conformation, susceptibility to cleavage and effect on channel function have not been measured in the fusion. The three repeats were synthesised with diversified synonymous codons to avoid a tandem DNA repeat, which is a common cause of synthesis failure and deletion.

RSET leader

The construct retains the short RSET leader (His6 + T7 tag + Xpress epitope) carried by the parent GCaMP6f plasmid. It is kept as an N-terminal tagging utility: an intended anti-His handle for confirming full-length fusion protein by western blot at the expected ~105 kDa. This page does not report that the leader has been shown experimentally to be inert in AeroSense cells. That western blot is a planned identity check, not a result published on this page.

Shared expression context

All ten plasmids use pcDNA3.1(+) in HEK293, the cell line used for the team’s wet-lab experiments. CMV drives the expression cassette, while the separate SV40 promoter drives the neomycin-resistance cassette. An SV40 origin requires SV40 large T antigen for episomal replication; no origin-dependent amplification benefit is assumed for this HEK293 workflow [8].

Methods · assay and interpretation framework

Fluorescence is not automatically function. This section states the experimental logic that makes nine receptors comparable. Exact volumes, timings, and instrument models belong on Experiments.

Can tell

  • mCherry can support evidence that the Module 1 cassette is transcribed and that the IRES has fired, because mCherry is a separate polypeptide from the same mRNA.
  • GCaMP6f can report calcium-dependent fluorescence when the indicator is in the cytosol and Ca²⁺ rises.
  • A Ca²⁺ response to VUAA1 is the intended functional readout of the Orco channel; a response to the tuning receptor’s reference ligand is the intended readout of OR specificity. Those assays are defined here as interpretation rules, not as completed measurements.

Cannot tell

  • mCherry cannot by itself prove OR folding, plasma-membrane trafficking, or VOC responsiveness.
  • GCaMP fluorescence cannot be interpreted as OR/Orco function without controls that separate channel activity from reporter artifacts, vehicle effects, and expression failure.
  • Neither reporter substitutes for receptor-level VOC validation. Zboray et al. observed VUAA1-positive lines that still lacked VOC-specific responses [5] Zboray et al., 2023 — full entry in References. .

Experimental design logic

  • Positive controls An Orco agonist (VUAA1) is the designed full-chain positive. A Ca2+ ionophore (ionomycin) is the designed reporter-bypass positive if it is in the SOP — concentration is not frozen here.
  • Negative controls Untransfected / empty-vector cells, vehicle injection, and a Ca2+-free condition are the designed ways to separate channel influx from sensor artefact or solvent effect.
  • Replicates Comparability requires biological and technical replication under the same plate/day reference, not a single well treated as a receptor identity.
  • Concentration range The current protocol specifies a 1 / 10 / 100 µM series. Compound identities are not assigned on this page.
  • Normalization Relative response R and plate/day VUAA1-normalized Rnorm are the designed comparability transform. Formulas live on Experiments.
  • Outlier policy Record technical failures and predefined image-quality exclusions with the raw data. Do not exclude fields because their signal is weak; any statistical outlier procedure requires a stated rationale and assumptions before use.

Discussion · construct risks and future work

  • No epitope tag on the receptors. Final OR constructs carry no FLAG or trafficking tag. That keeps the receptor sequence minimal and avoids perturbing folding of a multipass membrane protein, but it means there is no OR-specific antibody handle: tag-based localization is unavailable. An independent localization measurement would require a validated receptor-specific reagent or a revised tagged construct; function is assessed separately. Should trafficking prove limiting for a given receptor, published strategies — an N-terminal rhodopsin tag and ER-export signal [9] Miazzi et al., 2019 — full entry in References. — are available as a second-generation revision, not as part of the current constructs.
  • The EMCV poly(C) tract. The IRES region contains a 10× cytosine homopolymer. It is a known synthesis-difficulty region, but it is a genuine feature of the EMCV 5′ element and was deliberately retained rather than recoded.
  • Bicistronic expression ratio. IRES-dependent mCherry and cap-dependent receptor expression can differ; their ratio has not been quantified in these cells. For an expression marker this is acceptable, since mCherry is bright and constitutive; it was explicitly not acceptable for a dim calcium sensor, which is why the Orco module uses a fusion instead.
  • Panel risk. Seven of nine receptors lie outside the comparator panel described by Zboray et al.; broader novelty has not been established by that comparison. Receptor-level VOC function is not assumed from expression or from VUAA1.

Discussion · interpreting the first images

Separate expression, selection and function

A fluorescence image answers a narrower question than a calcium-response experiment. GCaMP6f brightness depends on calcium as well as reporter abundance [2]; a dim resting field cannot by itself distinguish low expression from low calcium. Similarly, mCherry supports expression only when separated from background and imaging artifacts. Cell death cannot be diagnosed from the red channel alone. The expression images and their acquisition limitations belong in Results.

G418 selection addresses enrichment of surviving, resistance-bearing cells. Because both plasmids share the same mammalian resistance marker, survival alone cannot prove that each cell retains both the sensing and reporter modules. Selection therefore needs to be interpreted alongside construct identity, expression and functional controls. A better-looking selected field is not, by itself, a quantitative improvement in transfection efficiency.

What would change the design?

Reporter activity with a weak Orco-dependent response would prioritize channel expression and localization; absence of reporter activity would first require an independent check of reporter abundance and imaging sensitivity. If the fusion compromises function, a separately expressed cytosolic reporter is a comparison architecture. Linker and trafficking revisions should be evaluated against the current construct under matched conditions, with changes attributed to independent culture preparations rather than multiple fields from one well.

Conclusion

The design provides a consistent way to exchange the tuning receptor while retaining a common reporter and expression backbone. It also gives the experiments a diagnostic structure: verify the construct, identify expression, test reporter competence, and then test Orco- and ligand-dependent responses. Demonstrating these steps in HEK293 is the route from a plausible cassette architecture to a characterized receptor collection.
