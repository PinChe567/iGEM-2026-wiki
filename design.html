Biological Design

Engineering a living-cell interface for odorant response

This page explains the proposed wet-lab sensing architecture and the reasoning behind it. It is not a Methods protocol and does not claim that planned constructs or assays are already completed. Execution belongs on Experiments; measured evidence belongs on Results.

Design intent · not completed validation

Design question

Can selected Drosophila olfactory receptors (ORs) co-expressed with Orco in HEK293T cells report odorant- or agonist-dependent calcium changes as optical GCaMP6 fluorescence that is usable by AeroSense’s computational and hardware layers?

The wet-lab system must produce, for each stimulus condition, a documented fluorescence time series (or derived response features) with enough metadata that Model can form receptor-response vectors and Hardware can eventually acquire comparable optical signals under stated constraints. Until those data exist, the design below remains a proposed interface, not a measured sensing platform.

System architecture

The proposed living-cell interface co-delivers a selected OR with Orco and a calcium reporter in HEK293T, then reads activation optically. Published heterologous insect-OR panels that used HEK cells, Orco, and GCaMP6 for VOC profiling motivate this layered design [1] Zboray et al., 2023 — full entry in References & Credits. . Orco’s role as a conserved co-receptor in insect OR cation channels is supported by structural work [2] Butterwick et al., 2018 — full entry in References & Credits. .

Construct strategy

Two expression assemblies are proposed so OR identity reporting and Orco/calcium reporting can be co-delivered. Sequence files, Registry IDs, and verification status live on Parts; this section states only the design logic. Diagrams remain concept illustrations until sequence-checked maps exist.

Why HEK293T?

Team rationale (proposal): HEK293T is the planned mammalian host for heterologous OR/Orco expression and optical calcium imaging in AeroSense. The proposal also discusses practical transfection and imaging workflows in adherent culture.

Literature support: Insect ORs have been co-expressed with Orco and GCaMP6 in HEK-line panels for odorant profiling [1] Zboray et al., 2023 — full entry in References & Credits. . That precedent supports HEK cells as a workable chassis class; it does not prove that AeroSense constructs will behave identically.

This page does not claim that HEK293T has “no background current,” zero endogenous olfactory signaling, or universal suitability for every VOC. Those statements would need team-approved measurements or carefully scoped citations. Chassis limitations (growth media, transfection variability, optical autofluorescence, containment) remain open experimental risks.

Functional validation design

Before VOC-response claims, the design requires a control matrix that separates Orco-dependent channel activity, reporter artifacts, and expression failure. VUAA1 is planned as an Orco-family agonist functional control based on published agonism of insect odorant receptor ion channels [5] Jones et al., 2011 — full entry in References & Credits. . Later Orco structural and mutagenesis work discusses VUAA1-related binding and translocation mechanisms [6] Pacalon et al., 2023 — full entry in References & Credits. ; an author correction was published in 2024 [7] Pacalon et al., 2024 author correction — full entry in References & Credits. . Any specific residue or binding-mechanism claim used for AeroSense mutagenesis must be re-checked by the team against the corrected article before it is treated as design truth.

Receptor optimization strategy

Structure-guided mutagenesis remains an active proposal track for improving VOC responses after a baseline OR/Orco/GCaMP system works. It is an investigation strategy, not evidence that optimized mutants already exist.

  1. Structure prediction: predict OR–Orco assemblies with team-chosen structure tools [TEAM CONTENT REQUIRED: tool versions]. Cryo-EM Orco structure provides co-receptor context [2] Butterwick et al., 2018 — full entry in References & Credits. .
  2. Docking: screen candidate VOCs against modeled pockets with team-chosen docking methods [TEAM CONTENT REQUIRED]. Docking scores are hypotheses, not measured affinities.
  3. Candidate-residue selection: prioritize positions for mutagenesis only after team review of models and literature. If Pacalon et al. (2023) residue or binding-mechanism statements are used [6] Pacalon et al., 2023 — full entry in References & Credits. , verify them against the 2024 author correction [7] Pacalon et al., 2024 author correction — full entry in References & Credits. before any residue list is treated as design-approved. Specific AeroSense mutagenesis targets: [TEAM CONTENT REQUIRED].
  4. Site-directed mutagenesis: introduce approved variants into Construct A (or the verified OR plasmid) using a team-approved kit/protocol.
  5. Functional rescreening: re-run the control matrix and VOC assay design; retain only variants with experimental support.

Risk of model-driven false confidence: attractive docking energies or predicted binding poses can prioritize the wrong residues. Computational ranking must not be reported as experimental affinity, potency, or AeroSense sensing performance.
