Wet Lab · Design

Biological Design

Why biological sensing was designed this way — not how it was tested.

AeroSense combines a Drosophila melanogaster tuning odorant receptor (OR) with the co-receptor Orco and the genetically encoded calcium indicator GCaMP6f in a mammalian host, so VOC recognition can be read as fluorescence. This page explains the current Sense-layer plasmid architecture (design choice). It is not a methods protocol and does not claim that the living-cell sensor — including the GCaMP6f–Orco fusion — has been functionally validated here. The current fusion architecture replaced an earlier bicistronic reporter design; see Engineering · Wet Lab for the redesign evidence. Execution belongs on Experiments; wet-lab evidence belongs on Results.

Design requirements

The Sense layer has to produce a comparable receptor-response vector, not a one-off fluorescence photograph. Those constraints, not sequence decoration, set the architecture.

  • Tuning receptor Each sensing cell needs one Drosophila OR that supplies ligand specificity. Nine ORs are used so the array can span chemical classes without collapsing every odor onto a single receptor.
  • Orco channel support Insect ORs are not GPCRs. The OR–Orco complex is a heteromeric ligand-gated ion channel [1], so Orco must be present as functional channel support in the same cell as the tuning receptor.
  • Optical conversion Channel opening must become an optical signal. GCaMP6f is the designed genetically encoded reporter of cytosolic Ca2+ [6].
  • Nine-receptor comparability Downstream decoding needs a vector, not nine incompatible assays. Receptor identity may change; chassis, backbone, reporter architecture, and assay logic should not.
  • Minimize unnecessary differences Only the OR open reading frame is intended to change across sensing plasmids. Promoter context, IRES marker, poly(A), and selection markers stay shared.
  • Standardized assay output The designed product of a well is a normalized, comparable receptor-response — not an ad hoc microscope snapshot.

Ten plasmids implement that contract: nine OR sensing modules and one GCaMP6f–Orco reporting module, all on pcDNA3.1(+).

Sensing mechanism

Insect ORs are not GPCRs. The OR–Orco complex is a heteromeric ligand-gated ion channel [1] Sato et al., 2008 — full entry in References. , so no additional signal-transduction cascade is required in a heterologous host. When a VOC binds the tuning OR, the channel opens and extracellular Ca²⁺ enters the cell. GCaMP6f is a published genetically encoded indicator of cytosolic Ca²⁺ [6] Chen et al., 2013 — full entry in References. , so the intended optical output of this architecture is a fluorescence rise. That is the entire detection mechanism as designed, and it is why a simple mammalian host such as HEK293 is sufficient.

  1. VOC
  2. tuning OR + Orco
  3. ligand-gated channel
  4. extracellular Ca2+ influx
  5. GCaMP6f fluorescence
  6. receptor-response vector

  1. 1. VOC recognition

    The tuning OR determines ligand specificity. Orco is the shared co-receptor in every sensing cell.

  2. 2. OR/Orco channel opening

    Ligand binding opens the heteromeric cation channel. No GPCR second-messenger pathway is required.

  3. 3. Ca²⁺ influx

    Extracellular Ca²⁺ enters through the open channel into the cytosol.

  4. 4. GCaMP6f fluorescence

    Cytosolic GCaMP6f is the intended optical reporter of that Ca²⁺ rise. This is the designed Sense-layer output, not a measured AeroSense trace.

Current architecture

Two genetic modules, one living cell. Ligand identity and optical reporting are split so the same Orco–GCaMP6f reporter can pair with any one of the nine tuning receptors. The figure below is the only complete construct map on this page.

The current fusion architecture replaced an earlier bicistronic reporter design; see Engineering · Wet Lab for the redesign evidence.

In this architecture, GCaMP6f is fused to Orco’s intracellular N-terminus so the indicator and co-receptor are one polypeptide. An IRES-driven sensor is expressed at a reduced level relative to the upstream cistron [7] Ibrahimi et al., 2009 — full entry in References. , which is why this module is a fusion rather than a bicistronic calcium reporter. OR–Orco channel assembly is the published detection mechanism [1]; this page does not report that assembly or trafficking has been measured in AeroSense cells. Receptor panel and codon optimisation

Nine tuning receptors were selected to span distinct chemical classes of plant and pathogen VOCs. The roster and the Zboray overlap are shown in Receptor panel.

All nine ORFs are codon-optimised for human expression (CTG constitutes ~92% of leucine codons; GC3 ≈ 0.83). Native Drosophila codon usage is a documented cause of poor OR expression in mammalian cells, and codon optimisation has been shown to rescue stable expression of otherwise non-functional insect ORs in HEK293 [2] Roberts et al., 2021 — full entry in References. .

What mCherry does and does not report

Because mCherry is translated from the same transcript but as a separate protein, red fluorescence confirms that the cassette is transcribed and that the IRES has fired. It does not report receptor folding or trafficking to the plasma membrane. Functional OR expression is established only by the calcium response itself — VUAA1 for the Orco channel, and the reference ligand for the tuning receptor. See Assay design for comparability.

Why comparability is designed in

Exact volumes and timings belong on Experiments. This list is the design contract.

  • Same mammalian chassis A shared HEK293-family host is specified so the array is not a mixture of cell backgrounds. HEK293 vs HEK293T identity is not frozen site-wide; Experiments currently names HEK293.
  • Same backbone All ten plasmids use pcDNA3.1(+).
  • Same promoter context where applicable CMV enhancer/promoter and BGH poly(A) are shared.
  • Same reporter architecture One GCaMP6f–(GGGGS)3–DmOrco ORF pairs with every sensing plasmid.
  • Same assay geometry Designed for a matched multiwell calcium-response format, not receptor-specific microscope recipes.
  • Same stimulus / control framework Vehicle, Orco agonist, and genetic negatives are defined as interpretation rules, not as completed measurements on this page.
  • Same normalization method Responses are designed to be comparable after a shared VUAA1-referenced normalization on Experiments.
  • Same downstream data schema The intended product is a receptor-response vector that can be compared across the nine receptors after the shared normalization.

Reporter topology

The current fusion architecture replaced an earlier bicistronic reporter design; see Engineering · Wet Lab for the redesign evidence.

N-terminal GCaMP6f — why topology matters

N-terminal placement of GCaMP6f is the central orientation decision in Module 2. It follows published receptor topology; it is not a claim that AeroSense has re-determined that topology.

Insect ORs and Orco adopt an inverted membrane topology relative to vertebrate GPCRs: the N-terminus is intracellular and the C-terminus extracellular [3] Benton et al., 2006 — full entry in References. , confirmed structurally by cryo-EM of Orco [4] Butterwick et al., 2018 — full entry in References. .

GCaMP6f must reside in the cytosol to sense the Ca²⁺ entering through the channel. Fusing it to Orco’s C-terminus would place the sensor in the extracellular space, where millimolar Ca²⁺ would saturate it permanently and render it blind to channel opening.

Placing GCaMP6f N-terminally is therefore required by that topology, and it has a second, expected design consequence:

  • Literature-supported requirement: the sensor faces the cytoplasm.
  • Design hypothesis / expected benefit: the sensor is tethered at the mouth of the channel, where the local Ca²⁺ microdomain is highest — giving a larger fluorescence change than a freely diffusing cytosolic sensor. That comparison is a rationale, not an AeroSense measurement on this page.

(GGGGS)3 linker

The (GGGGS)3 linker was chosen to give GCaMP6f enough conformational freedom to undergo its Ca²⁺-driven change while keeping it close to the channel. Glycine is used for backbone flexibility and serine to keep the linker hydrophilic. The Design source states that the sequence forms no secondary structure and contains no protease-sensitive motifs; this page does not report a structural assay or a named computational check of those properties. The three repeats were synthesised with diversified synonymous codons to avoid a tandem DNA repeat, which is a common cause of synthesis failure and deletion.

RSET leader

The construct retains the short RSET leader (His6 + T7 tag + Xpress epitope) carried by the parent GCaMP6f plasmid. It is kept as an N-terminal tagging utility: an intended anti-His handle for confirming full-length fusion protein by western blot at the expected ~105 kDa. This page does not report that the leader has been shown experimentally to be inert in AeroSense cells. That western blot is a planned identity check, not a result published on this page.

Shared expression context

All ten plasmids use the same vector, which simplifies cloning, sequencing, and selection. The overview and codon-optimisation text treat HEK293 as a sufficient heterologous host because no GPCR machinery is required (matching Engineering wet-lab wording in engineering_content.md). The SV40 origin is specified for episomal amplification in HEK293T (large T antigen positive). Those host statements are not collapsed into a single chassis name on this page. Site-wide HEK293 vs HEK293T identity remains not frozen (see also Safety / Engineering chassis notes) — do not treat the names as interchangeable.

The backbone was chosen without a WPRE element. WPRE modestly increases expression, but it is not explicitly listed on the iGEM White List; omitting it was the team’s design intent to stay within categories understood as pre-approved. Assay design for comparability

Fluorescence is not automatically function. This section states the experimental logic that makes nine receptors comparable. Exact volumes, timings, and instrument models belong on Experiments.

Can tell

  • mCherry can support evidence that the Module 1 cassette is transcribed and that the IRES has fired, because mCherry is a separate polypeptide from the same mRNA.
  • GCaMP6f can report calcium-dependent fluorescence when the indicator is in the cytosol and Ca²⁺ rises.
  • A Ca²⁺ response to VUAA1 is the intended functional readout of the Orco channel; a response to the tuning receptor’s reference ligand is the intended readout of OR specificity. Those assays are defined here as interpretation rules, not as completed measurements.

Cannot tell

  • mCherry cannot by itself prove OR folding, plasma-membrane trafficking, or VOC responsiveness.
  • GCaMP fluorescence cannot be interpreted as OR/Orco function without controls that separate channel activity from reporter artifacts, vehicle effects, and expression failure.
  • Neither reporter substitutes for receptor-level VOC validation. Zboray et al. observed VUAA1-positive lines that still lacked VOC-specific responses [5] Zboray et al., 2023 — full entry in References. .

Experimental design logic

  • Positive controls An Orco agonist (VUAA1) is the designed full-chain positive. A Ca2+ ionophore (ionomycin) is the designed reporter-bypass positive if it is in the SOP — concentration is not frozen here.
  • Negative controls Untransfected / empty-vector cells, vehicle injection, and a Ca2+-free condition are the designed ways to separate channel influx from sensor artefact or solvent effect.
  • Replicates Comparability requires biological and technical replication under the same plate/day reference, not a single well treated as a receptor identity.
  • Concentration range The current protocol specifies a 1 / 10 / 100 µM series. Compound identities are not assigned on this page.
  • Normalization Relative response R and plate/day VUAA1-normalized Rnorm are the designed comparability transform. Formulas live on Experiments.
  • Outlier policy Outlier removal is specified as a Grubbs procedure following the assay source; α is not frozen here.

Design considerations and honest limitations

  • No epitope tag on the receptors. Final OR constructs carry no FLAG or trafficking tag. That keeps the receptor sequence minimal and avoids perturbing folding of a multipass membrane protein, but it means there is no OR-specific antibody handle: surface immunostaining cannot confirm membrane localisation independently of function. Receptor expression must therefore be inferred functionally. Should trafficking prove limiting for a given receptor, published strategies — an N-terminal rhodopsin tag and ER-export signal [8] Miazzi et al., 2019 — full entry in References. — are available as a second-generation revision, not as part of the current constructs.
  • The EMCV poly(C) tract. The IRES region contains a 10× cytosine homopolymer. It is a known synthesis-difficulty region, but it is a genuine feature of the EMCV 5′ element and was deliberately retained rather than recoded.
  • Bicistronic expression ratio. mCherry is expressed below the level of the receptor by design. For an expression marker this is acceptable, since mCherry is bright and constitutive; it was explicitly not acceptable for a dim calcium sensor, which is why the Orco module uses a fusion instead.
  • Panel risk. Seven of nine receptors are, to our knowledge, new to a cell-based heterologous olfactory panel. Receptor-level VOC function is not assumed from expression or from VUAA1.

Cassette feature record

Role, rationale, and caveats for every labelled cassette feature. The inspector copies these notes when JavaScript is available.

CMV enhancer/promoter

Role. Strong constitutive transcription in HEK293.

Design rationale. All ten plasmids use the same vector, which simplifies cloning, sequencing, and selection. This is a bicistronic design: one CMV-driven mRNA encodes two separate proteins.

Kozak (GCCACC)

Role. Kozak sequence immediately 5′ of each ATG.

Design rationale. GCCACC is placed immediately 5′ of each ATG. In silico sequence checks include Kozak context.

OR open reading frame

Role. One Drosophila melanogaster odorant receptor, which confers ligand specificity.

Design rationale. Nine plasmids share one cassette layout. Only the OR open reading frame changes. Length is 1,164–1,242 bp (387–413 aa, receptor-dependent). Codon optimisation: Receptor panel.

Caveat. Final OR constructs carry no FLAG or trafficking tag. This keeps the receptor sequence minimal and avoids perturbing the folding of a multipass membrane protein, but it means there is no OR-specific antibody handle: surface immunostaining cannot be used to confirm membrane localisation independently of function. Receptor expression must therefore be inferred functionally. No ligand specificities are assigned to individual receptors on this page.

STOP (after OR ORF)

Role. Terminates the receptor open reading frame so OR and mCherry are separate proteins.

Design rationale. Architecture: CMV enhancer/promoter → Kozak (GCCACC) → OR ORF → STOP → EMCV IRES → mCherry → STOP → BGH poly(A). One CMV-driven mRNA encodes two separate proteins.

EMCV IRES

Role. Internal ribosome entry site that re-initiates translation of mCherry as an independent polypeptide.

Design rationale. Ribosomes translate the OR cap-dependently; the IRES then re-initiates mCherry. Specification: 587 bp.

Caveat. The IRES region contains a 10× cytosine homopolymer. It is a known synthesis-difficulty region, but it is a genuine feature of the EMCV 5′ element and was deliberately retained rather than recoded. mCherry is expressed below the level of the receptor by design. For an expression marker this is acceptable, since mCherry is bright and constitutive; it was explicitly not acceptable for a dim calcium sensor.

mCherry

Role. Bicistronic transcription / IRES marker (711 bp; 236 aa, ~26 kDa).

Design rationale. Because mCherry is translated from the same transcript but as a separate protein, red fluorescence confirms that the cassette is transcribed and that the IRES has fired.

Caveat. It does not report receptor folding or trafficking to the plasma membrane. Functional OR expression is established only by the calcium response itself — VUAA1 for the Orco channel, and the reference ligand for the tuning receptor.

STOP (after mCherry)

Role. Terminates the mCherry open reading frame so the marker is a separate protein.

Design rationale. Architecture places STOP after mCherry, before BGH poly(A). mCherry is therefore not fused to the receptor.

BGH poly(A)

Role. mRNA termination and stabilisation.

Design rationale. Shared on the pcDNA3.1(+) backbone with T7 and BGH priming sites for sequencing across both insert junctions.

CMV enhancer/promoter

Role. Strong constitutive transcription in HEK293.

Design rationale. All ten plasmids use the same vector. Module 2 is a single open reading frame encoding one fusion protein — not a bicistronic construct.

Kozak (GCCACC)

Role. Kozak sequence immediately 5′ of each ATG.

Design rationale. GCCACC is placed immediately 5′ of the fusion ATG. In silico sequence checks include Kozak context.

GCaMP6f
