Reusable chain

AeroSense reuse map

Our contribution is organized around five reusable packages. This map is the reuse chain another team can enter at any layer.

It is not the project-level story Sense → Read → Decode → Act on Description. That pipeline explains how AeroSense is supposed to work. This map explains what a future team can take apart and reuse.

A measurement leaves Biology as cellular activation, becomes fluorescence in the reader, a sampled digital record in the data contract, a sparse code in the model, and written reuse knowledge at the end. Hover, tap, or focus a stage to see what another team can take from that layer. The moving stroke is only an explanation of that hand-off; the packages below do not depend on it.

Focus or hover a stage to light the path up to that hand-off.

01 Biology

Biological Sensing Package

An Adaptation Map for Drosophila OR/Orco Cellular Sensing

A comparison that lets another team see published heterologous OR/Orco work separately from AeroSense implementation choices.

Current status: Documentation only

Why it matters

A future team interested in insect-receptor biosensing should not need to reconstruct the experimental logic from several papers and supplementary files. The goal is a tested starting point showing what to express, what controls to include, what metadata to record, and what failure modes to check first. That starting point is not yet a released protocol bundle.

Evidence

Wiki design intent and this adaptation map. No AeroSense Registry IDs, sequence dumps, or experimental validation of these constructs are published on this page.

Limitation

Pending final verification

Cellular platform is written as HEK293T pending confirmation of the final implementation. The exact GCaMP–Orco linker and sequence are not inserted. Registry Part IDs are not assigned on this page.

For future teams, simply citing that publication is not enough.

02 Measurement / Hardware

Weak-Fluorescence Measurement Hardware

From a Circuit Diagram to a Reproducible Measurement Instrument

System implementation, calibration workflow, and engineering documentation for adapting frequency-tagged weak-optical readout to a biological fluorescence reader — not a claim that FDM or lock-in detection are AeroSense inventions.

Current status: Documentation only

Why it matters

Another hardware team can enter at documentation of why layout, guarding, reference quality and optical leakage belong in the measurement budget, then replace AeroSense-specific parts without inheriting a black-box schematic.

Evidence

Architecture and review notes are documented on Hardware and Engineering · Hardware. Design files that currently resolve from this wiki are listed under Reusable resources and Engineering · Reproduce / reuse. Those pages are not a completed fluorescence-instrument validation claim.

Limitation

Some design files are downloadable now; a complete reproduction package is not. Firmware, unlocked editable V2 project packaging, gerbers-on-wiki, and validation-ladder measurements are still missing or not released here. Naming the ESP32 / TIA / ADC / FDM / DLIA chain documents design intent. It does not mean the reader has been calibrated as a fluorescence instrument on this Wiki.

Related pages

  • Hardware
  • Engineering
  • Description · Read
  • Engineering Lessons Worth Reusing

What we changed / contributed

Weak fluorescence measurement is not simply a matter of choosing a high-resolution ADC. An early design used a photodiode → high-gain TIA → ADC → ESP32 acquisition chain. Schematic and layout review showed that high-impedance performance can be limited by leakage paths, PCB contamination, guard implementation, feedback-component placement, power-supply coupling, SAR ADC kickback, reference stability and optical leakage. Those observations led to a redesign around measurement integrity rather than nominal gain alone.

A second system-level redesign removed a Raspberry Pi from the portable reader. The current intended architecture is ESP32-standalone: the microcontroller handles acquisition, modulation, embedded signal processing and wireless communication, and higher-level inference can run externally. Removing the Raspberry Pi reduces board area, heat, power consumption and mechanical complexity in that architecture. Raspberry Pi material on this page is an earlier engineering iteration, not the current reader.

The current documented reader chain is photodiode weak-fluorescence detection, a high-impedance TIA (LMP7721 on the Hardware page), a 16-bit SAR ADC (ADS8866 on the Hardware page), ESP32 control/acquisition, and FDM plus digital lock-in amplification/detection DLIA. Frequency-division multiplexing and digital lock-in detection have established precedent for multi-channel fluorescence and weak optical signal recovery [3] Tovar et al., 2019 — full entry in References & Credits. [4] Harvie and de Mello, 2023 — full entry in References & Credits. . The reusable contribution we are packaging is the system implementation, calibration workflow and engineering documentation for adapting those principles to this biological fluorescence reader.

What another team can reuse

The measurement-integrity argument, the ESP32-standalone system decision, wiki-hosted V1/V2 design files under Resources, and the failure-knowledge lessons. Failed assumptions and redesign responses are collected in Engineering Lessons Worth Reusing, not duplicated here. Releasing design files does not imply that every pending performance metric has been experimentally validated.

03 Data

Measurement & Data Contract

A Reusable Measurement and Data Contract

A common interface so Wet Lab, Hardware and Model do not silently use different assumptions about sample identity, calibration and signal quality.

Current status: Documentation only

Why it matters

This contract is independent of whether a final classifier reaches a particular accuracy. A standardized interface can remain useful after receptors, electronics or models change. No accuracy figure is reported here.

Evidence

Specified in prose on this page. No example files or schema diagram are linked.

Limitation

Field names such as “SNR / signal-quality metric” describe what a record should retain. They are not measured AeroSense SNR values.

Related pages

  • Experiments
  • Hardware
  • Model

What we changed / contributed

AeroSense defines a shared data interface across the three layers. The lists below are the contract, not a published dataset.

What another team can reuse

Minimum experimental metadata. Each biological measurement should retain:

  • sample ID
  • receptor / construct ID
  • cell batch
  • well / channel
  • biological and technical replicate
  • stimulus identity
  • stimulus concentration
  • acquisition time
  • baseline interval
  • positive/negative-control status

Minimum hardware metadata. Each reader output should retain:

  • device / PCB revision
  • channel
  • modulation frequency
  • raw ADC signal
  • demodulated amplitude
  • gain configuration
  • dark baseline
  • calibration ID
  • temperature where available
  • SNR / signal-quality metric
  • QC flag

Model-ready output. Processed datasets should include:

  • normalized fluorescence or ΔF/F
  • extracted time-series features
  • receptor-response vector
  • sample label
  • data-split group
  • preprocessing version
  • QC status

Reusable engineering knowledge

Engineering Lessons Worth Reusing

Future teams often benefit more from knowing why a design had to change than from seeing only its final schematic. Each card below is a reusable decision record: an observed risk, what it taught us, the design response, and where the supporting Hardware / Engineering write-up lives.

Failure is reusable when the reason is documented. These records are design knowledge. They are not measured instrument performance.

Cycle 1

From more gain / higher ADC resolution to measurement integrity

Schematic and layout review showed that high-impedance performance can be limited by leakage, contamination, guarding, reference quality and optical leakage. Those observations led to a redesign around measurement integrity rather than nominal gain alone.

High-impedance TIA leakage

  1. Observation / risk

    High-value TIA feedback node is vulnerable to leakage.

  2. What we learned

    Nominal resistance alone does not determine usable sensitivity.

  3. Design response

    Shorter high-impedance path, guarding, cleanliness and layout control.

  4. Evidence / where to read more

    Design record on Hardware. Dark-noise and leakage measurements are not reported there.

    • Hardware · Transimpedance amplifier
    • Hardware · High-impedance PCB
    • Engineering · PCB / signal-chain cycle

ADC resolution versus usable sensitivity

  1. Observation / risk

    ADC resolution does not guarantee useful sensitivity.

  2. What we learned

    Analogue noise and reference quality can dominate.

  3. Design response

    Dedicated reference/decoupling and measured noise-floor testing.

  4. Evidence / where to read more

    Hardware states that ADC quantization is not the experimental detection limit. No assembled-reader noise-floor result is published.

    • Hardware · Gain selection
    • Hardware · Digitization
    • Hardware · Measurement challenge

Optical excitation leakage

  1. Observation / risk

    Excitation light can overwhelm fluorescence.

  2. What we learned

    Optical rejection is part of the electrical dynamic-range budget.

  3. Design response

    Optical geometry + filtering + leakage test.

  4. Evidence / where to read more

    Optical-path requirements and a conceptual leakage diagram are on Hardware. The blank-sample leakage test is listed as pending.

    • Hardware · Optical design
    • Hardware · Validation · excitation leakage
    • Engineering · Optical-path cycle

Channel identity / frequency tagging

  1. Observation / risk

    Multiple excitation channels can overlap.

  2. What we learned

    Channel identity must survive at the detector.

  3. Design response

    Frequency tagging + synchronous demodulation.

  4. Evidence / where to read more

    FDM and DLIA are documented as the intended multiplexing method. No measured isolation matrix is reported.

    • Hardware · Multiplexing
    • Hardware · Channel tagging
    • Contribution · Weak-fluorescence hardware

A working PCB is not a calibrated instrument

  1. Observation / risk

    A “working PCB” is not yet a calibrated instrument.

  2. What we learned

    Biological measurements require traceable measurement quality.

  3. Design response

    Staged bring-up and calibration protocol.

  4. Evidence / where to read more

    Hardware lists a ten-stage validation ladder. Every stage is pending until the corresponding measurement exists. Planned sequence: dark → leakage → standard fluorescence → FDM.

Cross-track

Failure knowledge that travels with the files

These lessons are reusable engineering knowledge. They are not substitutes for missing firmware, sequences, or dry-lab packages.

Verify critical CAD symbols against manufacturer datasheets

  1. Observation / risk

    An incorrect / non-device-specific ADS8866 schematic symbol survived into the V1 package until review.

  2. What we learned

    CAD library convenience is not datasheet truth.

  3. Design response

    Rebuild critical symbols from the manufacturer datasheet before detailed routing.

  4. Evidence / where to read more

    • Engineering · Hardware Cycle 1
    • Engineering · Hardware change log

High-impedance front ends need layout / guarding discipline

  1. Observation / risk

    At 100 MΩ-class TIA gain, contamination, humidity, flux residue, or incomplete guarding can rival the photodiode current.

  2. What we learned

    High gain is not the same as high sensitivity.

  3. Design response

    Shorten the PD–TIA path, implement guarding, and treat the front end as a measurement instrument.

  4. Evidence / where to read more

    • Lesson · High-impedance TIA leakage
    • Engineering · Hardware Cycle 1

Single-seed model results are insufficient

  1. Observation / risk

    A ~0.90 single-seed F1 looked strong until multi-seed means (~0.85) were required.

  2. What we learned

    Single-seed headlines are luck until across-seed statistics exist.

  3. Design response

    Standing rule: later sweeps report across-seed statistics before promotion.

  4. Evidence / where to read more

    • Engineering · Dry Lab Cycle 0
    • Engineering · Reproduce / reuse (dry-lab package still not released)

Check simulation / update clocks when quantizing dynamic states

  1. Observation / risk

    quantize_states was scheduled on self.dt (1 ms) instead of Brian2 defaultclock.dt (0.1 ms).

  2. What we learned

    Bugs invalidate conclusions, not just code — prior bit-sweep claims require gated rerun.

  3. Design response

    Fix clock scheduling; flag precision numbers until re-measurement.

  4. Evidence / where to read more

    • Engineering · Dry Lab Cycle 1
    • Engineering · Figure E-D4

Reporter architecture can create hidden detection bottlenecks

  1. Observation / risk

    Orco–IRES–GCaMP6 gave a real but very weak VUAA1 response; GCaMP sat IRES-downstream.

  2. What we learned

    Amplitude failure can be a reporter-architecture problem, not only receptor expression.

  3. Design response

    Redesign toward stoichiometric, pore-proximal GCaMP fusion; adopt ionomycin × VUAA1 diagnostics.

  4. Evidence / where to read more

    • Engineering · Wet Lab Cycle 1
    • Engineering · diagnostic logic

What We Have Not Yet Contributed

Reproducibility also requires stating what remains incomplete.

At the current drafting stage, several planned resources still depend on final Build/Test work. Hardware architecture and engineering redesign should not be confused with demonstrated biological detection performance. Likewise, a defined Wet Lab or Model pipeline should not be presented as a validated contribution until the corresponding experimental or computational evidence is available.

Pending final verification

Final per-component status after Build/Test is not inserted on this page.

For each unfinished component, we will either:

  1. publish the partial resource with an explicit limitation, or
  2. omit unsupported performance claims entirely.

An honest partial contribution is more useful to future teams than a polished but irreproducible claim.

An earlier Contribution draft listed an education workshop packet. That packet is not one of the five reuse packages on this hub. See Education and the Learning Lab.

No complete contribution package is claimed on this page. Design files that currently resolve are listed under Hardware resources, Biology resources, and Engineering · Reproduce / reuse. When a matrix row says Released, the reuse link must resolve before anyone treats the files as a finished, performance-validated package.

If you remember one line

Contribution in One Sentence

AeroSense contributes not only a sensing concept, but a documented path for connecting engineered olfactory receptors to calibrated weak-signal measurement, structured data and computational odor decoding.

Our goal is that a future team interested in biological olfaction should be able to reuse one layer of AeroSense without needing to rebuild the entire project—and, if they choose to reproduce the full chain, should know both what worked and what to watch out for.
