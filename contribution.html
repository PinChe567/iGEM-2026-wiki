Weak-Fluorescence Measurement Hardware

Building a reproducible reader

System implementation, calibration workflow, and engineering documentation for adapting frequency-tagged weak-optical readout to a biological fluorescence reader — not a claim that FDM or lock-in detection are AeroSense inventions.

Current status: Documentation only

01 What we contributed

Weak fluorescence measurement is not simply a matter of choosing a high-resolution ADC. An early design used a photodiode → high-gain TIA → ADC → ESP32 acquisition chain. Schematic and layout review showed that high-impedance performance can be limited by leakage paths, PCB contamination, guard implementation, feedback-component placement, power-supply coupling, SAR ADC kickback, reference stability and optical leakage. Those observations led to a redesign around measurement integrity rather than nominal gain alone.

A second system-level redesign removed a Raspberry Pi from the portable reader. The current intended architecture is ESP32-standalone: the microcontroller handles acquisition, modulation, embedded signal processing and wireless communication, and higher-level inference can run externally. Removing the Raspberry Pi reduces board area, heat, power consumption and mechanical complexity in that architecture. Raspberry Pi material on this page is an earlier engineering iteration, not the current reader.

The current documented reader chain is photodiode weak-fluorescence detection, a high-impedance TIA (LMP7721 on the Hardware page), a 16-bit SAR ADC (ADS8866 on the Hardware page), ESP32 control/acquisition, and FDM plus digital lock-in amplification/detection DLIA. Frequency-division multiplexing and digital lock-in detection have established precedent for multi-channel fluorescence and weak optical signal recovery [3] Tovar et al., 2019 — full entry in References. [4] Harvie and de Mello, 2023 — full entry in References. . The reusable contribution we are packaging is the system implementation, calibration workflow and engineering documentation for adapting those principles to this biological fluorescence reader.

05 What another team can reuse

Minimum experimental metadata. Each biological measurement should retain:

  • sample ID
  • receptor / construct ID
  • cell batch
  • well / channel
  • biological and technical replicate
  • stimulus identity
  • stimulus concentration
  • acquisition time
  • baseline interval
  • positive/negative-control status

Minimum hardware metadata. Each reader output should retain:

  • device / PCB revision
  • channel
  • modulation frequency
  • raw ADC signal
  • demodulated amplitude
  • gain configuration
  • dark baseline
  • calibration ID
  • temperature where available
  • SNR / signal-quality metric
  • QC flag

Model-ready output. Processed datasets should include:

  • normalized fluorescence or ΔF/F
  • extracted time-series features
  • receptor-response vector
  • sample label
  • data-split group
  • preprocessing version
  • QC status

Neuromorphic Odor Decoding

Odor decoding package

A computational framework that translates receptor-array responses into a high-dimensional representation inspired by Drosophila olfactory processing, packaged with simpler baselines so added complexity can be judged.

Current status: Documentation only

02 Why it matters

A biologically inspired model is only useful if another team can determine whether its added complexity is justified. That is why baselines are part of the contribution, not an optional appendix.

03 Evidence

Model reports decoder figures and comparisons on external metal-oxide sensor data, plus physical simulation methods. The source archives for optics, airflow and transport are downloadable. The matching Brian2 decoder engine, folds, seeds and raw KT dataset are not supplied.

The quantization sweep is withdrawn pending a corrected clock schedule. The seed-100 weight candidate is a validation lead, not a promoted result. See the limitation and rerun gate.

04 Limits

External metal-oxide data do not establish performance on AeroSense cell signals. The four-feature NumPy MLP in the hardware software package is distinct from the PN–KC–MBON decoder. Its presence cannot reproduce the reported Brian2 figures.

02 / Knowledge to carry forward

Design lessons to reuse

Ten practical lessons from designing AeroSense. Explore the reasoning and the next step another team can take, or follow the full engineering record.

Hardware

Measurement

Protect the weak signal.

01 Protect high-impedance measurements +
Principle
Nominal feedback resistance does not determine usable sensitivity. Contamination, humidity, flux residue, and incomplete guarding can rival the photocurrent.
Why it mattered
A 100 MΩ-class front end is limited by leakage path length and cleanliness, not by the resistor value printed on the schematic.
What another team should do
Shorten the photodiode–TIA path, implement guarding, and budget leakage as a first-class noise source before chasing more gain. Read Hardware · TIA and high-impedance PCB.
02 Look beyond ADC resolution +
Principle
Analogue noise and reference quality can dominate long before quantization does.
Why it mattered
A higher-resolution converter does not by itself make a weak fluorescence signal usable.
What another team should do
Specify a dedicated reference and decoupling, then measure a noise floor on hardware — do not declare sensitivity from the ADC datasheet. Read Hardware · Digitization.
03 Budget for optical leakage +
Principle
Excitation light that reaches the detector spends the same dynamic range as fluorescence.
Why it mattered
A photodiode that can see the LED will swamp the biological signal regardless of TIA gain.
What another team should do
Design geometry, filtering, and a blank-sample leakage test as part of the analog chain, not as enclosure cosmetics. Read Hardware · Optical design.
04 Preserve channel identity +
Principle
If several excitation sources share one detector, identity must be recoverable after optical mixing.
Why it mattered
Overlapping LED channels cannot be separated by wiring labels once they hit a single photodiode.
What another team should do
Plan frequency tagging and synchronous demodulation (or an equivalent identity method) before layout freeze. Read Hardware · Multiplexing. An isolation matrix is not published here.
05 Calibrate the complete instrument +
Principle
Bring-up, dark noise, leakage, and a fluorescence standard are measurement quality gates, not optional extras.
Why it mattered
Biological numbers inherited from an uncalibrated reader cannot be interpreted as sensor performance.
What another team should do
Write the validation ladder before first layout so test points exist. Sequence: dark → leakage → standard fluorescence → multiplex recovery. Read Hardware · Validation.
Hardware

Implementation

Make each component count.

06 Check critical CAD against the datasheet +
Principle
Library convenience is not pin-out truth.
Why it mattered
A non-device-specific ADC symbol survived into a complete layout package until review.
What another team should do
Verify every high-risk symbol and footprint against the datasheet before detailed routing. Engineering cycle record: Hardware Cycle 1.
07 Give each processor a distinct purpose +
Principle
Function-map the controller before stacking compute. Overlap is weight, power, and failure surface.
Why it mattered
ESP32 already covered GPIO, SPI, UART, wireless, and onboard processing for acquisition. A companion computer added no unique measurement function.
What another team should do
List required I/O and compute jobs first; keep heavy analysis off-board if the MCU already closes the measurement loop. Engineering cycle record: Hardware Cycle 2.
Model + Wet Lab

Interpretation

Test what the response means.

08 Compare models across multiple seeds +
Principle
A lucky split is not a ranking. Across-seed statistics come before “better.”
Why it mattered
A ~0.90 single-seed F1 looked strong until multi-seed means (~0.85) were required.
What another team should do
Report mean ± spread across seeds (and, where possible, folds) before replacing a baseline. Engineering cycle record: Model Cycle 1.
09 Align quantization and simulation clocks +
Principle
A scheduling bug invalidates the conclusion, not only the line of code.
Why it mattered
State quantization ran on a 1 ms host step while the simulator integrated at 0.1 ms, so most substeps used un-quantized values.
What another team should do
Assert that bit-constraint updates fire on the same clock as the dynamics. Flag prior precision claims until the gated rerun exists. Engineering cycle record: Model Cycle 2.
10 Distinguish receptor and reporter bottlenecks +
Principle
A weak agonist response can be low reporter abundance or distance from the Ca²⁺ source, not only receptor failure.
Why it mattered
An IRES-downstream GCaMP produced a real but unquantifiable VUAA1 change, which is a different diagnosis from “Orco is not expressed.”
What another team should do
Separate receptor vs reporter failure with independent controls (for example ionomycin × agonist) before redesigning the sensing cassette. Engineering cycle record: Wet Lab Cycle 2 · diagnostic logic.
