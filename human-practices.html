Stakeholder input changed who AeroSense is for, what it can claim, and what the system must do.

Before the road

AeroSense before stakeholders

Stakeholders did not merely validate AeroSense. They changed the target, the customer, the claim boundary, the technical constraints, and the deployment model.

The project could be told as a general-purpose electronic nose: a living receptor layer plus lock-in electronics that might serve many facility-agriculture crops, compete as a cheaper laboratory instrument, and describe mold VOC detection as food-safety detection. Showing that AeroSense can detect odor — with strong electronics and algorithms — looked like enough to compete.

Who we spoke with

Compact overview. The road below carries only project-changing checkpoints. Contextual voices sit on side spokes of the map and in the archive. Planned voices stay visible because their absence is a limitation.

Values

  • Early enough to act — detect change before it is only visible after loss.
  • Uncertainty must be visible — risk tier + confidence, not a black-box binary.
  • Claims must follow evidence — screening language until validation exists.
  • Modular technology, target-specific claims — a receptor library does not excuse application-first marketing.

Method & ethics

  • Interviews and mentor reviews are paraphrased team notes unless a verified quote is marked otherwise.
  • Engaged ≠ contracted customer; Planned ≠ completed interview.
  • Identity withheld where confirmation is pending (electronic-nose CSO; some analytical contacts).
  • Public survey is directional (n = 28); instrument and counts live on Education.

  1. Agricultural context

    TY

    Taoyuan District Agricultural Improvement Station — Plant Protection Lab

    Field interview · date not published on this wiki

    Visual inspection is late; VOC early warning is a plausible gap

    Open conversation ↓
    What we thought
    A generic multi-crop electronic-nose pitch could stand without a ground-checked agricultural beachhead. Visual inspection and slow lab assays were the detection options we assumed the field already lived with.
    What we heard
    Asymptomatic spread and post-harvest timing make visual inspection late; VOC early warning is a plausible gap versus slow lab assays.
    What changed
    Supported narrowing toward an agricultural disease-detection beachhead rather than a generic multi-crop e-nose pitch.
    Where the change appears
    Entrepreneurship · beachhead (link back to this checkpoint rather than retelling it).
    Evidence / follow-up
    Plant-protection specialists at Taoyuan District Agricultural Improvement Station who work on crop disease problems in the field. Field interview and site visit. Completed interview; no consented photo in fig/ihp/people/. This checkpoint is the field origin of the beachhead story later tightened by FITI written reviews.

  2. User / buyer tension

    FA

    Local farmers’ associations and facility farms

    Completed interviews · exact dates not published on this wiki

    Institutional channels first — not every grower as buyer

    Open conversation ↓
    What we thought
    The individual agricultural grower is the obvious first customer, and a broad facility-agriculture story can stay multi-crop.
    What we heard
    Institutional agricultural channels are more plausible near-term partners than assuming every individual grower is the buyer. Field handling practices and pain points exist, but a broad multi-crop story still read as “technology looking for a use.”
    What changed
    Shifted outreach language toward associations / stations as validation and possible procurement channels; kept field evidence while the commercial beachhead narrowed to a specific expensive problem (banana export disease detection as candidate). Exporters remain a separate gap.
    Where the change appears
    Entrepreneurship · who needs this.
    Evidence / follow-up
    Local agricultural associations organize producers and channel information, practices, and sometimes procurement. Facility / protected-agriculture operators live with disease and quality risk in production. Completed interviews on real-world practices used in economic and outreach framing. Exporters remain uninterviewed — association interviews cannot substitute for exporter willingness-to-pay. No consented site photographs in fig/ihp/people/.

  3. Sensor-industry feedback

    EN

    Electronic-nose industry expert (CSO)

    Completed interview · date not published · identity withheld pending confirmation / consent

    The sensing element — not electronics alone — is where industry remains stuck

    Open conversation ↓
    What we thought
    Showing that AeroSense can detect odor — with strong electronics and algorithms — would be enough to compete. We needed to know what a VOC sensor must do for people who already ship electronic noses — not what makes a demo look good.
    What we heard
    Priorities: selectivity, field-robust sensitivity, lifetime, and reproducibility. The sensing element — not electronics alone — is where industry remains stuck.
    What changed
    Reframed AeroSense differentiation around the biological sensing layer and the hardware noise-rejection path needed to protect faint signals. Keep the biological receptor layer as the differentiation bet; require hardware noise-rejection (for example lock-in readout) so faint signals survive real environments.
    Where the change appears
    Hardware READ-layer integrity work; Engineering first-board → current-reader redesign drivers; Design / Parts receptor panel.
    Evidence / follow-up
    Technical-loop interview on sensing-element priorities under real deployment conditions. Company naming pending. Revalidation: staged hardware validation and field-like noise tests — not demo-only traces — remains pending. No consented photo in the people folder.

  4. Industry claim boundary

    CB

    Challenge Bioproducts Co., Ltd.

    12 Sep 2026

    VOC signal ≠ food-safety verdict

    Open conversation ↓
    What we thought
    Mold VOC detection can be described directly as food-safety detection. Choose an attractive application first (for example mold detection), then find receptor targets that fit it.
    What we heard
    Start from the technology, not the application — establish what AeroSense can actually distinguish before locking a market claim. Mold VOCs do not necessarily represent food safety — processing can kill viable fungi or change their odor profile while a toxin such as aflatoxin remains, so a mold-VOC signal and an actual safety verdict are not the same claim.
    What changed
    Use “VOC risk screening”; avoid definitive safe/unsafe claims; route high-risk samples to confirmatory laboratory testing; report risk tier with visible confidence. Adopted the target-selection framework: Detectability × Consequence × Unmet Need × Willingness to Pay. Separated the agricultural beachhead from a longer-horizon food-safety / pathogen-VOC thread on the same platform.
    Where the change appears
    Responsible implementation; Education messaging shifted away from absolute “safe” language; receptor choices remain evidence-gated on Design / Parts.
    Evidence / follow-up
    Completed industry interview (team record; paraphrases on IHP). Individual speaker names are not published here. Formal regulatory / biotech-law advice remains planned. Filling framework cells with measured detectability and buyer evidence remains pending — not literature hypotheses alone.

  5. Analytical-instrumentation perspective

    MS

    Analytical-instrumentation / GC–MS–LC–MS professional

    13 Sep 2026 · name withheld pending confirmation

    On-site screening before confirmation — not a cheaper GC-MS

    Open conversation ↓
    What we thought
    AeroSense could be positioned as a cheaper, faster alternative that stands in for conventional laboratory analytical instruments. The individual agricultural grower is the obvious first customer for a field VOC tool.
    What we heard
    Amortized instrument cost is not the prohibitive barrier assumed; trained personnel and infrastructure are. Individual growers often resist tools that flag their own product. Industry is already moving toward smaller mobile labs. Laboratory assays are already strong at identification; in many workflows their limits are time, destruction, infrastructure, and continuous impracticality — not the absence of analytical capability.
    What changed
    Position AeroSense as rapid, lower-burden, on-site screening before laboratory confirmation — Fast · On-site · Low operator dependence — an early decision-support layer, not a drop-in GC-MS substitute. Buyer segment corrected toward associations, stations, cooperatives, and certification bodies — without claiming any of them as a contracted customer.
    Where the change appears
    Description screening language; SCREEN → TRIAGE → CONFIRM.
    Evidence / follow-up
    Completed industry interview; documented in Engineering Cycle 3 and Entrepreneurship learning cards. Cost figures are interviewee-reported estimates. Operator workflow tests and confirmatory-lab handoff design with real QC actors remain pending. Measured screening performance still pending. Exporter and packing-house interviews on workflow, loss ownership, and willingness to pay remain pending.

  6. FITI / Garage+

    FT
    G+

    FITI mentor panels · NTHU Garage+ 18th cohort

    BP review 27 Aug 2026 · pitch review 7 Sep 2026 · Garage+ ongoing

    A specific expensive problem — not a general-purpose e-nose

    Open conversation ↓
    What we thought
    AeroSense could be pitched as a general-purpose electronic nose across many facility-agriculture crops at once — a “technology that can do a lot of things” story. Incubation would mainly support fundraising; a separate Garage+ pivot was not required if FITI had already narrowed the beachhead.
    What we heard
    A general-purpose electronic-nose story is not enough; FITI needs a specific customer with a clear, expensive problem. Garage+ reinforced the same discipline: lead with customer loss, not platform generality.
    What changed
    Narrowed to a candidate banana-disease beachhead (mango/tea as follow-ons) and reframed as a B2B agricultural VOC intelligence platform. Competitive axes redesigned around detectable-VOC range, detection limit, and fingerprinting — not cycle time alone. Garage+ continues to pressure financial and narrative materials; it does not create a paying customer by itself. No separate one-off pivot is attributed only to Garage+ beyond reinforcing the FITI beachhead direction.
    Where the change appears
    Entrepreneurship · beachhead; commercial narrative discipline on Entrepreneurship.
    Evidence / follow-up
    Written mentor reviewers through FITI (National Development Fund Innovation & Entrepreneurship Initiative): BP 11-point review and pitch-deck slide-level review. Direct exporter / packing-house interviews to test whether the paying customer matches the beachhead story remain pending. The banana-export beachhead is still a candidate narrative. Garage+ is an active incubation relationship (18th cohort), not a completed one-off Integrated HP interview. Neither is a paying customer.

Use with care

Responsible implementation

VOC screening can enable earlier triage — and can harm if outputs are over-trusted. Containment detail: Safety & Security. Footprint trade-offs: Sustainability.

Operating principle

Do SCREEN → TRIAGE → CONFIRM

Not SENSE → CERTIFY

AeroSense is an early signal that can prioritize attention. It does not finish a food-safety, toxin, or clinical question.

Constraint Stakeholder-driven rule
False negative Directional survey respondents punish missed real risk hardest (11 / 28 could not accept a missed real risk — not a population estimate). Do not ship a silent “safe” label.
False positive Over-caution can discard acceptable lots and harm smallholders. Triage must escalate, not auto-destroy.
Claim boundary Mold- or disease-associated VOC patterns are not a toxin assay or legal verdict (Challenge Bioproducts Co., Ltd.). Say “VOC risk screening.”
Screening vs confirmatory On-site screening before laboratory confirmation — not a drop-in GC-MS substitute (analytical instrumentation interview).
Decision workflow Report risk tier + visible confidence → recommended next action (isolate / confirm) → confirmatory lab. No SAFE output in the conceptual triage UI.

2026 live-cell scope remains laboratory contained-use only. Regulatory class is a working hypothesis pending qualified advice (planned stakeholder).

Public survey respondents

2026-08-23 · directional sample, n = 28 · contextual, not a main-road checkpoint

Open evidence
What we thought
Public-facing outputs could treat mold risk as a simple binary, and a consumer sample might be stretched into B2B demand or a home-gadget beachhead.
What we heard
In this sample, false-negative intolerance appears more often than false-alarm worry in published counts (11 / 28 could not accept a missed real risk — not a population estimate); residual misconception gaps sat on heat and look/smell checks. Whole-package discard rates are not verified as x/n in Education tables.
What changed
Reinforced risk-tier + confidence + confirmatory-pathway output design; reject silent “safe” outputs. Not treated as proof of exporter demand, B2B willingness-to-pay, home-gadget beachhead, or technical effectiveness.
Where the change appears
Education · survey · Responsible implementation.
Evidence / follow-up
General-consumer-skewed convenience sample on mold risk beliefs, storage habits, and error tolerance. Dated on the Entrepreneurship learning card; raw field log not in repo. What remains unresolved

01 Open technical questions

Questions the sensing stack and claim language still cannot answer with published AeroSense measurements.

Next

What target concentrations are realistically detectable?

Why it matters
Without calibrated detection limits for specific VOC markers, “early enough to act” remains a design intent, not a measured capability.
Who should answer it
Wet-lab assay owners + Hardware / Model teams using defined standards and blank controls.
What evidence would close the question
Concentration–response curves (or limit-of-detection estimates) for named targets under stated assay conditions — published on Results / Experiments, not inferred from literature alone.

See Target selection · Results · Experiments

Next

How stable are cell-based cartridges across batches and time?

Why it matters
Industry CSO input ranked reproducibility and lifetime as hard requirements. Batch drift can silently change risk tiers.
Who should answer it
Wet-lab cell-ops + Safety / QC protocol owners.
What evidence would close the question
A replicate protocol that reports cell-batch and time contribution to score drift, plus usable cartridge lifetime under stated storage and read conditions.

See Sensing bar · Responsibility · validation · Safety

Next

How well can related VOC / microbial signatures be distinguished?

Why it matters
The analytical-instrumentation interview framed resolution — not only anomaly detection — as the harder and more valuable problem. Over-claiming “species ID” from an odor pattern would be unsafe.
Who should answer it
Wet lab + Model, with panels of closely related odorants / matrices defined in advance.
What evidence would close the question
Confusion matrices or equivalent discrimination metrics on a locked panel; explicit statement of what cannot be resolved.

See Engagement · analytical · Model · Engineering

In progress

How robust is the reader outside controlled laboratory conditions?

Why it matters
Screening value depends on usable signal in noise, vibration, temperature, and ambient odor — not only on a bench demo.
Who should answer it
Hardware team executing the published validation ladder (dark noise → calibration → FDM/DLIA → biological fluorescence).
What evidence would close the question
Dated hardware validation records under stated environmental conditions before any packing-house or field performance claim.

See Sensing bar · Hardware

Blocked / needs partner

What false-negative rate is acceptable for each use case?

Why it matters
Directional survey respondents punish missed real risk hardest, but an acceptable FN rate is a use-case and liability decision — not a lab metric alone.
Who should answer it
Intended institutional operators, confirmatory-lab partners, and (for the beachhead) exporters / QC decision-makers — with Safety / HP facilitating.
What evidence would close the question
Documented acceptance thresholds per workflow (screen → triage → confirm), plus an operator confusion-cost study of risk-tier UI responses.

See Survey changes · Responsibility

Willingness-to-pay validation

Why it matters
Consumer survey mention counts and an unpublished NT$150 percentage are not B2B willingness-to-pay evidence for agricultural buyers.
Who should answer it
Plausible payers (associations, stations, exporters, certifiers) — not general-consumer respondents.
What evidence would close the question

Next

Confirmatory-lab workflow

Why it matters
SCREEN → TRIAGE → CONFIRM fails if the confirmatory handoff is too slow, costly, or socially rejected by operators.
Who should answer it
Testing-service / confirmatory-lab partners and the institutional operator who would escalate High / invalid reads.
What evidence would close the question
A timed handoff trial: sample type, assay chosen, turnaround, cost, and acceptance of “isolate → lab assay” as SOP.

See Responsibility · Analytical interview

Blocked / needs partner

Field pilot

Why it matters
No measured food-loss reduction or field detection performance is claimed from Human Practices alone — and none should be until containment, hardware, and partner access allow a real pilot.
Who should answer it
A willing institutional host (station, packing house, or association) plus Hardware / Wet lab under Safety constraints.
What evidence would close the question
A scoped pilot protocol with n, success criteria, confirmatory comparison, and explicit non-claims — only after laboratory validation and containment package.

See Safety · containment boundary · Beachhead

Next

IP / technology-transfer ownership clarity

Why it matters
Receptor and neuromorphic-layer IP originates in institute research; partnering and naming claims need ownership clarity.
Who should answer it
NTHU Technology Transfer Office (planned — not yet formally engaged on this wiki).
What evidence would close the question
Documented TTO engagement outcome on ownership, licensing path, and what the team may publicly claim.
