Operation

Operate and export

The implemented local interface supports setup, signal inspection and export in device-direct, private-LAN and demonstration modes. The English screenshots below were captured from the local DEMO server on 11 October 2026.

  1. Connect and identify the data source

    Choose the appropriate connection and confirm whether the session is a demonstration or device acquisition. Local operation does not require a public cloud service.

  2. Set a repeatable schedule

    Choose the number of blocks, interval, LED duty and active LED condition. Each acquisition block spans two seconds; the stop control turns the LEDs off.

  3. Inspect before correcting

    Check raw data and validity flags before applying an independently measured illuminated blank and fluorescence phase reference. Preserve failed records for troubleshooting.

  4. Export the measurement context

    Save raw I/Q, corrected values, calibration identifiers and timestamps so that the same decisions can be checked outside the interface.

Keep the raw signal, then correct it

The implemented correction routine is designed to subtract an independently acquired cell-free illuminated blank in complex I/Q space and use a separate fluorescence phase reference. Its export schema retains raw I/Q, unsmoothed corrected values, signed projection, calibration identifiers and timestamps. Host and synthetic tests exercise this path; actual blank and reference measurements have not been acquired. Living-cell F0 is a biological baseline, not a blank. Display averaging and blank subtraction cannot restore clipped samples or remove changing interference.

The implementation preserves raw records and QC flags without automatically applying the simulated mixing matrix. Battery state, cell temperature, gas flow and fan speed are not sensed by this design.

The interface is a delivered software component of the reader design.

Load firmware and the interface

J2 supplies USB-C power. Connect a 3.3 V USB–UART adapter to J3; hold SW3 while resetting with SW2 to select the upload mode. Flash the ESP32 firmware and the LittleFS interface, then connect through the documented local workflow.

Validation protocol

Standard-sequence specification

Protocol specification. The table defines the standard sequence and recorded quantities; it contains no bench measurements.

After unpowered fit and leak checks, confirm electrical rails, ADC headroom, DARK acquisition, stop control and observed D1–D4 mapping. Then use a traceable sodium-fluorescein standard: illuminated blank B_CAL → independent B_CHECK → four single-well C conditions → all wells at 0.5C → all wells at C → repeat 0.5C. These are nine illuminated analytical conditions, each with at least five distinct valid two-second technical blocks; DARK is saved separately.

The protocol specifies a 50/100 nM pilot and records fill, medium/pH, LED duty and filter. Its approximately five-second record spacing can miss fast GCaMP6f peaks; dye measurements and cell assays address different signals.

Four independently driven LEDs and one shared photodiode reduce the number of detector channels while preserving a route to frequency-resolved readout. This choice places the burden on stable acquisition and measured optical calibration.

05 / Enclosure

Enclose and cool

From flow model to enclosure

The printed gas lid carries an inlet and outlet on opposite sides of one shared chamber. Its integral shelf redirects the incoming stream above the four well openings. A removable compression plate presses the sealing stack around the wells, while flexible couplers connect the gas ports to tubing through the side openings.

Inlet filter→Gas lid and shelf→Shared well headspace→Outlet filter→Controlled suction

CAD dimensions · Assembly and gas connection guide

Working fill and model fill are different. The team reports that approximately 100 µL already fills the physical well. The updated manual therefore uses 80 µL only as a provisional working volume, subject to a leak/handling check. The CAD estimates 140 µL brim capacity; the published simulations used 100 µL and have not been rerun at 80 µL. Physical observations take precedence over the nominal capacity.

The capacity discrepancy also affects the existence and depth of the simulated gas gap. Read the headspace qualification before using the gas-side operating candidate.

Enclosure, cooling and assembly

Eight source parts

The nominal body is 108 × 108 × 66 mm, around an approximately 100 × 90 mm PCB, with a 40 × 40 × 10 mm fan below it. The base, hood, gas lid and compression plate use PC/GF-like Black; four separate replaceable wells use Aqua Clear Plus. Black opacity, clear-resin transmission/autofluorescence and cell compatibility remain unmeasured. No hinge kit, service tray, extra lid or PDMS film belongs to this current eight-part build.

Keep cooling separate from sample gas

The optical filter and well carrier keep the collection geometry repeatable. The gas lid supplies the shared headspace; a separate fan below the PCB cools the electronics. Sample gas and cooling air therefore have different paths and controls.

Assembly checkpoints

The assembly specification protects the electronics and keeps the well vessels replaceable.

  1. With power disconnected, inspect all eight prints and measure PCB, mounting holes, component heights and antenna insertion clearance.
  2. Fit the fan, captive nuts and PCB using the specified M3 screws and washers; never force a tight part into place with screws.
  3. Install the filter and optical seals, locate the four wells by their flanges, then fit the well seals and removable compression plate.
  4. Connect gas tubes to the gas lid and route them through the side openings; close the lid without displacing or pinching seals.
  5. Perform an off-board cell-free liquid leak check, then verify electrical rails, raw ADC headroom, stop control and actual LED orientation before acquisition.

The CAD includes an antenna aperture with about 0.66 mm nominal insertion clearance against the supplied drawing. Dimensional and procurement records: The antenna audit vendor checklist
