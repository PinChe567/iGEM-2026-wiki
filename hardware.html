Hardware

Portable optical readout for a living-cell sensing platform

Design in conflict · PCB drafts exist · no bench-validated instrument published

The need

Status Planned

AeroSense’s wet-lab track expresses insect olfactory receptors (ORs) with Orco and reports activity through GCaMP-class fluorescence [1] Chen et al., 2013 — full entry in References & Credits. . That fluorescence must be converted into documented digital time series that the Model interface can consume without silent rescaling (see Design · Interfaces with Model and Hardware).

Problem the hardware is intended to solve (lab workflow): dependence on shared microscopy or plate-reader setups for every receptor-array readout, with acquisition settings that are hard to version alongside living samples and model feature extraction.

Users / workflows affected (intended): wet-lab operators running agonist / VOC assays; dry-lab members who need provenance-labeled fluorescence features; future builders who would reproduce a portable optical stack. Field or clinical deployment need is not claimed here: the Integrated Human Practices page still lacks published stakeholder evidence for deployment requirements.

This page prioritizes need, conflicts, iteration status, and reproducibility over renders. Attractive CAD or marketing language is not evidence of a working instrument.

Optical design

Status Planned
  • Excitation: blue LED illumination matched to GCaMP-class excitation is intended. Exact peak wavelength is conflicted (legacy 488 nm copy vs design-report 470 nm vs unlocked BOM LED MPNs).
  • Geometry: design-report discussion uses side-view LED concepts with a photodiode collector; no measured ray-trace evidence is published, and simulated paths are not treated as data.
  • Emission filtering: required to reject excitation bleed-through; filter part numbers / OD not published.
  • Photodiode collection: current KiCad footprint library points at Vishay VEMD5060X01-class PD package for D1; optical alignment jig unpublished.
  • Ambient-light control: light-tight enclosure planned; no light-leak test log.
  • Cross-talk risks: four LED channels on the board draft can electrically or optically couple; isolation unmeasured.
  • Calibration: scaffolded in EXP-AS-08 (dark/blank, optional reference emitter).

Performance classes on this page: Target Simulated Bench-tested Integrated — only Target currently applies to optical numbers; no Simulated/Bench-tested/Integrated optical results are published.

Multiplexing and signal processing

Wiki scaffolding uses frequency-division multiplexing (FDM) and digital lock-in amplification (DLIA) as explanatory labels for separating multiple LED-driven optical channels in frequency space. The compact design report discusses LED modulation and digital lock-in as a capability direction.

  • Channel frequencies: Not published for a current firmware revision.
  • Sampling requirements: must satisfy Nyquist relative to chosen carriers once frequencies exist; ADC candidate and clocking not frozen.
  • Filter logic: conceptual digital lock-in / band isolation — no firmware listing on this wiki.
  • Expected vs observed separation: expected only as engineering intent; observed separation not measured on published Results.

Multiplexing language here explains a planned approach. It does not claim superior noise performance, mixture discrimination, or low-power edge inference.

Safety and responsible deployment

Optical, electrical, thermal, fluidic, UV, and enclosure hazards for the proposed device are inventoried on Safety & Security · Hardware and physical hazards. Do not energize uncovered UV modules, treat blue excitation as an eye hazard until enclosure controls are tested, and do not present field deployment as ready without containment and user-testing evidence.
