One reader, four fluorescent samples

We designed a compact reader to compare four cell-sensor samples exposed to a shared gas stream. A shared photodiode reduces detector count, while four frequency-tagged LEDs create separate excitation channels. 01 / PCB design

The measurement chain

Four independently driven LEDs and one shared photodiode reduce the number of detector channels while preserving a route to frequency-resolved readout. This choice places the burden on stable acquisition and measured optical calibration.

The circuit is designed to excite four wells, convert photodiode current to voltage through a TIA, digitize it and retain four demodulated channels on the ESP32. Templates for raw measurements and calibration records are published; no measured records exist yet.

The proposed acquisition uses a 4 kHz timebase and two-second I/Q blocks, with 41/67/89/113 Hz LED channels. Physical board mapping, timing and headroom still require verification; the firmware retains BOARD_VALIDATED=false.

Phase-sensitive detection motivates retaining the in-phase and quadrature components of each excitation frequency. Scofield’s frequency-domain treatment provides the lock-in principle; it does not establish the noise rejection or accuracy of the unbuilt reader. [1]

The first PCB combined the LED drivers, detector front end, ADC, reference, ESP32 and a Raspberry Pi interface. Its archived 2 August design audit recorded 73 DRC violations, four unconnected items, seven ERC errors and eleven ERC warnings. Clearance and solder-mask issues, incomplete connections and library inconsistencies made the board unsuitable for fabrication. We used that audit to define a professional schematic and layout review before proceeding to V2.

The revised layout was completed on 11 September. Its design changes included a shorter guarded high-impedance path, a corrected ADS8866 symbol and interface, separate analog and digital supply requirements, and removal of the Raspberry Pi interface. These are documented design changes; they do not substitute for an as-built electrical test. The detailed iteration history is recorded on Engineering.

Design rationale · gain, layout, digitization and standalone control

Gain must be chosen with headroom and noise

The photodiode current is converted to voltage through the LMP7721 feedback network. The current BOM specifies a 100 MΩ feedback resistor and 2.2 pF compensation capacitor. For a 2.5 V, 16-bit converter, the ideal code interval is 2.5 / 2¹⁶ = 38.15 µV; dividing by 100 MΩ gives 0.381 pA per code. That is a quantization calculation, not an experimental detection limit. Leakage, dark current, drift, noise and optical background must be measured before choosing a useful gain range. [7] [8]

The 100 MΩ / 2.2 pF parallel feedback network has a simple RC pole near 723 Hz. This is an estimate of that network, not a complete amplifier-stability calculation. Photodiode and input capacitance, board parasitics and amplifier response also contribute; settling, noise and frequency response remain bench tests.

Layout is part of the measurement

V2 places the photodiode, amplifier input and feedback network close together, with a short inverting-input path and a guard around the sensitive region. The guard and optical opening must coexist: a low-leakage electrical layout is useful only if components also preserve the side-view excitation and collection geometry. Inspection must include cleanliness, soldering and the assembled optical path.

Check the package and pinout before committing the board

The review found that the original ADS8866 symbol did not faithfully represent the manufacturer’s pinout. V2 rewired the device for its supported three-wire CS mode, with DIN held high, and added 47 Ω series damping on SCLK and CONVST. The TIA-to-ADC 100 Ω / 1 nF network has a first-order pole near 1.59 MHz; it addresses the SAR interface rather than setting the biological response bandwidth. Package pin numbers, land pattern, orientation and mechanical clearance are checked against the component documentation before manufacturing. [8]

Use one embedded controller, preserve external analysis

The ESP32 already supplies the communication and acquisition functions needed inside the reader, while heavier analysis can run externally. Removing the Raspberry Pi interface simplified the intended power and enclosure requirements. The current design combines USB-C power and single-cell charging with a boost stage, a separate digital regulator and the low-noise analog supply; battery operation and power consumption still require measurement. 02A / Optical decision

One LEE 101 layer, ahead of the detector

The selected build uses a single LEE 101 layer across the photodiode collection aperture. Keep the LED-to-sample path open. The aim is to reduce excitation leakage before the current reaches the receiver, while preserving useful green fluorescence.

Assembly checkpoints

The assembly order protects the electronics while making the well vessels replaceable. These checks define how the CAD will be compared with the as-built device.

  1. With power disconnected, inspect all eight prints and measure PCB, mounting holes, component heights and antenna insertion clearance.
  2. Fit the fan, captive nuts and PCB using the specified M3 screws and washers; never force a tight part into place with screws.
  3. Install the filter and optical seals, locate the four wells by their flanges, then fit the well seals and removable compression plate.
  4. Connect gas tubes to the gas lid and route them through the side openings; close the lid without displacing or pinching seals.
  5. Perform an off-board cell-free liquid leak check, then verify electrical rails, raw ADC headroom, stop control and actual LED orientation before acquisition.

The antenna aperture is already present, but the insertion path has only about 0.66 mm nominal clearance against the supplied drawing. This does not certify physical fit or radio performance. The antenna audit and vendor checklist retain the unresolved dimensions.

05 / Interface and operation

Make each measurement traceable from setup to export

The compiled software package implements a bilingual local interface for setup, signal inspection and export, with device-direct, private-LAN and demonstration modes. These are software capabilities awaiting verification on a flashed physical board. The screenshot is from demonstration mode.

  1. Connect and identify the data source

    Choose the appropriate connection and confirm whether the session is a demonstration or device acquisition. Local operation does not require a public cloud service.

  2. Set a repeatable schedule

    Choose the number of blocks, interval, LED duty and active LED condition. Each acquisition block spans two seconds; the stop control turns the LEDs off.

  3. Inspect before correcting

    Check raw data and validity flags before applying an independently measured illuminated blank and fluorescence phase reference. Preserve failed records for troubleshooting.

  4. Export the measurement context

    Save raw I/Q, corrected values, calibration identifiers and timestamps so that the same decisions can be checked outside the interface.

Keep the raw signal, then correct it

The implemented correction routine is designed to subtract an independently acquired cell-free illuminated blank in complex I/Q space and use a separate fluorescence phase reference. Its export schema retains raw I/Q, unsmoothed corrected values, signed projection, calibration identifiers and timestamps. Host and synthetic tests exercise this path; actual blank and reference measurements have not been acquired. Living-cell F0 is a biological baseline, not a blank. Display averaging and blank subtraction cannot restore clipped samples or remove changing interference.

There is no automatic use of the simulated mixing matrix. If the TIA/ADC operating point clips at the lower rail, correct the circuit; do not disable QC or mask the fault with background subtraction. There are no sensors for battery state, cell temperature, gas flow or fan speed, and no validated odor classifier.

Inspect software evidence and the setup guide

The software evidence summary records firmware and LittleFS builds, 23 host tests, 10 calibration checks, 33 measured-correction checks, 12 DOM/API integration checks, 4 synthetic model checks and 48 DLIA scenarios. These categories cannot be summed into an experimental sample size. Physical flashing and a complete visual browser check of the device UI are not established by these records.

Discussion · limitations

What the current design does not establish

The archived result is a circuit redesign, computational studies and implemented software. The PCB has not been fabricated and a complete reader has not been assembled. Accordingly, simulated optical powers, ideal ADC resolution and software checks are not a measured detection limit, calibrated accuracy or functional proof of concept.

  • Geometry and delivery. The physical fill observation may invalidate the nominal gas domain. The 3 mL/min candidate, shelf comparison and gas wash-in times remain conditional until dimensions and repeated gravimetric fills are checked and the verified geometry is rerun.
  • Optical and electrical uncertainty. Monte Carlo precision does not include real resin, filter-angle response, cell distribution, detector placement, TIA/ADC transfer, saturation, jitter or assembly variation. The four wells require measured standards, blanks and a transfer matrix.
  • Biological coupling. No odor-evoked HEK293 response has been acquired on this reader. Gas-interface wash-in does not establish delivery through the liquid or reporter kinetics at the cells; fluorescent standards cannot close that gap.
  • Use and replication. The BOM contains unresolved purchasing specifications, including the custom emission filter. Battery stability, leakage, independent assembly and target-user workflow have not been validated. A rendering or host test does not establish physical fit or usability.

These boundaries determine the prioritized physical validation gates below, rather than being treated as successful tests.

Validation protocol

Test the instrument before claiming a biological readout

Planned · bench performance measurements have not yet been completed. The sequence below defines the evidence to collect; it contains no measured sensitivity, detection-limit or odor-recognition result.

After unpowered fit and leak checks, confirm electrical rails, ADC headroom, DARK acquisition, stop control and observed D1–D4 mapping. Then use a traceable sodium-fluorescein standard: illuminated blank B_CAL → independent B_CHECK → four single-well C conditions → all wells at 0.5C → all wells at C → repeat 0.5C. These are nine illuminated analytical conditions, each with at least five distinct valid two-second technical blocks; DARK is saved separately.

The provisional 50/100 nM pilot and the chosen fixed fill, medium/pH, LED duty and filter must be recorded. Approximately five-second record spacing can miss fast GCaMP6f peaks. A later cell comparison requires established wet-lab controls, the same cell batch and a common time window; it cannot be substituted by dye measurements.

Optical output

Concentration response, a 4 × 4 single-well matrix and held-out blank before/after correction. The analysis rejects DEMO, duplicate or incompatible records.

Independent assembly

Ask 2–3 non-designers to use the same manual. Record completion, time, help, orientation and seal errors; fix the most important issues and retest.

What remains open

Real-board function, gas-to-cell delivery, material compatibility, odor classification and target-user adoption. No participant outcomes are supplied yet.

The familiarized standard sequence is planned for 60–120 minutes; assembly, flashing and troubleshooting are separate. Five blocks are technical repeats, not five biological experiments. Independent assembly feedback belongs here, with revisions and retests linked to Engineering. Its commercial relevance is explained on Entrepreneurship; a usability trial is not automatically customer validation.

The current result is a documented engineering design with computational and software checks. Functional and biological claims advance only when corresponding raw measurements exist. See Safety & Security, the completion plan and the model-to-build decision table.
