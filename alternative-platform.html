Alternative Platform

Engineering insect receptor expression in a mammalian host.

An evidence record for reuse: the design, the measurement and the limits that determine the next experiment.

Abstract

A missing mammalian promoter and a weak IRES-downstream reporter changed how we built AeroSense. The current design uses HEK293 cells to host insect odorant receptors with a shared GCaMP6f–(GGGGS)₃–Orco fusion. Nine tuning-receptor modules vary the proposed recognition layer, while a common mammalian backbone and reporter aim to make measurements comparable.

The present experimental evidence is an expression-screening record: 25 fixed-cell fields, 12 labels including an earlier Or67b construct and two controls, and 2,624 resolvable DAPI objects before G418 selection. It does not establish a functional odor sensor. This page explains why we chose the host, how the output depends on that host, what the engineering record taught us, and what another team should check before reusing the platform.

Introduction · why a mammalian host?

Our target measurement is receptor-coupled calcium-dependent fluorescence. HEK293 provides a mammalian expression context in which insect OR/Orco systems have been studied, and permits the culture, transfection and optical assay workflow available to this project. Published HEK293 receptor assays provide a relevant starting point, not evidence that every AeroSense construct works. [1]

Insect cells and oocytes are alternative expression systems. We selected HEK293 to keep the proposed receptor panel in a shared host and assay context, rather than assuming that a successful construct in one chassis transfers unchanged to another. Mammalian cultivation also introduces substantial constraints: maintenance conditions, cell-state variability, containment and sample-interface compatibility. These costs remain part of the design, particularly for a future field-use device.

The platform cannot be evaluated by reporter brightness alone. Insect receptor folding and membrane trafficking are host-sensitive engineering problems; the HEK293 literature identifies trafficking as a material constraint. [2] Our fixed endpoint images do not resolve that constraint.

Design · couple the output to the host

Sensing module: CMV-driven OR coding sequence, stop codon, EMCV IRES and mCherry. The tuning receptor and red marker are separate proteins. Red fluorescence can support expression-associated screening; it cannot establish receptor abundance at the membrane, orientation or ligand recognition.

Reporting module: RSET leader–GCaMP6f–(GGGGS)₃–DmOrco, a single open reading frame in pcDNA3.1(+). The design places the calcium reporter at the inferred intracellular N terminus, motivated by the atypical topology of insect odorant receptors. [3] Structural precedent is a design rationale, not a topology or function assay of our fusion.

The output depends on several host processes: transcription and translation, membrane delivery of the receptor complex, calcium entry and cellular calcium handling, reporter state, and optical acquisition. A defect in any link can lower fluorescence. Human-oriented codon optimization was selected for a consistent expression design; published improvements in other constructs do not substitute for a native-versus-optimized comparison here. [4]

The interactive cassette diagrams and linked 2D/3D Parts explorer retain the detailed sequence architecture. The part IDs identify reusable modules; the physical plasmid backbone, cassette sequence and microscopy label must remain distinguishable.

Orco-only has 0/342 RED-positive objects; Or22a and Or98a do not meet the nominal RED Holm 0.05 cutoff. These observations guide retesting without ranking whole cultures.

The current nine-receptor collection excludes the first-generation Or67b sensing design, although Or67b remains an acquired image label and a separately listed basic part. Keeping those identities distinct prevents treating 12 image conditions as12 validated final platform variants.

Discussion · what another team should watch for

Check the whole expression context

A correct receptor coding sequence cannot compensate for a missing host-compatible promoter. Confirm backbone, promoter, reading frame, start/stop positions, linker and both insertion junctions together. Colony growth and a construct map do not establish sequence identity; the archived plate photographs are not chromatograms.

A red marker is not a trafficking assay

The IRES architecture separates mCherry from the tuning receptor. Reporter expression and membrane delivery can diverge. Likewise, a fusion links translation but may change channel function. Preserve separate checks for expression, location, calcium reporter activity and ligand response.

Assembly standards and cloning strategy are different

The current sequence records retain internal restriction sites. A seamless pcDNA3.1(+) assembly can be practical while a part remains incompatible with an iGEM assembly standard. Avoid PstI diagnostics where internal sites invalidate the expected pattern; inspect BsaI/SapI and RFC compatibility for the actual sequence before reuse. No silent domestication is implied. See the part-level QC record.

Dense HEK293 cultures can defeat the denominator

Only 7.1–20.9% of segmented DAPI foreground is retained in the receptor-condition object analysis. The excluded area may contain merged nuclei; it is not a known count of failed or untransfected cells. Sparse and dense fields need independent segmentation annotation, and whole-population claims require a validated denominator.

Do not confuse broad background with saturation

Or98a has 99.10% GREEN area above the global blank threshold, yet 0.50% positive objects after local-background subtraction. Its GREEN export clipping is only 0.0002%. This supports an area/local-contrast disagreement, not a demonstrated saturated reporter image. Lower-density, multi-exposure reacquisition can test background and segmentation explanations without changing the original data.

Selection is a new experiment

The G418 batch was reported separately and has no archived survival trajectory here. Shared resistance markers do not prove that each survivor retains both plasmids. Selection needs parental controls, reporter retention and functional follow-up; a visually brighter selected field cannot by itself establish improved transfection efficiency.

Future work · the next evidence that matters

First recover the missing execution metadata and independently repeat a small controlled set: blank, Orco-only and one OR condition. Validate segmentation with manually annotated sparse and dense fields and retain raw acquisition settings. Then test ionomycin, VUAA1 and vehicle responses before extending to receptor-specific VOCs and matched post-G418 samples.

A functional platform claim will require independent culture preparations, receptor-specific responses with appropriate negatives, and a link from those responses to the proposed reader. A deployable food-screening claim additionally requires biological containment, stable operation in the sample interface and reference-labelled samples. Those are distinct endpoints rather than one generic “validation” task.

Conclusion

HEK293 made the mammalian expression workflow and calcium-reporter design accessible, but the platform’s outputs remain tightly coupled to host biology and acquisition. The completed contribution is a traceable construct-design record, a controlled endpoint image screen and a reproducible account of its measurement limits. Functional insect-OR sensing in this engineered system remains the next decisive experiment.
