What do our biological measurements establish?

  1. Home

Abstract

We followed the progression from odor-related fluorescence in the fly brain to construct preparation, reporter expression and stimulus-associated fluorescence in engineered HEK293 cells.

Three light-sheet recordings show regional signals during odor delivery. Colony plates and DNA gels document construct preparation; microscopy fields and two kinetic odor assays show reporter-associated fluorescence alongside their controls.

01 · Biological inspiration

Fly brain imaging

AeroSense approaches odor sensing as a pattern distributed across channels. We examined that principle by following regional fluorescence during odor delivery. TH-GFP reveals labeled structures; GH146-GCaMP6f records regional fluorescence across seven odor presentations. The two GH146 recordings come from the same fly. Figure 1 defines the measured regions; the recordings show their image context, and Figure 2 compares regional time courses with whole-image means.

In the shared three-odor sequence, the middle and upper GH146 regions showed mean local ΔF/F₀ changes of +1.73% to +2.67%, while the right region ranged from −0.61% to +0.48%. Across the four additional odor windows, regional means ranged from −1.00% to +1.56%. Figures 3 and 4 align each odor window to stimulus onset and its own pre-odor baseline, allowing the regional patterns to be compared locally.

Colony plates

The engineered-cell experiments required twelve vector constructs. We selected isolated colonies for plasmid preparation and subsequent DNA checks. Figures 5–7 show colony plates from this twelve-vector set; the markings identify the colonies selected for follow-up.

Discrete colonies were available alongside denser growth regions, allowing individual candidates to be selected for plasmid preparation.

DNA electrophoresis

05 · Odor testing

Odor testing

We followed fluorescence over time in ten OR + ORCO conditions, ORCO-only cells and the control condition specified for each test. Figures 11–14 show Test 1 and Test 2 time courses, condition comparisons, control-referenced maps and the Test 2 VUAA1-referenced map.

Test 2 contains 72 wells and 1,440 readings across ten OR + ORCO conditions, ORCO-only cells and the blank control. Each construct × stimulus has one well.

VUAA1 is an established Orco agonist used in heterologous receptor assays. [2] [3]

In Test 2, ten of twelve VUAA1 time courses end above their first reading, while eleven conditions have a positive corrected change. Or7a + ORCO and the blank control end lower; a positive corrected score does not necessarily mean that the measured trace rises.

VUAA1 signals and control behavior

Figure 12 compares all twelve conditions. In Test 2, corrected changes are +36.67 pp for Or98a + ORCO, +18.69 pp for ORCO only and −0.11 pp for the blank control.

Test 1 includes a large positive buffer-control signal, whereas the twelve Test 2 DMSO-control scores range from −1.79 to −0.31 pp. The corrected values are descriptive results from individual wells, rather than activation thresholds.

Responses relative to assay controls

Figure 13 adds the six-stimulus Test 2 screen to the original response maps. Test 2 corrected VUAA1 values range from −0.11 to +39.23 pp. Valeric acid spans −0.77 to +8.17 pp; the other three odor rows range from −2.06 to +1.36 pp. We show vehicle subtraction and the blank adjustment separately so that control-dependent changes remain visible.

post-addition fluorescence dynamics and the DoOR reference

A separate analysis of the retained post-addition fluorescence readings compares four odorant profiles across ten OR + ORCO conditions with the DoOR receptor database. The two views below place measured, vehicle-referenced fluorescence changes beside the receptor patterns used to inform our decoding work. The analysis uses the experimenter-confirmed channel labels and source-cell mapping in the post-addition fluorescence workbook; it reuses the Test 2 post-addition fluorescence records.

Figure 15 shows distinct profiles across the four odorants. Valeric acid has the largest positive contrast, +6.97 percentage points (pp) for the supplied Or22a channel, followed by +3.02 pp for Or67a and +2.98 pp for Or35a. Isopentyl acetate is highest for Or42a (+1.35 pp), while pentyl acetate is highest for Or67a (+1.74 pp). Values retain their signs and are shown on a common fluorescence scale.

The across-receptor comparisons show weak positive association with DoOR for 2-heptanone (Pearson r = +0.317; Spearman ρ = +0.406), isopentyl acetate (r = +0.325; ρ = +0.442) and valeric acid (r = +0.211; ρ = +0.283). Pentyl acetate is close to zero (r = −0.002; ρ = +0.006). Thus, the experimental profiles share part of the database ordering for three odorants, with agreement depending on the odorant.

Discussion

The Test 2 VUAA1 time courses show within-acquisition rises in most receptor conditions. After subtracting each well’s F0 change, eleven conditions are positive and the blank value is close to zero. Test 1 and Test 2 use different control designs and calculations, so they provide separate observations rather than interchangeable estimates.

In Test 1, the ORCO-only VUAA1 well showed an 11.86% fluorescence excursion, compared with 0.98% in the untransfected well. The 35.25% excursion in a buffer-only well shows why a rise alone cannot establish ligand-specific activation.

Colony growth and undigested-plasmid migration document preparation, but neither establishes insert sequence or construct identity. Separate RED and GREEN images likewise do not establish receptor localization or channel function.

GCaMP6f is a calcium-sensitive indicator. Its resting fluorescence is not a constitutive, calcium-independent measure of transfection. Low green signal may reflect indicator state, expression, acquisition sensitivity or background, and a single endpoint cannot distinguish these explanations. Functional interpretation requires time-resolved, controlled stimulation. [1]

The fixed-cell screen at 44–48 h and Test 1 at approximately 72 h are separate pre-selection records. Their position in the workflow does not establish that they came from the same cell batch.

The microscopy fractions all come from one experiment. Uneven field coverage, within-field clustering and density exclusions prevent ranking receptor conditions as independently replicated estimates of transfection or expression. Blank and Orco-only controls provide within-study context, but do not supply biological replication.

Test 1 used a laboratory plate reader. These results characterize the biological assay; they do not establish the performance of the assembled AeroSense reader.

Control comparisons use separate single wells and describe fluorescence patterns rather than receptor-specific activation. Test 2 includes a DMSO reference; Test 1 lacks a matched vehicle control. Unrecorded gain, cell-state variation and dilution-only concentration records limit interpretation. Vehicle-matched tests at documented doses in independent cultures are needed to separate these effects.

The post-addition fluorescence comparison provides an exploratory connection between the cell measurements and the DoOR reference used in Model. Its metric is C = Dodor − DDMSO, based on early and late post-addition fluorescence means; it is distinct from the F0-subtracted S values and VUAA1-referenced N ratios above. Positive C can represent a smaller decline than DMSO, rather than a rising trace. The experimenter confirmed matched DMSO fraction and addition volume, while gain consistency and addition-to-first-read delay remain unconfirmed. The experimenter-confirmed post-addition fluorescence channel map supersedes the earlier Test 2 receptor labels; the underlying fluorescence readings are unchanged.

The first three odorants show weak positive cross-platform agreement, while pentyl acetate does not. Leaving one receptor out gives Pearson ranges of +0.080 to +0.502, +0.198 to +0.488, +0.169 to +0.550 and −0.284 to +0.182, respectively. These ranges describe sensitivity to receptor selection, not confidence intervals. One well per condition, shared DMSO references and different measurement platforms limit transfer claims; timepoints do not increase biological n. This reanalysis does not provide an independent experimental validation, receptor-specific activation test or demonstrated fluorescence-to-odor classifier.

Conclusion

The expression screen detected reporter-associated fluorescence above the blank-derived threshold in resolvable DAPI objects. RED-positive fractions across the OR + ORCO conditions ranged from 0.7% to 16.5%; dense overlap prevents interpreting these fractions as whole-culture transfection efficiency.

The odor assays provide time-resolved evidence of VUAA1-associated fluorescence. In Test 2, F0-subtracted VUAA1 changes are +36.67 pp for Or98a + ORCO, +17.52 pp for Or85b + ORCO, +29.46 pp for Or35a + ORCO, and +18.69 pp for ORCO only, compared with −0.11 pp in the blank control. The control maps and retained raw records make these observations inspectable.

The post-addition fluorescence reanalysis adds odorant-dependent comparisons with DoOR: weak positive across-receptor associations for three odorants and near-zero association for pentyl acetate. The shared reference makes the measured profiles and their correspondence to the decoding database directly inspectable.

Density and thresholds matter

The images contain broad connected DAPI regions and clipping at the maximum exported value. Even a sharp edge around a fluorescent patch can contain several overlapping nuclei. Retaining a small, isolated subset avoids inventing divisions within those clusters, but introduces a selection bias. The excluded regions remain visible in every overlay.

Threshold choice also matters. For Or98a + Orco, the RED-positive fraction changes from 30.8% at the less stringent blank P95 threshold to 0.7% at P99. We use the conservative P99 rule as the primary analysis and report the full sensitivity table.

Measurement audit

What the controls show

The control comparison asks whether the object-calling procedure separates reporter-associated signal from the blank within this image set. We recomputed the primary counts from the object-level CSV, retaining the positive-object numerator and eligible-object denominator before comparing each nonblank condition with the untransfected control. Eight of ten OR-labelled conditions have nominal RED Holm-adjusted values below 0.05 under an independent-object model. Or22a (0.080) and Or98a (1.000) do not cross that diagnostic cutoff. These values describe the selected image objects under the stated statistical model.

Interpretation boundary. These are post-hoc, exploratory object-level calculations. Objects share fields and a single experiment, so the independence assumption is not satisfied; the blank also fitted the positivity threshold. Neither Fisher testing nor Holm correction repairs pseudoreplication or that calibration reuse. We report the values to make the comparison inspectable, without biological-significance stars or population claims. The full statistical specification is on Measurement.

The nominal 95% Wilson limits in the download quantify binomial counting uncertainty only under independent-object sampling. They exclude field clustering, threshold fitting, staining incompleteness and segmentation bias; they are not confidence intervals for a culture’s transfection efficiency.

Bright area is not localized signal

An image can look broadly fluorescent even when little signal is locally distinguishable around a resolved object. To examine that difference, Figure 17 compares the median field-level fluorescent-area percentage with the pooled positive-object fraction. The area denominator is the analyzed image pixels outside the fixed annotation exclusion; the object denominator is the eligible DAPI objects across the acquired fields. This asks whether broad area and localized object contrast vary together, rather than assuming they measure the same property.

Across 12 conditions, median fluorescent area and pooled object fractions have RED Spearman ρ = 0.23 (descriptive p = 0.47) and GREEN ρ = −0.02 (p = 0.95). Within the ten receptor labels, the corresponding ρ values are −0.32 and 0.006. Small samples, shared acquisition and different denominators prevent a precise agreement claim.

Or98a makes the difference concrete: median GREEN-positive area is 97.55%, but only 2/286 eligible objects (0.70%) exceed the local GREEN threshold; RED area is 23.61% versus 2/286 objects (0.70%). RED object positivity changes from 30.77% at P95 to 0.70% at P99. Broad fluorescence and local object-to-background contrast are measuring different properties.

Area coverage and detector clipping quantify different properties. Only 0.0002% of Or98a GREEN export pixels reach 255 DN in each field, while median GREEN intensity is 39–41 DN. RED clipping is 0.0002–0.0003%. DAPI has substantial clipping and merged regions, but the reporter exports do not support calling Or98a “near-saturated.” Elevated local background is a possible contributor to lower object contrast.

The measurement audit details the difference between export clipping, broad background and segmentation.

VUAA1-reference normalization

For Test 2, S is the F0-subtracted change defined above. For each construct c and stimulus s, N(s,c) = [S(s,c) − S(DMSO,c)] / [S(VUAA1,c) − S(DMSO,c)]. This sets the measured DMSO and VUAA1 reference wells to 0 and 1; it does not impose a biological response bound. Negative ratios and ratios above 1 remain valid numerical outcomes and are not clipped.

All eleven Test 2 receptor-containing denominators are positive (2.19–39.54 pp). Seven of the 44 odor ratios are negative, and none exceeds 1. Or7a’s denominator is only 2.19 pp, compared with at least 9.43 pp for the other constructs; its valeric-acid ratio of 0.972 comes from a 2.13 pp vehicle-subtracted response. The blank control is omitted from the construct-referenced ratio.

Test 1 is not shown in this normalized map: eight of its eleven buffer-referenced VUAA1 denominators are nonpositive, and its buffer row is not a matched DMSO vehicle. Direct and control-referenced ratios for both tests are retained in the research audit. These single-well ratios cannot remove well-to-well transfection differences or supply uncertainty estimates.

Processing definitions

For Test 2, we calculate the endpoint change within each acquisition and subtract the F0 change from the post-addition change. S is expressed in percentage points (pp). For Or98a + ORCO with VUAA1, +34.09% − (−2.57%) gives +36.67 pp using unrounded values. ORCO only gives +18.69 pp, while the blank control gives −0.11 pp.

ΔF_after = 100 × (F_after,last / F_after,first − 1)
ΔF0 = 100 × (F0last / F0first − 1)
S = ΔF_after − ΔF0

The Test 2 subtraction compares a 168 s post-addition window with a 48 s F0 window. It is a descriptive disturbance correction, not a fitted bleaching correction. A matched 0–48 s calculation is included in the numerical audit: the eleven receptor-containing VUAA1 conditions remain positive, while the blank value is −7.83 pp. For VUAA1, the shorter window preserves the direction but changes the magnitude.

Control behavior remains important. In Test 1, the Or35a + ORCO buffer-only well had a 35.25% excursion. In Test 2, all twelve DMSO-control S values lie between −1.79 and −0.31 pp. Or7a + ORCO has a small positive S of +0.40 pp even though its post-addition signal falls by 1.47%, because F0 falls further. These examples show why both subtraction components accompany the corrected values.

Calculation and interpretation

B(s,c) = S(s,c) − S(DMSO,c)
C(s,c) = B(s,c) − B(s,blank)

For Test 1, the same between-well operations use its buffer-corrected excursion and buffer-only row. The four-acquisition Test 1 protocol and the two-acquisition Test 2 protocol have different within-well definitions; their magnitudes are not interchangeable. The original earlier numerical tables are retained in the audit.
