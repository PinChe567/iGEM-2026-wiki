Abstract

The project starts with fly olfaction. Our results first present TH-GFP structures and GH146-GCaMP6f fluorescence during odor delivery, then examine construct preparation, reporter expression and stimulation of the engineered HEK293 system.

The microscopy dataset covers 25 fields from 12 conditions. In the kinetic assay, the ORCO-only VUAA1 well showed an 11.86% fluorescence excursion, compared with 0.98% in the untransfected well. A larger 35.25% buffer-only excursion limits stimulus attribution.

01 · Biological inspiration

Fly brain imaging

We used light-sheet microscopy to connect our olfactory inspiration with an optical measurement. TH-GFP reveals labeled structures; GH146-GCaMP6f records regional fluorescence during seven odor presentations. The two GH146 recordings come from the same fly.

In the shared three-odor sequence, the middle and upper GH146 regions showed mean local ΔF/F₀ changes of +1.73% to +2.67%, while the right region ranged from −0.61% to +0.48%. Across the four additional odor windows, regional means ranged from −1.00% to +1.56%. The odor-aligned curves below show these spatial differences.

Engineered cell assays

AeroSense requires three distinct observations: delivery and expression of its genetic components, functional activity of the engineered receptor system, and reproducible responses to relevant odor stimuli. A fluorescence image addresses the first question only indirectly. The construct design assigns mCherry to the OR cassette and GCaMP6f to the calcium-reporting module. Red and green image channels are analyzed separately here, without assuming that a visible reporter proves correct receptor localization or channel function.

GCaMP6f is a calcium-sensitive indicator. Its resting fluorescence is not a constitutive, calcium-independent measure of transfection. Low green signal may reflect indicator state, expression, acquisition sensitivity or background, and a single endpoint cannot distinguish these explanations. Functional interpretation requires time-resolved, controlled stimulation. [1]

The fixed-cell screen at 44–48 h and P2 at approximately 72 h are separate pre-selection records. Their position in the workflow does not establish that they came from the same cell batch.

For preparation, segmentation, reporter thresholds and display settings, see Endpoint microscopy and image analysis in Experiments.

05 · Odor testing · P2

Odor testing before G418 selection

We measured one plate containing ten OR + ORCO conditions, ORCO alone and untransfected HEK293 cells. Measurements were acquired approximately 72 h after transfection, before G418 selection. The experiment contains one well per condition × stimulus. Its 3,840 fluorescence readings document the time courses; repeated readings do not increase the number of independent samples.

VUAA1 is an established Orco agonist and a useful probe in published heterologous receptor assays. [2] [3] Here it was dissolved in DMSO; the source report lists a nominal final concentration of 50 µM. The final DMSO percentage and a matched DMSO-only condition are unavailable. We therefore describe the observed rise as VUAA1-associated fluorescence, without assigning its mechanism.

What the ORCO-only well contributes

A11 has a paired baseline of 2,210.8 RFU and a maximum three-reading mean of 2,473.0 RFU. Its test excursion is +0.11860. Subtracting the earlier buffer excursion of −0.09624 produces a corrected metric of +0.21484. The latter is a difference between two excursions, not a 21.48% fluorescence increase during the test. The A12 untransfected corrected metric is +0.00407.

Or35a + ORCO well A10 also rises during the VUAA1 run, with a 17.76% test excursion and a +0.13730 corrected metric. A10 is not called a weak positive: there is no validated decision threshold, and its corresponding buffer-only condition is problematic.

Controls limit the conclusion

The ORCO-only buffer well H11 has a corrected metric of −0.01672. It is a separate well from A11. More importantly, buffer-only Or35a + ORCO well H10 shows a 35.25% test excursion and a +0.44865 corrected metric, larger than any retained VOC result. A gradual rise is therefore insufficient to identify receptor activation in this plate.

A different effect occurs in buffer-only Or42a + ORCO well H03: the corrected metric is +0.31504, although the test excursion is only 1.42%. Most of that value comes from subtraction of the large decline in the first buffer round. Reporting the corrected number alone would hide this behavior.

VOC specificity remains unresolved

Four VOC rows can be retained for a conditional, exploratory comparison. The ethyl-propionate row has reported mismatch in both baseline–response pairs; the isopentyl-acetate row has a mismatched first pair and cannot yield an interpretable corrected metric. The remaining values are neither confirmed responses nor demonstrated nonresponses: doses are unknown, vehicle matching is incomplete, and buffer-only excursions overlap or exceed the VOC observations.

The ORCO-only wells also yield positive corrected metrics for 3-octanol (+0.07301), benzaldehyde (+0.13780), 2-heptanone (+0.18545) and valeric acid (+0.15768). Because these conditions lack a tuning OR, this pattern does not support an OR-specific odor code. Optical changes, vehicle effects, cell state and biological responses remain competing explanations; this plate does not distinguish their contributions.

Discussion

Reporter expression and fluorescence kinetics answer different questions. The endpoint analysis detects reporter-associated signal around resolvable DAPI objects; density exclusions prevent a whole-culture transfection estimate. In the kinetic assay, the larger buffer-only rise shows why fluorescence change alone cannot identify ligand-specific activation.

P2 used a laboratory plate reader. These results characterize the biological assay; they do not establish the performance of the assembled AeroSense reader. Full assay scope →

Conclusion

The expression screen detected reporter-associated fluorescence above the blank-derived threshold in resolvable DAPI objects. RED-positive fractions across the OR + ORCO conditions ranged from 0.7% to 16.5%; dense overlap prevents interpreting these fractions as whole-culture transfection efficiency.

The kinetic assay recorded an 11.86% VUAA1-associated excursion in the ORCO-only well. A larger buffer-only excursion and acquisition inconsistencies limit ligand-specific interpretation. Together, the images and time courses provide an inspectable record of expression and fluorescence behavior under the tested conditions.

Density and thresholds matter

The images contain broad connected DAPI regions and clipping at the maximum exported value. Even a sharp edge around a fluorescent patch can contain several overlapping nuclei. Retaining a small, isolated subset avoids inventing divisions within those clusters, but introduces a selection bias. The excluded regions remain visible in every overlay.

Threshold choice also matters. For Or98a + Orco, the RED-positive fraction changes from 30.8% at the less stringent blank P95 threshold to 0.7% at P99. We use the conservative P99 rule as the primary analysis and report the full sensitivity table.

Measurement audit

What the controls show

We recomputed the primary counts from the object-level CSV before comparing each nonblank condition with the untransfected control. Eight of ten OR-labelled conditions have nominal RED Holm-adjusted values below 0.05 under an independent-object model. Or22a (0.080) and Or98a (1.000) do not cross that diagnostic cutoff. This does not prove equivalence to background, and it does not justify declaring eight successful biological transfections.

Interpretation boundary. These are post-hoc, exploratory object-level calculations. Objects share fields and a single experiment, so the independence assumption is not satisfied; the blank also fitted the positivity threshold. Neither Fisher testing nor Holm correction repairs pseudoreplication or that calibration reuse. We report the values to make the comparison inspectable, without biological-significance stars or population claims. The full statistical specification is on Measurement.

The nominal 95% Wilson limits in the download quantify binomial counting uncertainty only under independent-object sampling. They exclude field clustering, threshold fitting, staining incompleteness and segmentation bias; they are not confidence intervals for a culture’s transfection efficiency.

Bright area is not localized signal

Our two image measurements use different denominators and are not interchangeable. Across 12 conditions, median fluorescent area and pooled object fractions have RED Spearman ρ = 0.23 (descriptive p = 0.47) and GREEN ρ = −0.02 (p = 0.95). Within the ten receptor labels, the corresponding ρ values are −0.32 and 0.006. Small samples, shared acquisition and different denominators prevent a precise agreement claim.

Or98a makes the difference concrete: median GREEN-positive area is 97.55%, but only 2/286 eligible objects (0.70%) exceed the local GREEN threshold; RED area is 23.61% versus 2/286 objects (0.70%). RED object positivity changes from 30.77% at P95 to 0.70% at P99. Broad fluorescence and local object-to-background contrast are measuring different properties.

High area is not evidence of detector saturation. Only 0.0002% of Or98a GREEN export pixels reach 255 DN in each field, while median GREEN intensity is 39–41 DN. RED clipping is 0.0002–0.0003%. DAPI has substantial clipping and merged regions, but the reporter exports do not support calling Or98a “near-saturated.” The original camera dynamic range is unavailable. Elevated local background could suppress object contrast; this remains a testable explanation, not a proven failure mechanism.

The measurement audit details the difference between export clipping, broad background and segmentation.

The audit also identifies two interpretation hazards: the supplied QC column labeled as a median contains first readings, and the supplied VUAA1 normalization uses a negative Or7a denominator, reversing signs. The source’s six retained untransfected corrected values reproduce SD = 0.04483 and 3 SD = 0.13448. Assay scope

Kinetic assay

P2 contains one well per condition, measured with a laboratory plate reader. Unknown VOC doses, unmatched vehicle conditions, missing numeric gain and buffer-only rises prevent ligand-specific or reproducibility claims. Membrane localization, ionomycin controls and channel currents were not measured. Cell detachment was observed but not quantified; its contribution to the traces is unresolved.

Endpoint microscopy

Positive fractions describe isolated DAPI objects from one batch; dense aggregates were excluded. They do not estimate whole-culture transfection efficiency. [5] GCaMP6f brightness depends on calcium, expression and optical sensitivity, so a green-negative fixed-cell endpoint does not identify an untransfected cell. [1] No live/dead stain was acquired; mCherry alone does not establish viability. [6]

Scope and provenance

No paired measurements connect P2 to the assembled AeroSense reader. The CMV constructs have no documented doxycycline induction. All fields, exclusions, thresholds and source hashes accompany the analysis downloads; G418 and post-selection records are in the Notebook. [4] [7]
