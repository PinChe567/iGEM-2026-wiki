Results

What do our biological measurements establish?

  1. Home
  2. Wet Lab
  3. Results

Abstract

The results combine reporter-associated fluorescence, a pre-selection kinetic pilot and three colony-plate photographs. G418 activities and the post-selection odor test are recorded as completed activities; their quantitative datasets are absent from this archive.

We imaged HEK293 cultures 44–48 h after transfection, before G418 selection, to examine fluorescence associated with the AeroSense expression system. The dataset contains 25 fields from 12 conditions, including an untransfected control, Orco alone and ten OR + Orco labels. Each field has brightfield, DAPI, green and red images. Using a conservative segmentation rule and a blank-derived fluorescence threshold, we identified 2,624 eligible DAPI objects across the dataset. RED-positive fractions in the OR + Orco conditions ranged from 0.7% to 16.5% within this selected subset. GREEN-positive fractions ranged from 0.4% to 5.5%.

The P2 pilot recorded fluorescence kinetics from one 96-well plate approximately 72 h after HEK293 transfection, before G418 selection. The ORCO-only well exposed to nominal 50 µM VUAA1 showed an 11.86% peak excursion above its paired baseline; the untransfected VUAA1 well showed 0.98%. A buffer-only Or35a + ORCO well showed a larger 35.25% excursion, and two stimulus rows had reported gain mismatch. These control observations constrain stimulus attribution.

Dense aggregates and overlapping blue signal prevented a reliable whole-population denominator. Eligible objects account for only 9.5–22.8% of segmented DAPI foreground among the OR + Orco conditions. These measurements therefore describe reporter-associated fluorescence around resolvable DAPI objects; they do not establish population transfection efficiency, viability, stable expression or odor sensing. All fields, thresholds, exclusions and source-file checksums are available below.

Introduction

AeroSense requires three distinct observations: delivery and expression of its genetic components, functional activity of the engineered receptor system, and reproducible responses to relevant odor stimuli. A fluorescence image addresses the first question only indirectly. The construct design assigns mCherry to the OR cassette and GCaMP6f to the calcium-reporting module. Red and green image channels are analyzed separately here, without assuming that a visible reporter proves correct receptor localization or channel function.

GCaMP6f is a calcium-sensitive indicator. Its resting fluorescence is not a constitutive, calcium-independent measure of transfection. Low green signal may reflect indicator state, expression, acquisition sensitivity or background, and a single endpoint cannot distinguish these explanations. Functional interpretation requires time-resolved, controlled stimulation. [1]

The fixed-cell screen at 44–48 h and P2 at approximately 72 h are separate pre-selection records. Their position in the workflow does not establish that they came from the same cell batch.

For preparation, segmentation, reporter thresholds and display settings, see Endpoint microscopy and image analysis in Experiments.

Density and thresholds matter

The images contain broad connected DAPI regions and clipping at the maximum exported value. Even a sharp edge around a fluorescent patch can contain several overlapping nuclei. Retaining a small, isolated subset avoids inventing divisions within those clusters, but introduces a selection bias. The excluded regions remain visible in every overlay.

Threshold choice also matters. For Or98a + Orco, the RED-positive fraction changes from 30.8% at the less stringent blank P95 threshold to 0.7% at P99. We use the conservative P99 rule as the primary analysis and report the full sensitivity table.

What the controls show

We recomputed the primary counts from the object-level CSV before comparing each nonblank condition with the untransfected control. Eight of ten OR-labelled conditions have nominal RED Holm-adjusted values below 0.05 under an independent-object model. Or22a (0.080) and Or98a (1.000) do not cross that diagnostic cutoff. This does not prove equivalence to background, and it does not justify declaring eight successful biological transfections.

Interpretation boundary. These are post-hoc, exploratory object-level calculations. Objects share fields and a single experiment, so the independence assumption is not satisfied; the blank also fitted the positivity threshold. Neither Fisher testing nor Holm correction repairs pseudoreplication or that calibration reuse. We report the values to make the comparison inspectable, without biological-significance stars or population claims. The full statistical specification is on Measurement.

The nominal 95% Wilson limits in the download quantify binomial counting uncertainty only under independent-object sampling. They exclude field clustering, threshold fitting, staining incompleteness and segmentation bias; they are not confidence intervals for a culture’s transfection efficiency.

Bright area is not localized signal

Our two image measurements use different denominators and are not interchangeable. Across 12 conditions, median fluorescent area and pooled object fractions have RED Spearman ρ = 0.23 (descriptive p = 0.47) and GREEN ρ = −0.02 (p = 0.95). Within the ten receptor labels, the corresponding ρ values are −0.32 and 0.006. Small samples, shared acquisition and different denominators prevent a precise agreement claim.

Or98a makes the difference concrete: median GREEN-positive area is 97.55%, but only 2/286 eligible objects (0.70%) exceed the local GREEN threshold; RED area is 23.61% versus 2/286 objects (0.70%). RED object positivity changes from 30.77% at P95 to 0.70% at P99. Broad fluorescence and local object-to-background contrast are measuring different properties.

High area is not evidence of detector saturation. Only 0.0002% of Or98a GREEN export pixels reach 255 DN in each field, while median GREEN intensity is 39–41 DN. RED clipping is 0.0002–0.0003%. DAPI has substantial clipping and merged regions, but the reporter exports do not support calling Or98a “near-saturated.” The original camera dynamic range is unavailable. Elevated local background could suppress object contrast; this remains a testable explanation, not a proven failure mechanism.

The measurement audit details the difference between export clipping, broad background and segmentation.

04 · Odor testing · P2 · 10–11 October 2026

Odor testing before G418 selection

We measured one plate containing ten OR + ORCO conditions, ORCO alone and untransfected HEK293 cells. The team confirmed that measurement occurred approximately 72 h after transfection, without G418 selection. The experiment contains one well per condition × stimulus. Its 3,840 fluorescence readings document the time courses; repeated readings do not increase the number of independent samples.

VUAA1 is an established Orco agonist and a useful probe in published heterologous receptor assays. [2] [3] Here it was dissolved in DMSO; the source report lists a nominal final concentration of 50 µM. The final DMSO percentage and a matched DMSO-only condition are unavailable. We therefore describe the observed rise as VUAA1-associated fluorescence, without assigning its mechanism.

What the ORCO-only well contributes

A11 has a paired baseline of 2,210.8 RFU and a maximum three-reading mean of 2,473.0 RFU. Its test excursion is +0.11860. Subtracting the earlier buffer excursion of −0.09624 produces a corrected metric of +0.21484. The latter is a difference between two excursions, not a 21.48% fluorescence increase during the test. The A12 untransfected corrected metric is +0.00407. These observations make A11 a priority for a matched-vehicle repeat.

Or35a + ORCO well A10 also rises during the VUAA1 run, with a 17.76% test excursion and a +0.13730 corrected metric. A10 is not called a weak positive: there is no validated decision threshold, and its corresponding buffer-only condition is problematic.

Controls limit the conclusion

The ORCO-only buffer well H11 has a corrected metric of −0.01672. It is a separate well from A11. More importantly, buffer-only Or35a + ORCO well H10 shows a 35.25% test excursion and a +0.44865 corrected metric, larger than any retained VOC result. A gradual rise is therefore insufficient to identify receptor activation in this plate.

A different effect occurs in buffer-only Or42a + ORCO well H03: the corrected metric is +0.31504, although the test excursion is only 1.42%. Most of that value comes from subtraction of the large decline in the first buffer round. Reporting the corrected number alone would hide this behavior.

VOC specificity remains unresolved

Four VOC rows can be retained for a conditional, exploratory comparison. The ethyl-propionate row has reported mismatch in both baseline–response pairs; the isopentyl-acetate row has a mismatched first pair and cannot yield an interpretable corrected metric. The remaining values are neither confirmed responses nor demonstrated nonresponses: doses are unknown, vehicle matching is incomplete, and buffer-only excursions overlap or exceed the VOC observations.

The ORCO-only wells also yield positive corrected metrics for 3-octanol (+0.07301), benzaldehyde (+0.13780), 2-heptanone (+0.18545) and valeric acid (+0.15768). Because these conditions lack a tuning OR, this pattern does not support an OR-specific odor code. Optical changes, vehicle effects, cell state and biological responses remain competing explanations; this plate does not distinguish their contributions.

Open raw control kinetics and the independent numerical audit

All 420 supplied numeric baseline and excursion entries that could be compared match the independent reconstruction to the source’s four-decimal rounding. The audit also identifies two interpretation hazards: the supplied QC column labeled as a median contains first readings, and the supplied VUAA1 normalization uses a negative Or7a denominator, reversing signs. Transparent QC diagnostics replace undocumented ratios; VUAA1 normalization is omitted from the new figures.

The source’s six retained untransfected corrected values reproduce SD = 0.04483 and 3 SD = 0.13448. These are heterogeneous single wells exposed to different additions, not a calibrated blank distribution. We do not use this value as a detection limit, a significance test or a positive/negative cutoff.

Interpretation and limits

What the kinetic pilot changes

The endpoint screen asked whether reporter-associated fluorescence could be found. P2 now provides actual stimulation time courses and a VUAA1-associated rise in the ORCO-only condition. Yet an unmatched DMSO vehicle, unknown VOC doses, missing numeric gain and buffer-only rises prevent a ligand-specific conclusion. One plate with one well per condition cannot estimate reproducibility. No membrane-localization experiment, ionomycin reporter control or direct channel-current measurement accompanies P2.

Cell detachment was reported but not quantified, so its contribution to individual traces is unresolved. The CMV-based constructs have no documented doxycycline-induction step.

Further reading: [5]

What the images can establish

The dataset supplies a traceable pre-selection fluorescence record with negative controls. The object-level analysis detects localized signal above the selected blank threshold and exposes how strongly density and threshold choices constrain the result. It does not support a single reliable transfection-efficiency percentage for each complete culture. Fluorescent pixel area is also not a substitute for the number of fluorescent cells divided by a validated total-cell denominator.

Weak GCaMP6f signal

GCaMP6f fluorescence depends on calcium state, expression and optical sensitivity. A green-negative endpoint therefore does not identify an untransfected cell. The fixed-cell record contains no stimulus-dependent calcium measurement. [1]

mCherry and cell viability

No live/dead stain was acquired, so viability is unavailable. mCherry expression, rounding, detachment and aggregates do not resolve membrane integrity or cellular enzyme activity. [6]

Resolving the dense-center bias

Dense and overlapping nuclei were excluded from the object denominator. Field selection and segmentation were not independently validated against a complete cell count. The 25 fields improve spatial coverage but come from one experimental batch.

Instrument scope

P2 was measured with a laboratory plate reader. The dataset characterizes that assay and contains no paired measurements from the assembled AeroSense reader.

All fields, object exclusions, thresholds and source hashes are preserved in the analysis downloads. G418 activity records are indexed separately from these pre-selection measurements.

Further reading: [7]

Conclusion

The supplied HEK293 images contain reporter-associated fluorescence above the blank-derived threshold in a subset of resolvable DAPI objects. Dense overlap prevents a validated population denominator, and no viability or independent biological-repeat data accompany the images. The analysis therefore establishes a transparent expression-screening record and defines the next experiments needed to evaluate stable expression and receptor function.

The P2 experiment adds a reproducible kinetic record and an ORCO-only VUAA1-associated rise to that expression evidence. Its controls also expose nonspecific changes and sensitivity to baseline correction. The most defensible outcome is a defined next experiment: repeat the candidate response with verified dosing, matched vehicle, documented acquisition settings and independent biological replication before interpreting receptor function or VOC specificity.
