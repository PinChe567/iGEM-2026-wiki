What do our biological observations tell us?

Follow the biological evidence from exploratory fluorescence imaging to the questions that define our living-cell sensing assays. Construct design, experimental methods and the engineering decisions are linked beside the relevant question.

From fluorescence to a sensing question

Our light-sheet recording follows TH-associated fluorescence in a fly preparation during presentations of isopentyl acetate, 3-octanol and 4-methylcyclohexanol. The regional traces show fluorescence changes around these presentations, motivating the question behind AeroSense: how can an odor-dependent biological response become a readable optical signal?

This observation concerns the fly preparation. The engineered HEK293T sensor is a separate experimental system, evaluated through the questions below.

How the evidence guides engineering

The biological design separates receptor identity from a shared optical reporter, so expression and channel function can be assessed before interpreting a target-odor response. The Wet Lab engineering record traces the construct choices and assay decisions; Reporter topology explains the final fusion architecture.

Interpretation and scope

The published light-sheet traces are exploratory observations. They do not establish a replicated dose response, classify food spoilage, or validate the engineered OR/Orco–GCaMP6f sensor. No reviewed quantitative performance package for that cell sensor is included in the supplied record. Biological replicate counts, stimulus timing, controls and uncertainty are needed to draw those further conclusions.
