From nose to biosensor

Explore how biology senses invisible molecules, and how synthetic biology can turn that principle into an early warning food sensing system.

Purpose

Why learn
through interaction?

A smell is invisible. A receptor response is invisible. A neural code is invisible.

Reading about them is not the same as testing what happens when a pattern changes. This lab turns those hidden steps into things you can manipulate, compare, and observe — from molecule to receptor pattern, fluorescence, neural-style representation, and a responsible risk decision.

Path

Your learning journey

Five modules. Open any of them in any order. Nothing is locked.

Swipe sideways to see every module.

  1. 01 Discover

    How do we smell?

    Explain receptor patterns, combinatorial coding, and why sparse codes can help separate similar odors.

    ~8 min Not started

  2. 02 Question

    When does mold become a food risk?

    Distinguish mold growth from mycotoxin contamination, and test claims that look, smell, or heat cannot settle.

    ~8 min Not started

  3. 03 Build

    Can a cell learn to smell?

    Assemble OR, Orco, and GCaMP in a living cell, and keep that culture behind a barrier.

    ~8 min Not started

  4. 04 Decode

    An odor is a pattern

    Read a multi-receptor fingerprint, then ask what contrast, sparse codes, and a classifier can and cannot claim.

    ~8 min Not started

  5. 05 Decide

    A signal is not a verdict

    Sit as a QC manager: screening guides the next action; it does not replace confirmatory testing.

    ~8 min Not started

All five modules are full interactive lessons. Progress stays in this browser and is not a published learning outcome.

System

The three layers

Hover, tap, or focus a card to see what you will learn. Same content on every device.

What you will learn

Why odors are patterns across many receptors; how fly OR proteins with Orco can be expressed in HEK293T cells; and why fluorescence is a reporter, not a finished product.

What you will learn

Why a glow is not yet a measurement. Follow light to photodiode, TIA, ADC, and edge readout — including noise, not only the happy path.

What you will learn

How to talk about screening versus diagnosis, false alarms, missed risk, and why AeroSense results must not be over-claimed in food or clinical language.

Design

What makes this different

Learn by doing

Test your assumptions against a pattern you can change, not only a paragraph you can reread.

See your progress

Pre/post knowledge comparison is designed into each module. Scores will stay on this device until a consented evaluation exists.

Understand uncertainty

Learn what sensing systems can and cannot claim — including AeroSense as a research prototype.

Shape the project

Your feedback can inform how AeroSense communicates risk. Education here is a two-way loop, not a booth lecture.

Educator Resources

Module summaries

  1. Discover — human vs fly olfaction as combinatorial codes.
  2. Question — mold, mycotoxins, and the limits of odor-based screening.
  3. Build — heterologous receptors and fluorescent readout.
  4. Decode — antennal-lobe-inspired contrast and mushroom-body-inspired sparse codes.
  5. Decide — from laboratory signal to responsible food-risk language.

Suggested classroom sequence

01 → 02 → 03 → 04 → 05 matches the AeroSense functional system architecture (Sense → Read → Decode → Act), not the team workstream hierarchy. Learners may still jump to a single module if time is short.

Printable discussion questions

  • Why is one sensor channel a weak substitute for an odor identity?
  • What is the difference between seeing mold and claiming a mycotoxin risk?
  • If fluorescence rises, what else must be true before you trust the reading?
  • What would a false positive or false negative mean for a food handler?
  • Which claim language would you refuse to print on a device screen?
