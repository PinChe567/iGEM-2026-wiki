Transferable lesson

Verify that every host-required expression element exists before ordering / synthesis — not after delivery. Written up for other teams on Contribution.

Design

Our first synthesised constructs were delivered in a high-copy cloning backbone. We intended to express them directly in HEK293.

Build

Constructs were received as plasmid DNA in the supplied backbone.

Test

Before transfection, we annotated the backbone sequence in full. It contained a ColE1 origin, a β-lactamase gene and sequencing adapters — and no mammalian promoter and no polyadenylation signal.

Learn

The construct could be propagated in E. coli but could never be transcribed in a mammalian cell.

We evaluated two rescue routes: overlap-extension PCR to build a linear CMV–insert–polyA cassette, or moving to a proper mammalian expression vector.

Redesign

We chose the vector route and consolidated all ten constructs onto pcDNA3.1(+), which supplies a CMV promoter, a BGH polyadenylation signal, and a NeoR cassette for G418 selection.

Key decision

Stop assuming a synthesis backbone is expression-ready; migrate the panel to pcDNA3.1(+) before mammalian work.

Cycle 2 Orco–IRES–GCaMP6 gave a very weak signal

Evidence status DATA

Evidence → Learning → Change

Evidence
VUAA1 challenge produced a real but very weak green fluorescence increase — visible on careful inspection, too small to record reliably or quantify. Consistent with a functional Orco–Ca²⁺–GCaMP pathway; amplitude was the problem. Does not independently prove trafficking or which component limited signal.
Learning
Initial hypothesis (poor Orco expression) was challenged by construct-map re-examination: GCaMP6 is IRES-downstream, so reduced reporter abundance is a plausible explanation for the weak signal — a mechanistic hypothesis, not a measured diagnosis.
Change
Redesign the reporting module as a translational fusion (Cycle 3) to restore 1:1 stoichiometry and pore-proximal sensing.

Design

Our first reporting module used a bicistronic architecture:

CMV → Orco → STOP → EMCV IRES → GCaMP6 → STOP → polyA

Intent: Orco and GCaMP6 as separate, native proteins — Orco without a fusion partner; free cytosolic GCaMP6 for Ca²⁺ sensing. The same bicistronic pattern was used for OR–IRES–mCherry modules, which appeared to work.

Build

The construct was synthesised and transfected into HEK293 cells alongside the OR modules.

Test

We applied VUAA1, the universal Orco agonist [2]Jones et al., 2011 — full entry in References., which is intended to open the OR–Orco channel and therefore to stress the intended Orco–Ca²⁺–GCaMP reporting pathway in a single measurement.

Result: a real but very weak green fluorescence increase — visible on careful inspection but too small to record reliably or quantify (Figure E-W1 — primary image/trace not archived).

Interpretation care: a weak VUAA1-evoked fluorescence change is evidence consistent with a functional Orco–Ca²⁺–GCaMP response pathway whose limiting problem was amplitude. It does not independently establish expression level, membrane trafficking, localization, or which component limited amplitude. Separating receptor vs reporter failure modes is why we later adopted ionomycin + VUAA1 diagnostics (Diagnostic logic).

Learn

Initial hypothesis (poor Orco expression) was challenged by re-reading the construct map: in Orco–IRES–GCaMP6, GCaMP sits IRES-downstream, where abundance is often lower than the upstream cistron. That made reduced reporter abundance a plausible amplitude limit — a mechanistic hypothesis, not a measured diagnosis.

Architecture rationale for the fusion that followed: Design · Module 2. Diagnostic separation of receptor vs reporter failure modes: Diagnostic logic.

Redesign

Move to a translational fusion that restores 1:1 stoichiometry and places the sensor at the channel mouth — Cycle 3.

Key decision

Treat amplitude failure as a reporter-architecture problem first; redesign for stoichiometric, pore-proximal GCaMP rather than chasing Orco expression alone.

Hardware V1 → V2 (measurement integrity)

Showing Before · V1

Gain-first V1

  • 100 MΩ TIA emphasis for maximum amplification
  • Longer high-impedance PD/TIA routing risk
  • Shared analog/digital supply priorities

Design

Our first architecture was built around a straightforward signal chain:

LED → sample → photodiode → LMP7721 TIA → ADS8866 ADC → ESP32

We initially focused on achieving sufficient analog gain. A 100 MΩ feedback resistor was selected as the primary starting point because it provided high transimpedance gain while remaining more practical than immediately using a 1 GΩ feedback path Texas Instruments · LMP7721 datasheet ↗ (opens in a new tab) Team archive.

V1 also integrated power regulation, four LED switching channels and a Raspberry Pi interface on a four-layer PCB.

Build

By early August, we had completed:

  • the V1 schematic;
  • PCB layout;
  • initial BOM;
  • footprints and 3D models; and
  • ERC/DRC reports.
Test

Our first formal test was not biological—it was the electrical design audit.

V1 electrical design audit — August 2 PCB archive Team archive

These counts are evidence that stopped an unready board from being manufactured — not a post-mortem for its own sake. Primary report files: Figure E-H1B.

  • 73 DRC violations
  • 4 Unconnected items
  • 7 ERC errors
  • 11 ERC warnings

Issues included clearance and solder-mask violations, incomplete connections, dangling guard routing, power-rule errors, and symbol/footprint inconsistencies. We therefore stopped before manufacturing V1.

The design was then submitted to iST Group for professional schematic and layout review Professional review — iST Group attribution (on this page). The review focused on LMP7721 pin mapping and guard implementation, photodiode pad interpretation, TIA placement, ADC/reference decoupling, SAR input behavior, ESP32 EN/BOOT circuitry, and critical capacitor placement.

Learn

The most important lesson was conceptual:

High gain is not the same as high sensitivity.

At 100 MΩ, currents caused by PCB contamination, humidity, flux residue, incorrect guarding or nearby switching activity can become comparable with the desired photodiode current.

A high-resolution ADC cannot recover information that has already been corrupted before digitization.

See the persistent conceptual pivot above for the objective change that followed.

Redesign

These findings were translated into concrete V2 design changes:

  • shortened photodiode–LMP7721 high-impedance routing;
  • a guard structure around the sensitive photodiode/TIA feedback region;
  • relocation of the TIA to remain close to the photodiode while avoiding the primary optical path;
  • correction of component symbols and pin mappings, including rebuilding the ADS8866 symbol from the manufacturer datasheet Texas Instruments · ADS8866 datasheet;
  • implementation of the ADS8866 in 3-wire CS mode with DIN held high and series damping on SCLK/CONVST;
  • separation of low-noise analog and higher-current digital 3.3 V supplies;
  • improved local decoupling and ADC/reference placement;
  • cleaner analog/digital return-current management.

This was the first major engineering cycle of AeroSense.

Key decision

Stop V1 before fab; treat DRC/ERC plus professional review as the cycle’s Test evidence; redesign V2 around measurement integrity rather than maximum gain.

Cycle 2 Removing Hardware Instead of Adding It

▣ Evidence status REVIEW-SUPPORTED

Evidence type: this cycle is a functional design review / architecture review (mapping required functions onto available silicon) — not a physical experimental validation of a fabricated board.

Evidence → Learning → Change

Evidence
Function-by-function mapping showed the ESP32 already provides GPIO, SPI, UART, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and enough embedded computation for acquisition and digital lock-in Espressif · ESP32 datasheet. Heavier NeuroAI analysis can run on an external server.
Learning
The Raspberry Pi did not provide a unique measurement function, while it added board area, energy, heat, cost, mechanical height, and integration complexity.
Change
Remove the Pi from V2 → standalone ESP32 reader with rechargeable single-cell battery, USB-C charging, power-path management, and a 5 V boost stage.

Architecture before / after

Showing Before · V1

ESP32 + Raspberry Pi

  • ESP32: LED timing, ADC acquisition, low-level processing
  • Raspberry Pi: higher-level control, networking, computation
  • Power concept depended on the Raspberry Pi 5 V supply
  • Reader framed as a PCB attached to another computer

ESP32 standalone + external server

  • Raspberry Pi removed — no unique measurement role remained
  • ESP32 retains acquisition, DLIA-class processing, and wireless links
  • NeuroAI / heavier analysis → external server
  • Power: rechargeable single-cell battery · USB-C charging · power-path management · 5 V boost
  • Reader framed as an independent sensing instrument
Design question

The original architecture used both an ESP32 and a Raspberry Pi.

The intended division of labor was:

  • ESP32: LED timing, ADC acquisition and low-level processing;
  • Raspberry Pi: higher-level control, networking and computation.
Build

This cycle did not add a new fabricated assembly. The “build” was the architecture itself: a dual-processor V1 concept that still carried a Raspberry Pi interface on the four-layer board from Cycle 1.

Circuit-level detail stays on the Hardware page; here we track only the compute/power decision.

Test / review

Instead of assuming both processors were necessary, we mapped every required function to the available hardware.

The ESP32 already provides GPIO, SPI, UART, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and sufficient embedded computation for acquisition and digital lock-in processing Espressif · ESP32 datasheet.

More computationally intensive NeuroAI analysis does not need to occur on the reader itself and can be performed on an external server.

Learn

The Raspberry Pi did not provide a unique measurement function, while it added:

  • board area
  • energy consumption
  • heat
  • cost
  • mechanical height
  • integration complexity
Redesign

We therefore removed the Raspberry Pi from V2 and redesigned the device as a standalone ESP32-based reader.

This decision also changed the power architecture. Instead of depending on the Raspberry Pi 5 V supply, the V2 concept uses a rechargeable single-cell battery, USB-C charging, power-path management and a 5 V boost stage.

This redesign moved AeroSense from a PCB attached to another computer toward an independent sensing instrument.

Key decision

Drop the Raspberry Pi; keep measurement and lock-in on the ESP32; move heavy NeuroAI off-board; redesign power for portable standalone operation.

  9. 5 Fix + rerun required Quantize on every real integration step, then re-measure

OFAT / refinement sweep — not yet the new best model

Baseline macro F1 = 0.8676. OFAT + refinement on w_kc_mbon ∈ [11, 20] found w_kc_mbon=13 → macro F1 = 0.8870 at the fixed seed; F1-vs-weight curve was jagged / non-monotonic (reproducible at that seed, not sampling noise).

◌ CANDIDATE — validation gate pending

Applying Cycle 1’s rule: a seed=100-only improvement of this size is not yet trustworthy as “better.” Multi-seed / k-fold gate must run before this can replace baseline.

Design

Two questions: (a) for an eventual hardware e-nose, how much numeric precision does each stage need; (b) with a reproducible baseline (macro F1 = 0.8676), can its neighborhood be searched systematically instead of by hand?

Build

Added -adc-bits and -quantize-bits / -quantize-g-bits / -quantize-g-fs; built OFAT sweep + refinement on the most sensitive factor.

Test

Apparent results before the bug catch: raw 24-bit ADC downsampled losslessly to 16-bit; quantizing v to 10 bits cost −0.007 F1 (−0.013 with g); full bit sweep looked lossless to ~10 bits with a clear drop only at ≤5 bits.

Those precision conclusions are now under INVALIDATED / RERUN REQUIRED because of the clock-scheduling bug documented above.

Sweep side: see candidate card — w_kc_mbon=13 / 0.8870 remains gated.

Learn

Future hardware precision budget should prioritize weight / PN-encoding resolution over analog neuron fidelity — subject to re-measurement after the quantize fix.

Cycle 1 rule applies directly: seed=100-only improvements are not yet “better.”

Redesign

Fixed clock scheduling; flagged bit-sweep numbers for re-measurement now that quantization runs on every real integration step.

Queued (not yet run): validation tool against best_wmbon13_seed100 before it can replace baseline.

Further probes spun up: CMA-ES KC readout ceiling (bypass STDP), continual-learning forgetting test, re-run of Cycle 1 KC-coding SVM diagnostic against current best config.

Key decision

Publish the bug; revoke trust in the old bit-sweep; keep w_kc_mbon=13 labeled candidate until the validation gate passes.

Practice

What this changed in our modeling practice

  • Multi-seed statistics by default — a single attractive F1 is not a publishable claim.
  • Preprocessing must be audited — missing StandardScaler can invent sensor “importance.”
  • Improvements require validation gates — candidates stay labeled until multi-seed / k-fold support exists.
  • Bugs invalidate conclusions, not just code — the quantization clock bug downgraded prior precision claims until rerun.

Reflection

What we would do differently

  • Hardware — verify critical symbols/footprints against datasheets before routing; define the validation ladder before first layout; treat the PD/TIA region as a measurement instrument (detail).
  • Wet lab — verify host-required elements before synthesis orders; apply ionomycin × VUAA1 diagnostics from the first reporter test (matrix).
  • Dry lab — multi-seed statistics and preprocessing audits before trusting rankings or “improvements.”
  • External evidence sequencing — interview paying customers earlier; commercial and education narratives stay on their owner pages.

Reflection

Hardware in particular

  • Verify every critical symbol and footprint against the manufacturer datasheet before beginning detailed routing.
  • Define the complete validation ladder before layout so that test points, gain options, optical blanks and calibration access were built into the first PCB.
  • Most importantly: treat the photodiode/TIA region as a measurement instrument, not as an ordinary analog circuit. That lesson now guides V2.
