Education

Learning with communities, not only teaching at them

Structure ready · no verified programs or learning outcomes published yet

Educational purpose

AeroSense sits at the intersection of synthetic biology, insect-inspired olfaction, neural-style computation, and portable sensing hardware (see Description). Public and classroom explanations of such systems often collapse into two failure modes: treating living-cell sensors as finished products, or treating “AI/neural” labels as proof of understanding.

The education track is therefore designed around three learning needs the team identified from the project itself (not from a completed community needs assessment):

  1. Misunderstanding: odor sensing is often imagined as a single “nose molecule detector,” rather than overlapping receptor patterns that require careful interpretation.
  2. Access gap: fly-circuit ideas (antennal-lobe contrast, mushroom-body sparse codes) and living-cell fluorescence assays are usually unavailable outside specialized labs.
  3. Responsible-technology need: learners should practice separating illustrative models from experimental results and refusing unsupported clinical or field claims—aligned with Integrated Human Practices and project boundaries.

This page uses an outcomes-and-iteration narrative. Event counts and photo galleries are not substitutes for evaluation. No programs, partners, attendance figures, feedback, learning gains, quotes, or continuation promises are invented here.

Interactive tools (software under development)

Status In development

The team maintains a local static suite called Odor Pixel Suite (Pattern Recognition; Identity & Path Deduction; Mixture Inference). It uses illustrative virtual-receptor / fictional-gate models to practice pattern reading, deduction, and mixture intuition—not experimental measurement reports. It is not published as a download from this wiki page, has unfinished media-license verification in its asset manifest, and is not counted as a delivered outreach program or measured learning gain.

Tool Plain-language description (non-interactive alternative) Wiki download
Game 1 · Pattern Recognition Learners see an illustrative multi-channel “LED scent code” and choose which labeled odor pattern it matches. Teaching point: identification from a pattern across channels, not a single sensor spike. Not hosted on this wiki
Game 2 · Identity & Path Deduction Learners gather clues in a fictional gate labyrinth to identify a “phantom” by combining identity evidence along a path. Teaching point: sequential deduction under incomplete information. Not hosted on this wiki
Game 3 · Mixture Inference Learners inspect a 12-channel illustrative mixture signal and infer simplified sources/ratios. Teaching point: mixtures are not always linear sums; game outcomes must not be read as real concentrations or affinities. Not hosted on this wiki

Progress in the suite is intended to stay in browser local storage without remote gameplay APIs. Until the suite is copied into wiki static hosting with verified licenses, facilitator guide, and an evaluation plan, it remains a development asset—not an Education medal evidence item.

Limitations and next steps

  • Limitation: zero verified delivered programs on this wiki; no attendance, feedback, or learning-gain data.
  • Limitation: Odor Pixel Suite is not yet a licensed, wiki-hosted, facilitator-ready toolkit.
  • Limitation: primary application domain for AeroSense remains open, which constrains domain-specific education examples (see Human Practices · responsibility question).
  • Next step: confirm audiences and partners; write objectives + instruments before delivery.
  • Next step: release a CC-licensed facilitator packet (guide, slides/worksheets or approved alternatives, answer key) usable outside NTHU.

  • Next step: log iterations in the Notebook and refuse photos of identifiable minors without permission.
