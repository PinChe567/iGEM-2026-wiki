Measurement

Define how each dataset becomes a measurement

  1. Home
  2. Dry Lab
  3. Measurement

Abstract

AeroSense combines microscopy, fluorescence time series, a four-channel reader and computational predictions. We define each dataset’s observation unit, reference and transformation, separating reporter-positive fractions, corrected responses and prediction scores. Source records, exclusions and sensitivity analyses make these comparisons traceable.

Introduction

GCaMP fluorescence reflects calcium and reporter abundance. Our analysis therefore distinguishes image-based expression screening, stimulus-associated kinetics, instrument calibration and model evaluation. Each uses an explicit denominator and comparison. Acquisition and biological procedures are documented in Experiments.

GCaMP reference [1] · Microscopy reporting reference [2] · Experiments

Methods and data analysis

Original acquisitions remain unchanged. Derived tables identify the condition, channel or receptor, source file, time or field, exclusions and technical repeat structure.

Microscopy: reporter-associated objects

The screen contains 25 matched fields, 12 conditions and 2,624 eligible DAPI-connected objects from 8-bit channel exports. A 3 × 3 median filter and BLUE threshold >128 digital numbers (DN) define 8-connected components, without watershed. Every component ≥200 pixels receives an inclusion or exclusion record. Blank and receptor conditions use identical rules.

StepRetained analysis rule
Object eligibilityArea 200–2,200 pixels; bounding-box aspect ratio ≤2.5; fill fraction ≥0.4; at least 49 pixels from the image border; no overlap with the masked annotation neighborhood.
Reporter regionDAPI object plus up to 12 pixels assigned to the nearest BLUE component. Local background uses a 24–48-pixel ring, excludes BLUE foreground and requires at least 100 pixels.
Object score90th percentile of reporter-region intensity minus the median local background. Strictly greater than the channel threshold defines positivity.
Reported fractionSum of positive objects divided by sum of eligible objects, with field-level counts retained. RED and GREEN are scored separately.

Thresholds and statistical checks

The threshold is the largest 99th percentile among three blank-field score distributions: RED 16.60 DN and GREEN 11.95 DN. The 95th- and 99.5th-percentile rules provide sensitivity checks. Leave-one-blank-field-out analysis trains on two fields and tests the third; the all-blank fitted result is separate. ORCO-only, which lacks mCherry, supplies an additional RED-negative comparison.

One-sided Fisher exact tests compare positive/negative object counts in 11 nonblank conditions with blank. Holm correction is applied separately to the 11 RED and 11 GREEN tests. We report fraction differences in percentage points and nominal 95% Wilson intervals.

Area fraction and object fraction

Pixel-area and selected-object fractions retain separate denominators. Eligible objects cover 9.5–22.8% of segmented DAPI foreground in receptor conditions. QC records exclusions, background-pixel counts, clipping, source labels and threshold sensitivity. Display contrast adjustments do not enter measurement tables.

Odor assays: baseline and reference corrections

Test 1: peak response relative to its preceding baseline

Test 1 records four stages per condition: five baseline readings, 15 buffer readings, five new baseline readings and 15 test readings. P3(x) is the largest mean of three consecutive readings. Each excursion uses its preceding baseline.

R = 100 × [P3(test) / mean(F0b) − 1] − 100 × [P3(buffer) / mean(F0a) − 1] (percentage points)

Separate buffer-reference and untransfected-control contrasts remain in the tables. Source-reported gain incompatibility in rows B and C excludes 24 cross-stage metrics; their raw traces remain. The other 72 metrics are conditional on available metadata because numerical gain was not exported. Test 1 has no matched DMSO control.

Test 2: post-addition change minus pre-addition change

Test 2 has five pre-addition readings over 48 s and 15 post-addition readings over 168 s, at 12 s intervals. Each stage is normalized to its own first reading before subtraction. The primary endpoint uses both complete stages; an equal-window first-48-s comparison tests sensitivity to duration.

δFpre = 100 × [Fpre(48) / Fpre(0) − 1]; δFpost = 100 × [Fpost(168) / Fpost(0) − 1]; S = δFpost − δFpre (percentage points)

The 72 wells cover 12 constructs or controls × six stimuli, with one well per condition. The mean post-addition / mean pre-addition ratio remains an alternative audit quantity. Peak-based R and endpoint S are reported separately.

Construct-matched VUAA1 reference

Test 2 uses same-construct DMSO and VUAA1 wells as references. The ratio covers ten tuning-receptor + ORCO constructs and ORCO-only, excluding the blank control. VUAA1 provides an Orco agonist reference.

N = (Sodor − SDMSO) / (SVUAA1 − SDMSO) (dimensionless)

DMSO and VUAA1 anchor 0 and 1 without imposing bounds. Negative values and values above 1 are retained.

Odor-response figures and data · VUAA1 reference [3]

Light-sheet recordings: ROI fluorescence over time

Each ROI uses acquisition timestamps and ΔF/F0 = 100 × (F/F0 − 1). Full-recording F0 is that ROI’s mean over 8 ≤ t < 16 s; local comparisons use 10 to 2 s before odor onset. All 696 frames per retained curve remain. TH-GFP uses supplied ImageJ bleaching-corrected intensities; GH146-GCaMP6f uses measured means without added bleaching correction or smoothing.

The GH146 recordings share a fly and field; TH uses a different field and reporter. ROI and whole-image traces remain separate.

Hardware: demodulation, calibration and cell trajectories

Complex signals and isolated-source mixing

One photodiode records four LED-frequency channels as z = 1000(I + iQ) mV. Subtract the contemporaneous illuminated-water mean in complex space. The own-well 10 µM control supplies unit phase p, giving the signed projection Re[(z − zblank) × conjugate(p)].

Each isolated-source response divided by 10 µM forms a column of M. Stacking four I and four Q components gives an 8 × 4 matrix. Ridge inversion uses ĉ = (MᵀM + λσmax²I)⁻¹Mᵀy, with λ = 0.0001 selected from 15 single-source/blank technical leave-one-acquisition-out folds. Mixed-loading truth does not tune λ; negative estimates remain.

Uniform-loading fluorescein calibration

Set A uses linear y = a + bc/10 with standards 0, 0.1, 1 and 10 µM; entire 0.1 and 1 µM levels are withheld in turn. Its phase reference is the highest remaining training standard. The expanded 0–20 µM dataset, Set B, uses:

y(c) = a + b × expm1(kc/20) / expm1(k); c = (20/k) × log1p[(y − a) × expm1(k)/b]

All Set B channels use this exponential family with separate coefficients and equal-weight loading means. Entire 1, 5 and 10 µM levels, including technical reads, are withheld in turn; 0 and 20 µM remain anchors. Reportable estimates require a unique inverse within 0–20 µM. Family selection compares holdout RMSE and monotonicity. Both 2.5 µM loadings remain in QC sensitivities, outside the primary fit. Extra residual corrections, tested with nested concentration holdouts, were not adopted.

Living cells: normalize each acquisition to its own start

Cell trajectories use raw amplitude A = 1000√(I² + Q²) mV. Blank and post-VUAA1 sequences are each divided by their first amplitude. The late-window contrast is 100 × [mean(final three normalized post-VUAA1 readings) − mean(final three normalized blank readings)], in percentage points. Absolute pre/post triplicates are reported separately.

Cross-platform analysis pairs five nominal times, 0, 12, 24, 36 and 48 s: hardware readings 1/3/5/7/9 and corresponding wet-lab readings, without interpolation. Pearson/Spearman levels, first differences and detrended curves are compared across all 4 × 4 pairings, including ORCO-only. A control-adjusted sensitivity subtracts medium from VUAA1 own-start ratios in hardware, and DMSO from VUAA1 own-start ratios in wet lab, without further F0 or N(t) correction. First-point-omission, ten-point interpolated and original-clock sensitivities are retained.

Discussion

Microscopy fractions describe selected objects sharing fields and cultures. Fisher tests and Wilson intervals omit clustering, segmentation and threshold uncertainty; blank holdouts measure within-batch behavior. GFP and GCaMP traces are different biological readouts. Neither brightness nor the separate-well VUAA1 ratio measures transfection efficiency or isolates coexpression. Or7a’s denominator is only 2.19 percentage points, amplifying small changes. Odor wells lack biological replication; Test 2 also compares unequal 48 s and 168 s windows. Corrected changes alone do not establish receptor-specific activation.

Calibration-family selection reuses the evaluation folds and remains exploratory, not prospective validation. The isolated-source matrix does not establish arbitrary-mixture recovery, and dye calibration does not transfer directly to cells. Own-start normalization conceals absolute post-addition drops; dilution, optics and cell state remain unresolved explanations.

Time-point correlations are descriptive: autocorrelation, common trends and interpolation can produce apparent agreement. No p-values or biological-replicate counts are inferred, and the nominal hardware schedule is not a verified synchronized clock. Row-normalized optical matrices compare dependent patterns, not absolute power and voltage. Model splits define generalization; simulation variation omits material and force-field uncertainty. The PN–KC–MBON training engine and complete fitted preprocessing records remain reproducibility gaps.

Conclusion

Explicit denominators, controls and evaluation splits make each result interpretable. Preserving raw scales, exclusions and sensitivity analyses supports reuse of the measurements and identifies where further validation is needed.

Supplementary methods

Complete odor curves with a fixed reference denominator

For stimulus s and construct c, subtract the measured 48 s pre-addition endpoint change from every post-addition point, then subtract DMSO at the same local acquisition time. Divide by the fixed VUAA1-minus-DMSO endpoint difference at 168 s. The final point equals Results Figure 14; VUAA1 equals 1 at that endpoint by definition. The pre-addition change is a constant correction, not an extrapolated trace; initial offsets remain. Test 1 retains its four raw stages.

Ss,c(t) = 100 × [Fpost,s,c(t)/Fpost,s,c(0) − 1] − δFpre,s,c; Ns,c(t) = [Ss,c(t) − SDMSO,c(t)] / [SVUAA1,c(168) − SDMSO,c(168)]

Nominal object-count uncertainty

Fisher tests use [[positive, eligible − positive], [blank-positive, blank-eligible − blank-positive]] with the greater alternative. Holm adjustment orders p-values, multiplies by the remaining test count, applies the cumulative maximum and caps at 1. Wilson intervals use z = 1.96 and the eligible object count.
