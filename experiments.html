Abstract

This record covers bacterial cloning and plasmid preparation, HEK293 expression and odor-response assays, and fly brain imaging. It documents the acquired 25-field microscopy dataset and P2 pilot alongside clearly identified reference protocols. Results consolidates the interpretation of the measurements.

The executed P2 kinetic pilot is documented separately: one 96-well HEK293 plate at approximately 72 h after transfection, before G418 selection, with 32 archived instrument runs. It differs from the 384-well protocol specification below in plate geometry, volume, manual addition and sampling. No unrun specification is retrospectively assigned to P2.

Introduction

DNA preparation, engineered-cell assays and fly-brain imaging answer different questions. Each track retains its own samples and conclusions. Mammalian experiments use HEK293; missing execution details are not inferred from a published protocol.

Six stages, three tracks

The core wet-lab record follows colony plates → DNA electrophoresis → transfection efficiency assessment → odor testing → G418 selection → post-selection odor testing. The fly-brain study remains an independent supporting track. Follow the same six stages in Results.

Cloning supplies the DNA. HEK293 assays assess the engineered sensor. Fly-brain imaging provides biological context in a separate preparation.

03 · Image analysis

Endpoint microscopy and image analysis

Reproduce the DAPI-object fluorescence screen reported in Results.

Executed analysis. All 25 fields and all 2,624 eligible objects are retained in the published data tables and reproducibility package.

Source data and experimental context

The host was HEK293, as confirmed by the team. Imaging occurred approximately 44–48 h after transfection and before G418. The team fixed cells with 2% paraformaldehyde (PFA), performed no permeabilization step, and then stained with DAPI. The source workbook contains plate-layout and reagent-calculation records, including DAPI/G418 layouts and lipid-reagent mixtures. These records do not independently establish experimental execution or an assay outcome; the VUAA1/odor grids contain labels without response measurements. The workbook does not supply objective, numerical aperture, camera model, gain, acquisition software, DAPI concentration, fixation duration, or field coordinates. Neither the source TIFF tags nor the workbook establishes these missing acquisition details. The biological preparation and transfection record is linked from Experiments; undocumented settings are not reconstructed from a literature protocol.

Conservative DAPI segmentation

The BLUE plane was extracted from each exported TIFF. A 3 × 3 median filter was used for segmentation only, followed by a common threshold of >128 on the 0–255 export scale and 8-connected labeling. The bottom-right annotation rectangle (x ≥85% of image width and y ≥90% of image height) was excluded equally in every field. Eligible objects had area 200–2200 pixels, bounding-box aspect ratio ≤2.5, and object area/bounding-box area ≥0.4. Objects within 49 pixels of a frame edge or whose measurement neighborhood intersected the annotation exclusion were rejected. No watershed split was imposed on merged objects.

These rules define a conservative image-analysis subset, not a validated nuclear census. Segmentation has been visually inspected, but not benchmarked against independently annotated cell boundaries. Small touching nuclei or nuclear fragments can still pass these geometric filters, so an accepted object is not guaranteed to equal one cell. DAPI is a nuclear counterstain commonly used with fixed preparations. Here, fixation is confirmed and no permeabilization was performed; completeness and uniformity of nuclear labeling were not independently assessed. Fixed DAPI images do not recover the cells’ viability before fixation. [4]

Reporter measurement and positivity threshold

The full Measurement record adds an explicit statistical specification, all three same-batch blank-field holdouts and the area-versus-object audit. These additions leave the original segmentation and primary thresholds unchanged.

For each eligible object, the signal region contains its nuclear mask plus pixels within 12 pixels, assigned to the nearest blue connected component to limit sharing between adjacent objects. The local background is sampled 24–48 pixels from that object, excluding blue foreground; at least 100 background pixels are required. For RED and GREEN separately, the score is the signal region’s 90th percentile intensity minus the background median. Measurements use the original exported channel values, before the display adjustments.

This nearest-DAPI expansion does not segment cell boundaries. Excluding blue foreground from the background ring also does not exclude every neighboring cell’s cytoplasm. Fluorescent cytoplasm within that ring can raise the estimated background and reduce an object's score, particularly in dense fields.

The primary positivity threshold is the maximum of the three blank-field 99th percentiles of these object scores: RED >16.60 DN and GREEN >11.95 DN. DN denotes the 8-bit exported digital value. Alternative P95 and P99.5 blank thresholds are reported for sensitivity analysis. Thresholds are calibrated and evaluated using the same blank set, so the blank fraction is a calibration diagnostic, not an independent false-positive validation.

The reported fraction is 100 × positive eligible objects / all eligible objects. Condition summaries pool these counts across fields; they do not average percentages with different denominators. “Both” indicates both channel criteria within one object's measurement region. It does not prove two-plasmid delivery, protein localization, or co-expression in a viable cell.

Image processing and presentation

Original TIFF and workbook files were opened read-only and verified by SHA-256 checksums. New figures use fixed linear display windows across all conditions: DAPI 0–255, GREEN 0–120 and RED 0–180; brightfield remains 0–255. The enhanced display can clip bright exported values and is used only for viewing. Original-level views remain available. No sharpening, deconvolution, content removal, generative enhancement or reconstruction of missing detail was applied. The same-field RGB merges combine the original channel positions; no registration correction was imposed. [5] [6]

Web previews are resized with Lanczos resampling and saved as lossless WebP. The original scale bars remain within the full field; they were not used to infer pixel size. Object overlays mark classifications on a dimmed merge for legibility and have full-resolution PNG versions for audit.

04 · Pre-selection

P2: the 96-well kinetic assay

Reproduce the fluorescence time courses and descriptive well metrics reported in Results.

Executed; critical metadata incomplete. Runs 904–935 were acquired on 10–11 October 2026. This is a manual-addition 96-well pilot, separate from the unrun 384-well specification that follows.

Samples, experimental unit and preparation

The team confirmed on 11 October 2026 that HEK293 cells were measured approximately 72 h after transfection, without G418 selection. Only this plate is currently available. The plate contains one well per condition × stimulus; longitudinal readings are technical timepoints, not replicate wells or independent cultures. The labels identify intended construct conditions and do not establish stable clonal lines. Their identity is not automatically matched to the earlier 44–48 h fixed-cell microscopy cultures.

Columns 1–10 are Or7a, Or19a, Or42a, Or98a, Or22a, Or67a, Or67b, Or59b, Or85b and Or35a, respectively, each with ORCO. Column 11 is ORCO only; column 12 is untransfected HEK293. Rows A–H receive VUAA1, ethyl propionate, isopentyl acetate, 3-octanol, benzaldehyde, 2-heptanone, valeric acid and buffer alone, respectively.

The confirmed assay buffer contains 140 mM NaCl, 5 mM KCl, 2 mM CaCl2, 1 mM MgCl2, 5 mM HEPES-Na and 20 mM glucose, at pH 7.3 and 340 mOsm. The team reports initially aqueous VOC preparations subsequently diluted into this buffer. Final VOC concentrations are unknown. VUAA1 uses DMSO; the final DMSO fraction and an equivalent DMSO-only vehicle condition are not documented. The source report lists nominal final VUAA1 at 50 µM, without an independently verified preparation record.

Acquisition and additions

The report identifies a BMG CLARIOstar Plus with SMART Control/MARS and a GREINER 96 F-bottom plate. It records bottom optics, a −4.1 mm focal height, LP 504 dichroic, 20 flashes per well per cycle, 0.5 s settling, enhanced dynamic range off, temperature control off and no shaking during the response read. These are source-reported settings. The instrument CSVs directly confirm the 482–16 excitation / 530–40 emission filter labels and 12 s read spacing; they do not contain numeric gain values.

  1. At the reported initial 100 µL volume, acquire F0a: five readings at 0, 12, 24, 36 and 48 s.
  2. Add 50 µL plain buffer and acquire the first response, labeled blank: fifteen readings from 0 to 168 s, every 12 s. The well now contains 150 µL.
  3. Acquire F0b in the same wells: five readings with the same timing.
  4. Add 50 µL of the reported 4× working preparation, or buffer for row H, to reach 200 µL. Acquire the test response: fifteen readings from 0 to 168 s.

The reported manual sequence uses an eight-channel pipette for columns 1–8 followed by 9–12. Injection timestamps and addition-to-first-read delays are unavailable. Each CSV restarts its time coordinate at its first reading; no interpolation bridges the unmeasured gaps. Header run-start times span 22:11:23 on 10 October to 00:35:25 on 11 October. The final recorded response contains a further 168 s of within-run observations. The headers do not encode a timezone.

Independent calculation

Each F0 is the mean of five readings. For each 15-reading response, calculate all thirteen contiguous three-reading means and take their maximum, Fpeak3. This reduces the influence of an isolated point but still selects a maximum; it is a descriptive summary rather than an unbiased effect estimator. The reported peak time is the center of the earliest maximizing window.

ΔFbuffer = (Fpeak3, buffer − F0a) / F0a
ΔFtest = (Fpeak3, test − F0b) / F0b
ΔFcorr = ΔFtest − ΔFbuffer

The two terms use different baselines and well volumes. Their difference is dimensionless; multiplied by 100 it is a difference in percentage points, not a single test-run fluorescence increase. Time-course plots show every measured test point relative to the paired F0b; raw-RFU panels are also provided. No cell-free-background subtraction, gain rescaling, interpolation, fitted response or significance test is applied.

Quality control, exclusions and normalization

The source report identifies gain mismatch in both baseline–response pairs of row B and in the first pair of row C. Coherent shifts across wells are consistent with this concern. All 24 corrected metrics from B/C are excluded; C’s second-pair arithmetic remains available as exploratory data. The remaining 72 corrected metrics are retained conditionally. Without recorded numeric gain, this does not certify the other pairs as gain-matched. Changing detector gain changes the RFU scale, so comparisons require acquisition consistency rather than a guessed correction from untransfected wells. [7]

The independent QC table reports each untransfected stage’s first, mean and median RFU, plus the median across the twelve wells of response-mean / paired-baseline-mean ratios. These formulas are explicit. The original QC field named median_col12_F0a equals the first reading in each row; its published ratio algorithm is unspecified. The original table is retained for audit, not silently relabeled.

All 96 well summaries retain unmasked arithmetic in clearly labeled *_computed columns. The *_retained columns leave excluded values empty, never zero. The source’s VUAA1 normalization is not used: it includes a negative Or7a denominator and otherwise depends on single, unvalidated reference wells. The recalculated six-well 3 SD value is an audit of the source report only; it is not a limit of detection or a response cutoff. No technical-read error bars, confidence intervals or p values are used to imply biological replication.

Acquisition limits

Numeric gains, final VOC concentrations, exact addition delays and a matched DMSO-only condition are unavailable. The source records partial detachment without quantifying it. P2 contains one well per condition × stimulus and no independent culture repeats; Results explains the consequences for interpretation.

The schedule below is the documented literature-informed reference protocol. The acquired 44–48 h microscopy and approximately 72 h P2 records both precede G418 selection.

Key parameters

Table 4. Literature-informed G418 selection and maintenance schedule, listing nominal concentrations and durations. It is a protocol target, not an AeroSense-validated kill curve or completed selection result.
Stage G418 concentration Duration
Selection start (24–48 h post-transfection) 100 µg/mL 2 weeks
Selection raised 150 µg/mL 1 week
Maintenance ~75 µg/mL Maintenance phase

Method

  1. Before transfection, run a G418 kill curve on untransfected host cells (draft range 0–800 µg/mL) for that cell lot, confluence, and G418 batch. Do not treat the table below as a confirmed effective dose.
  2. After transfection, allow 24–48 h recovery. Do not add G418 at seeding.
  3. Start selection at 100 µg/mL G418 for 2 weeks. Refresh selection medium every 2–3 days.
  4. Raise G418 to 150 µg/mL for 1 week.
  5. Maintain lines at ~75 µg/mL G418 thereafter.

The reference protocol describes little visible effect at days 1–2, bulk cell death at days 3–5 and resistant colonies at days 10–14. These intervals describe the reference method.

Controls & critical notes

  • Negative control Untransfected well at the same G418 dose throughout selection — every cell in it should die.

Add G418 24–48 h after transfection, not at seeding, so cells can recover and express NeoR.

The 100 → 150 → ~75 µg/mL schedule is retained in the reference protocol; the G418 record identifies the available project evidence.

Method

Read on a multimode plate reader with a two-channel injector, ex 485 nm / em 535 nm.

  1. Seed cells at 12,000–18,000 per well in black-wall, clear-bottom 384-well plates, 48–72 h before measurement.
  2. On the day of measurement, replace growth medium with 20 µL assay buffer per well.
  3. Round 1 — vehicle: record baseline F0 for 5 s, inject 7 µL plain VOC buffer, record 30 s at 1 read/s.
  4. Round 2 — odorant: into the same wells, inject 9 µL of VOC at 4× final concentration, same recording protocol.
  5. Layout: one compound per row; up to eight cell lines per 24-column plate. Use three wells per cell line and measure each VOC–OR pair on at least three separate days.

The volumes are self-consistent: 20 + 7 = 27 µL, then + 9 = 36 µL; 9/36 = 1/4, so a 4× stock dilutes exactly to 1× final. The vehicle injection simultaneously establishes the volume for the second round and measures the solvent and injection artefact alone.

Reagent preparation VUAA1 stock and VOC handling

VUAA1. Dissolve in DMSO to a 50 mM stock (18.4 mg/mL). Aliquot and store at −20 °C protected from light. Prepare the 4× working dilution fresh in VOC buffer.

VOCs. Dilute directly in VOC buffer, or first in DMSO for water-insoluble compounds. Prepare working solutions fresh daily. Store concentrated stocks in amber glass with PTFE-lined caps.

Controls & critical notes

  • Vehicle control Round-1 injection of plain VOC buffer — solvent and injection artefact.
  • Normalisation reference 50 µM VUAA1 for each cell line on the same plate/day. Normalise to the mean VUAA1 response of that line, not a same-well VUAA1 injection.
  • Specificity control Ca2+-free condition. Zboray et al. showed that Ca2+-free and Ca2+-free + EGTA buffers abolish the VUAA1 response [2].

Keep DMSO identical in VOC, vehicle, and VUAA1 wells.

CaCl2 in the assay buffer is required.

Use HEPES-Na rather than bicarbonate: the plate sits in ambient air.

06 · Analysis specification

Data analysis

Convert raw fluorescence into a VUAA1-normalised relative response for each receptor–stimulus pair.

Reference analysis. These equations describe the cited relative-response method. The acquired P2 data use the P2 analysis; fixed-cell images use the microscopy pipeline.

Relative-response definition, the ≤ 0.05 floor, plate/day VUAA1 mean normalisation, and Grubbs’ outlier removal follow Zboray et al. [2] Zboray et al., 2023 — full entry in References. . Zboray et al. denote the relative response R as ΔF. This page uses R and Rnorm to avoid mixing ΔF with ΔF/F0.

Key parameters

Table 7. Definitions and processing rules for the reference calcium-response analysis, including baseline, peak, relative response and same-plate VUAA1 normalization. These rules are not a claim of calibrated calcium concentration.
Parameter Value
Raw fluorescence Plate-reader intensity at ex 485 nm / em 535 nm
F0 Baseline recorded for 5 s before the relevant injection
Fmax Peak fluorescence in the post-injection window
Raw relative response R = (Fmax − F0) / F0
VUAA1-normalised response Rnorm = RVOC / mean(RVUAA1, corresponding cell line, same plate/day)
Zero floor Rnorm ≤ 0.05 treated as zero, following [2]
Outliers Removed by Grubbs’ test, following [2] (α not specified here)

Method

  1. Record raw fluorescence at ex 485 nm / em 535 nm. Take F0 as the 5 s baseline before the relevant injection.
  2. Take Fmax as the peak in the post-injection window. Compute R = (Fmax − F0) / F0.
  3. Normalise each VOC response to the mean 50 µM VUAA1 response of the corresponding OR cell line measured on the same plate/day: Rnorm = RVOC / mean(RVUAA1, corresponding cell line, same plate/day). Do not use a same-well VUAA1 injection as the denominator.
  4. Following [2], treat Rnorm ≤ 0.05 as zero, and remove outliers by Grubbs’ test.

Controls & critical notes

VOC responses are normalized to the mean 50 µM VUAA1 response of the corresponding OR cell line measured on the same plate/day. Do not use a same-well VUAA1 injection as the denominator.

Flies and orientation

The TH preparation was 5137;TH, using the UAS-mCD8::GFP reporter. The GH146 preparation used UAS-GCaMP6f. Female flies were approximately 7–14 days old, starved for 2–6 h and kept on ice under cold anesthesia for 2 h.

The TH field was acquired from the fly’s left side; GH146 was acquired from its right side. In the supplied images, posterior is left and anterior is right. ROI names refer to image coordinates.

Acquisition and odor timing

Each recording contains 696 five-plane stacks acquired with 488 nm excitation and approximately 33 ms exposure per plane. Analyses use the elapsed time in the original TIFF filenames: 0–132.013 s for TH and 0–132.012 or 132.013 s for GH146. A stack interval is approximately 0.190 s.

The table gives team-confirmed stimulus windows. Web recordings follow acquisition time; the source AVI playback rate of 6 frames/s is not the acquisition clock.

RecordingOdor presentation
TH-GFP and GH146: three odors20–40 s · isopentyl acetate; 60–80 s · 3-octanol; 100–120 s · 4-methylcyclohexanol
GH146: four odors20–35 s · ethyl propionate; 55–70 s · benzaldehyde; 90–105 s · 2-heptanone; 120–132 s · valeric acid

ROI analysis

GH146 regional means were measured from maximum-intensity projections of the five-plane stacks, using the saved ImageJ rectangular ROIs. Whole-image means include the complete field. No bleaching correction or background subtraction was applied to GH146.

TH ΔF/F₀ curves use the supplied ImageJ bleaching-corrected ROI values. The correction used an exponential fit to the tissue-reference ROI named background. Original and corrected measurements remain in the source data.

For each odor, F₀ is the mean fluorescence over the 8 s window ending 2 s before stimulus onset. Local ΔF/F₀ (%) = 100 × (F − F₀) / F₀. The common protocol therefore uses baselines of 10–18, 50–58 and 90–98 s. The four-odor GH146 sequence uses 10–18, 45–53, 80–88 and 110–118 s.

For the full 132 s curves, each ROI uses its own mean over 8 ≤ t < 16 s as F₀, with ΔF/F₀ (%) = 100 × (F − F₀) / F₀. TH uses bleaching-corrected measurements and GH146 uses the original regional means. These full-recording baselines stay fixed; the odor-aligned panels use the local baselines above. All curves are unsmoothed.

Results record

Evidence is reported on the Results page, where images and analyses can be read alongside their controls and limitations. The protocols above specify what each assay is intended to measure. A fluorescence image alone does not establish cell viability or a cell-count-based transfection efficiency, and survival under G418 does not by itself demonstrate a functional odor response. The fly recordings and regional fluorescence analyses are in Results.

Method provenance

The archive separates three forms of evidence: source photographs and instrument files, team-confirmed activity records, and reference protocols. Methods above identify which record supports each experiment; Results consolidates the analytical limits.

Fly imaging, fixed-cell expression screening and P2 kinetics retain separate preparation and acquisition records, so their findings can be traced to the experiment that produced them.

Conclusion

The three experimental tracks separately document cloning, HEK293 expression and odor testing, and exploratory fly brain imaging. Its value depends on keeping construct verification, expression, selection and sensing performance as distinct evidence requirements. The available methods support that progression without claiming completion of experiments whose records are still being assembled.

Safety

All work is contained. E. coli DH5α is Risk Group 1 (BSL-1); HEK293 is handled at BSL-2 in a Class II biosafety cabinet. Aromatic VOC ligands are flammable and toxic and are handled in a fume hood per their Safety Data Sheets. Nothing is released outside the laboratory.
