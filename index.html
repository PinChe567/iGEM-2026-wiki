01 / The crate

The fruit still looks as it should.

A stored batch can begin to fail long before the surface gives anything away.

02 / The air

The first change is not a bruise. It is a smell we cannot quite name.

Biological activity reshapes the volatile mixture around food while everything still looks ordinary.

03 / Another reader

A fruit fly would have known sooner.

Insect olfaction evolved to read those airborne patterns. Our eyes were never built for this.

04 / A borrowed nose

So we asked living cells to do the smelling.

Drosophila odorant receptors, with Orco, are planned for HEK293T cells. Calcium flux becomes light through GCaMP.

05 / Making light legible

A faint glow is not yet a measurement.

Hardware is being designed to catch that fluorescence — light, photodiode, amplification, then a digital trace.

How the reader is built →

06 / A grammar for odor

The trace still has to become a pattern we can trust.

The decoder follows fly computation: contrast first, then a sparse code, then a call we can write down.

07 / Before the warning

That is the story AeroSense is trying to finish.

A bio-digital platform being developed for earlier screening of fungal-associated odor-pattern change in stored food. It is research in progress, not a finished detector.
