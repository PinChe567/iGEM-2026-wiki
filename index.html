Decoding airborne signals with living cells.

AeroSense is designed to integrate engineered HEK293T sensor cells, fly-inspired neural computation, and portable optical readout into a bio-digital platform for recognizing volatile organic compound patterns.

The signal we are missing

Patterns of volatile organic compounds (VOCs) in air can carry information relevant to agriculture, environmental monitoring, food safety, and health. Conventional electronic noses remain limited when mixtures are complex and long-term stability matters [CITATION REQUIRED — TEAM TO VERIFY]. AeroSense does not claim to have detected a disease or to have reached a stated sensitivity target; this page introduces the design direction and the evidence we still need to gather.

Prior work shows that insect olfactory receptors can be expressed in mammalian cell lines for odorant profiling, including volatile plant biomarkers [1] Zboray et al., 2023 — full entry in References & Credits. . Separately, sparse coding ideas drawn from insect circuits have been formalized as locality-sensitive hashing algorithms for efficient similarity search [2] Dasgupta et al., 2017 — full entry in References & Credits. . AeroSense plans to connect these research threads into one documented platform.

One signal, three engineered layers

The system is designed as three coupled layers. Each layer is a research track with its own page; none of the summaries below are reported as completed AeroSense results.

Layer 01 · Living sensor

HEK293T receptor cells

Drosophila olfactory receptors (ORs) with the co-receptor Orco are planned for heterologous expression in HEK293T cells, with GCaMP6 fluorescence as the designed calcium readout.

Open Design
Layer 02 · Neural decoder

Fly-inspired computation

The computational track is designed around antennal-lobe-inspired contrast processing and mushroom-body-inspired sparse representation for classification workflows.

Open Model
Layer 03 · Portable reader

Optical and edge hardware

The hardware track targets optical excitation and readout, a photodiode signal chain, and an edge-computing workflow for local preprocessing and downstream analysis.

Open Hardware

How a measurement moves through AeroSense

The sequence below is the intended path from exposure to an interpretable output. It is labeled as a proposed workflow until the team supplies end-to-end validation.

Proposed system workflow
  1. VOC exposure Sample gas contacts the sensing interface under controlled delivery conditions.
  2. Receptor activation Engineered OR/Orco complexes are designed to respond to target odorants.
  3. Calcium-dependent fluorescence GCaMP6 is planned to convert intracellular calcium change into an optical signal.
  4. Optical acquisition Excitation and photodiode readout are designed to capture the fluorescence response.
  5. Model transformation Time-series signals are intended to enter fly-inspired preprocessing and representation stages.
  6. Interpretable classification output Downstream models are planned to return a labeled pattern call with documented uncertainty.
