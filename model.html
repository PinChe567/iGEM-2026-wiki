Gas arrival at the wells

Arrival is measured from a concentration step at the modeled inlet to the area-mean gas concentration in the first layer above the liquid. The calculation stops at the gas side of that interface; it does not predict uptake into the medium or the cell response.

We first compared how a concentration step moves through the chamber. The movie holds the color scale fixed across geometries and flows; the maps below resolve that progression in a vertical section and immediately above each well.

The maps reveal where gas remains dilute, but choosing a flow also requires a time threshold. We therefore tracked each well’s surface-area mean as it approaches the inlet concentration; the shaded curves retain spatial variation around that mean.

At 3 mL/min and nominal gas diffusivity 10 mm²/s, the four surfaces reach 10% of inlet concentration in 5.30–6.29 s and 90% in 47.80–49.42 s. Across the three tested diffusivities, the slowest surface reaches 90% in approximately 50 s on the main grid.

Geometry was compared at the same flow and diffusivity to separate redistribution from simply pumping faster. Read each interval from t10 to t90 and compare its t50 marker: the shelf redirects gas without giving the shortest interval in every case.

For the selected shelf, the slowest surface t90 is 149.3, 49.4 and 29.9 s at 1, 3 and 5 mL/min. The 3 mL/min candidate retains timing margin without treating faster arrival as the only design criterion. The geometry comparison and numerical sensitivity checks below test the effects of chamber design and discretization.

Finally, flow and diffusivity sweeps test the operating range, while grid and time-step checks show how much the timing depends on discretization. The small separation between geometries must be read alongside these numerical shifts.

Hardware simulations / 02

Liquid and membrane transport

Gas must also cross the liquid to reach bottom-attached cells. We varied liquid depth and diffusivity before adding a membrane; compare depth effects first, then the membrane’s added delay.

The first comparison places gas-side wash-in and delivery to the well floor on their respective time axes. It tests whether chamber equilibration is a useful proxy for exposure of bottom-attached cells.

At 100 µL per well, the nominal liquid depth is 8.929 mm. With an ideal concentration step at the surface, no mixing and liquid diffusivity D = 10⁻⁹ m²/s, the model gives a bottom t90 of 22.83 h. The relation t90 ≈ 1.031105 L²/D makes liquid depth a strong design variable: halving the path reduces this idealized time fourfold. [1]

Depth and effective diffusivity were varied independently to ask which design change could shorten that path. The depth sweep tests geometric distance; the mixing multiplier represents a hypothetical transport enhancement.

A membrane adds another barrier whose importance may depend on liquid mixing. The thickness–diffusivity–partitioning sweep separates those regimes: color tracks delay, while gray marks conditions that do not reach the target during the simulated window.

The coupled PDMS sweep tests 243 material and geometry combinations. A representative 100 µm film changes t90 from 22.85 to 23.39 h under stagnant-liquid assumptions; with liquid diffusion increased 100-fold, the same comparison becomes 0.25 versus 0.85 h. The liquid can dominate one regime and the membrane another. This supports the no-film baseline until a target molecule and measured permeability justify adding a layer.

Hardware simulations / Discussion

Discussion

Optical geometry, filtering and transport impose different limits. Nonzero optical mixing calls for a measured channel-mixing matrix; LED-synchronous leakage calls for an illuminated blank. Gas-surface arrival establishes when the chamber supplies a well, while liquid diffusion determines the separate path to bottom-attached cells. A single end-to-end response time cannot be inferred by substituting one for another.

The shelf is a compact way to redirect flow, not the fastest geometry in every comparison. The selected 3 mL/min condition provides timing margin against the stated 60 s criterion; grid sensitivity makes a near-threshold 2.5 mL/min result less decisive. Together with the membrane sweep, these comparisons favor measurable delivery and a simple no-film baseline over adding uncharacterized layers.

The optical and filter results are predictions under their stated source and noise assumptions. The constructed OD4 disc also differs from the selected film in mounting geometry; its modeled performance does not establish compatibility with the reader.

The gas calculation ends above the liquid surface; this separate layer model describes subsequent transport. Its diffusivities and partition coefficients are sensitivity inputs. No-film delivery and recovery at the cell plane provide the physical comparison for any proposed membrane.

Physical checks must cover blank and dye calibration, actual detector/filter response, one fluorescent source well at a time, LED-synchronous background and noise, and assembled-path flow, pressure and interface concentration history. These measurements test the design decisions rather than being supplied by the simulations.

Hardware simulations / Conclusion

Conclusion

The calculations support one LEE 101 layer, an integral shelf and a conditional 3 mL/min operating point. They also identify liquid-path length as a potential bottleneck. These design choices now have explicit optical, gas-delivery and cell-plane measurements against which they can be tested.

Odor decoding / Introduction

The challenge: sensor drift

Electronic noses combine cross-sensitive chemical sensors with pattern recognition. Aging, poisoning and environmental changes can shift the response to the same analyte, weakening a classifier trained on earlier measurements. Calibration samples, ensembles and adaptation can reduce this loss, at the cost of additional measurements or assumptions about the shift. [1] [2]

We tested sparse neural representations and a locally learned readout using pooled classification and unseen-day SNN evaluation. The separate long-term drift analysis uses conventional classifiers.

Explore the circuit

Biological rationale and implementation mapping

In Drosophila, projection neurons (PNs) relay olfactory information from the antennal lobe to Kenyon cells (KCs). Sparse PN→KC connectivity expands the representation; feedback from the anterior paired lateral (APL) neuron constrains KC activity. Mushroom body output neurons (MBONs) read this activity through synapses shaped by learning. [3] [4] [5] [6]

This organization suggests a practical division: keep a random feature expansion fixed, regulate its activity, and learn a small readout. Related motifs support similarity search and insect-inspired chemical classification. [7] [8] [9] Expansion alone does not guarantee robustness: its benefit depends on input statistics, connectivity and sparseness. [10] [11]

Odor decoding / Methods 03

Keep the three evaluation splits separate

Random exposure splits test separation within the collection; whole-day holdouts test unseen collection conditions. Both differ from the chronological UCI analysis. Preprocessing is fitted within each training partition.

KT evaluation uses five folds under two strategies. A gas-stratified random split mixes measurement days across training and testing. A whole-day split keeps each day intact, so test days are unseen. Imputation, scaling where applicable, PCA and bin edges are fitted to training data. Training comprises 300 cycles, each presenting every gas once; testing uses fifty presentations per gas with frozen learning.

The corpus unit is an exposure; a sampled presentation is a simulation trial. Repeated draws from an exposure do not create independent measurements. Macro-F1 is the unweighted mean of classwise F1 scores; accuracy is the fraction classified correctly. The metrics remain distinct throughout.

Odor decoding / Results 01

Classification varies across five gases

An overall score can conceal repeated class confusion. Compare the confusion matrix with sensor-response patterns, then use spike rasters to trace example decisions; the rasters explain dynamics rather than estimate accuracy.

Under stratified random five-fold evaluation, KT mean macro-F1 is 0.87 with reported variation ±0.03; the source separately reports approximately 88% accuracy. This extends the earlier single-run 0.868 baseline without implying an identical seed or test partition.

The dominant error is toluene → ethanol

In the illustrated classwise result, NO₂ recall is 98%. Toluene recall is 60%, with 24% assigned to ethanol, consistent with their similar sensor profiles. The illustrated F1 scores are SO₂ 0.88, NO₂ 0.93, toluene 0.73, ethanol 0.83 and NH₃ 0.83; these are not the five-fold class means.

A sparse response precedes the decision

Eight PN units receive current per KT exposure. KC activity is initially stronger, then APL inhibition suppresses it after approximately 5 ms; the source reports about 11% active KCs on average. Four of the five illustrated trials are correct, while toluene is classified as ethanol. These examples explain dynamics rather than provide an independent accuracy estimate.

Errors may reflect upstream information loss or an inadequate learned output. Compare conventional probes across representations on a common split, then with MBON output, to locate the loss.

Conventional probes were trained on sensor, PCA, PN and KC representations using the same 6,692 training and 1,674 held-out KT exposures. The separate UCI batches test long-term gain changes and classification loss. Read sensor traces before their common and gas-dependent components: a shared correction may not fit every sensor–gas pair.

The curves compare training-batch choice and test-concentration coverage, separating within-batch classification from between-batch transfer rather than estimating a single drift penalty.

Odor decoding / Results 05

New-gas learning trades off retention

We tested whether learning a new gas disrupts earlier classes. Read old- and new-gas fractions together; success on the added class alone would conceal forgetting.

Sequential learning can interfere with earlier knowledge. [16] In an initial KT experiment, the network learns four gases and then receives ten cycles of NH₃ alone, without rehearsal of the old gases.

The four old classes lose 4–10 percentage points while retaining most of their initial performance in this run. The discussion considers the role of representation overlap.

The teacher schedule prevents the proposed error signal

The implemented dopamine-inspired rule weakens connections to a wrong MBON, but that event did not trigger: the correct teacher acted early and inhibition suppressed competing responses.

Odor decoding / Discussion

Discussion

The practical lesson is to vary collection conditions independently before replacing the classifier. Record gas, device, concentration, humidity and day; interleave gases within days; evaluate feature-specific reference correction; and compare readout changes on fixed exposure partitions before increasing network size.

The HEK293 observations and the external sensor-array benchmarks are separate datasets. Results contains the acquired biological measurements.

The source describes integer arithmetic, but the released figures report simulation benchmarks without a corresponding embedded timing, memory or power measurement.

Dataset provenance: “KT” is the label in the team analysis record; its expansion, thesis author, institution and stable source have not been recovered. The KT scores rest on a corpus readers cannot presently obtain from this release. The separately cited UCI dataset is publicly retrievable. No decoder score on this page is measured AeroSense cell performance.

The fold plots label their uncertainty as “spread”; the source does not specify whether ±0.03 denotes standard deviation or another statistic. We therefore report fold variation, not a confidence interval. New-run seeds, fold assignments and machine-readable predictions are not included. Parameter sweeps selected settings, but a separate nested selection protocol is undocumented; these estimates do not establish performance on a completely untouched deployment population.

KC overlap was not quantified in the continual-learning run, so the contribution of representation overlap to retention remains a question for controlled ablation.

Odor decoding / Conclusion

Conclusion

Whole-day evaluation reveals losses that random exposure splits conceal. Layerwise probes locate the largest measured loss at the learned readout, and reference calibration improves cross-batch classification only with suitable gas and concentration coverage. For AeroSense, the supported next design choice is controlled acquisition and evaluation before increasing network complexity.

Discussion

The strongest predictive benefit comes from receptor sequence within a species. Measured infrared spectra, quantum modes and frozen odor embeddings do not provide a stable gain across models and cohorts, so richer molecular descriptions alone do not solve transfer. The mutation comparisons likewise depend on receptor context and matched evaluation.

The structural calculations add a second constraint: a wider entrance or an apparent geometric tunnel does not guarantee passage of a complete, flexible ligand. Protein and lipid obstacles, conformation choice and relaxation all affect the route. Prediction can prioritize a candidate, but functional change requires a matched receptor assay; geometric access requires a sustainable molecular path.

Receptor engineering / Conclusion

Conclusion

The study narrows receptor candidates with controlled prediction and structural comparisons. Sequence remains the useful predictive baseline, and whole-ligand tests constrain which access routes deserve further examination. The resulting candidates and routes are testable selections, rather than demonstrated improvements in receptor function.

Membrane assumptions

The layer equation is K ∂u/∂t = ∂(DK ∂u/∂x)/∂x, with gas-equivalent concentration u and material concentration c = Ku. It assumes dilute linear transport, instant interfacial partitioning, uniform area and no convection, reaction or cellular consumption. The 243-condition sweep varies membrane thickness, diffusivity and partition coefficients; 74 cases do not reach t90 within seven days. A 20-minute pulse gives a delayed 2.78% peak after 3.92 h in the illustrated case.

Direct ligand pulling and receptor-distance guidance are separate protocols.
