12 / Dry Lab · Decode layer

A fly-inspired spiking network for gas classification

A Brian2 PN→KC→MBON model on the KT Batch1–3 metal-oxide aging corpus — with a pooled baseline and an open question on drift robustness.

Pooled baseline reported · cross-batch drift evaluation not yet performed

Electronic noses often fail as sensors age; insects keep recognizing odors anyway. This page documents a spiking neural network (SNN) organized like the mushroom body and asks whether sparse expand–sparsify–learn coding can help gas classification without an explicit drift-correction stage. The current evidence answers pooled-class separability (macro-F1 0.868, seed 100) — not yet cross-batch drift robustness.

03 · Problem

The Drift Problem

Electronic noses pair chemical-sensor arrays with pattern-recognition algorithms for gas and odor identification — from environmental monitoring to food quality control and disease screening [1] Marco & Gutiérrez-Gálvez, 2012 — full entry in References & Credits. .

Metal-oxide and conducting-polymer arrays share a well-documented failure mode: sensor drift. Responses to the same analyte shift over days to months through poisoning, aging, and environmental fluctuation, so classifiers trained on early measurements degrade on later ones [1] [2] Vergara et al., 2012 — full entry in References & Credits. .

Common mitigations — recalibration with labeled references, classifier ensembles, or explicit drift-correction transforms — help, but usually demand extra calibration data or an explicit model of the drift process itself [2] .

This page’s completed baseline does not claim that sensor-drift robustness has already been demonstrated. That requires a cross-batch or temporal evaluation still marked Planned below.

04 · Biological inspiration

Why the Fly Brain?

Drosophila olfaction faces an analogous reliability problem: recognize odors despite concentration change, background chemistry, and receptor adaptation over a lifetime.

Odor information enters the antennal lobe and is relayed by projection neurons (PNs) to the mushroom body, where a much larger Kenyon cell (KC) population receives sparse, largely random PN→KC connectivity [3] Turner et al., 2008 — full entry in References & Credits. [4] Caron et al., 2013 — full entry in References & Credits. .

Random expansion into a high-dimensional space, combined with global feedback inhibition from the anterior paired lateral (APL) neuron that enforces sparse KC activity, yields decorrelated, near-orthogonal odor codes [5] Lin et al., 2014 — full entry in References & Credits. . Those KC representations are read out by mushroom body output neurons (MBONs) through synapses shaped by learning, including STDP-like plasticity [6] Aso et al., 2014 — full entry in References & Credits. .

The motif — expand, sparsify, then learn the readout — already travels beyond neuroscience: it appears in fly-inspired similarity search [7] Dasgupta et al., 2017 — full entry in References & Credits. and in insect-inspired chemical-sensor classifiers [8] Schmuker & Schneider, 2007 — full entry in References & Credits. 05 · Computational translation

From Sensor Signal to Spikes

One gas exposure becomes a sparse PN spike pattern the SNN can train on. The walkthrough below is for a non-ML reader; expandable panels hold the preprocessing detail a computational reviewer needs.

Leakage Guard

PCA and preprocessing parameters are fit on training data only.

Held-out Test exposures never enter the PCA fit [10] Jolliffe, 2002 — full entry in References & Credits. . Missing-value medians used for imputation follow the same train-only discipline in the documented method. This is an evaluation-integrity rule, not a drift-robustness claim.

Data pipeline

Follow one exposure from the raw KT recording into the SNN. Select a step for a plain-language note.

Raw KT recording

A multi-channel metal-oxide time series for one experimental run on the KT Batch1–3 corpus. Recordings alternate clean-air and gas-exposure segments before any feature is computed.

Feature extraction — what “delta” means

For every Test segment, each sensor’s response is measured against a baseline from the preceding Dilute (clean-air) segment. Three signed deltas are taken per sensor, plus baseline-relative counterparts.

Full preprocessing details
  1. Segment structure. Each raw recording alternates Dilute (clean-air) and Test (gas-exposure) segments.
  2. Baseline. For every Test segment, baseline = median of the preceding Dilute segment’s final readings.
  3. Per-sensor deltas. Extract signed delta-median, delta-steady, and delta-peak, plus their baseline-relative counterparts → one feature vector spanning all 14 sensors.
  4. Fault filtering. Exclude known-faulty Batch2 devices 1–2 before analysis.
  5. Missing values. Median-impute missing feature entries (train-side discipline under the Leakage Guard).
  6. PCA. Fit PCA on the training split only; project to pca_dim = 8 in the v0 baseline [10] .
  7. PN discretization. Each PC axis → pn_bins = 20 bins; one active PN index per axis → sparse combinatorial PN spike pattern (antennal-lobe / glomerular analogy).
  8. Hand-off. PN spikes drive the Brian2 PN→KC→MBON network (Inside the network).

No closed-form feature equations appear in model_content.md beyond these operational definitions; the schematic above is the primary visual explanation.

07 · Network

Inside the PN–KC–APL–MBON Network

The spiking network is implemented in the Brian2 simulator [11] Stimberg et al., 2019 — full entry in References & Credits. using conductance-based leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) neurons with AMPA/GABA/ACh synaptic dynamics.

  • PN

    Projection-neuron analogue · sparse combinatorial spike code from binned PCA axes

    Feeds the expansion stage; one active bin index per principal-component axis.

  • KC

    Kenyon-cell expansion · kc_num = 1000

    Sparse fixed-weight random PN→KC connectivity (p_pn_kc = 0.05), mirroring divergent wiring in Drosophila [3] [4] .

  • APL-like inhibition

    Global feedback · sparsify and decorrelate

    A global inhibitory pathway (w_apl_kc, lateral) feeds back onto all KCs, analogous to APL [5] . An intrinsic-plasticity term (ip) slowly adapts each KC’s firing threshold to recent activity so sparsity stays bounded.

  • MBON readout

    Learned classifier · STDP on KC→MBON weights

    All-to-all excitatory KC→MBON synapses evolve under an event-driven, pair-based STDP rule (learning_rate) [12] Song et al., 2000 — full entry in References & Credits. — the same “fixed random expansion, learned readout” strategy used at real KC→MBON synapses [6] .

08 · Evaluation protocol

Training & Evaluation

The v0 baseline learns only the KC→MBON readout with STDP, then scores held-out exposures. The split used here is pooled and stratified by gas label — it does not test temporal or cross-batch sensor drift (Drift Robustness).

Evaluation protocol

Split strategy
Stratified train/test split by gas label on the pooled Batch1–3 exposures. Not a train-earlier / test-later or cross-batch drift protocol. Train/test ratio and exposure count N are not stated in model_content.md and are not invented here.
Seed
seed = 100 (single fixed seed for reported baseline runs)
Training cycles
cycles = 300 presentations; only KC→MBON weights update via STDP
Test iterations
test_iterations = 50 presentations per checkpoint
Decision rule
Predicted class = MBON unit with the strongest response (argmax)
Metrics scored
Macro-averaged precision, recall, and F1 per training cycle

Logging intent (when code is released): fully resolved config, spike rasters, per-class reports via model_evaluation/performance_analysis.py ((script not in this repository).

What this split does not show

A pooled stratified random split answers: “Can the SNN separate the five gases when train and test draws mix the same overall pool?”

It does not answer: “Does accuracy hold when the sensor array has aged into a later batch?” That requires the planned cross-batch / temporal evaluation.

Do not read F1 = 0.868 as drift-robustness evidence.

What Did the Model Teach Us?

Keep a pooled reference baseline

Implemented
Evidence
Fly-SNN v0 reaches macro-F1 = 0.868 on a pooled stratified split at seed 100 (model_content.md; Preliminary — see Evidence A).
Interpretation
Under mixed-pool conditions, the PN→KC→MBON pipeline can separate the five KT gas classes well enough to serve as a text-documented comparison anchor for later encoding, connectivity, and sampling changes. Full run-level reproducibility still needs archived configs, seeds beyond 100, and metric files (Reproduce).
Design consequence
Retain the documented v0 preset (PCA-8, KC=1000, p_pn_kc=0.05, w_kc_mbon=10, learning_rate=0.025) as the comparison anchor. Do not replace it with the Engineering w_kc_mbon=13 candidate until multi-seed / k-fold gates pass.

Source: model_content.md; candidate gate cross-check — Engineering · Dry Lab.

Do not treat pooled F1 as drift proof

Proposed
Evidence
model_content.md states that cross-batch / temporal drift evaluation is not yet performed for this SNN (Evidence C).
Interpretation
Pooled random-split performance answers gas separability in a mixed pool. It does not answer how much baseline / batch drift the classifier can tolerate after sensors age.
Design consequence
Validation strategy: require an explicit train-earlier / test-later (or cross-batch) protocol before any public robustness claim. Decision pending validation on how much drift AeroSense can tolerate.

Source: model_content.md method + limitations framing.

Require multi-seed reporting before promotion

Standing rule · v0 multi-seed still pending
Evidence
Dry Lab Cycle 0 documented that a single-seed F1 near ~0.90 was luck; a 3-seed mean framing was closer to ~0.85 on the configs tested (Engineering · Dry Lab). The Model v0 headline remains single-seed (100).
Interpretation
Seed variance can dominate apparent gains. A “better” preset is not trustworthy until across-seed statistics exist.
Design consequence
Standing validation rule: do not promote a candidate over v0 on a single seed. Multi-seed uncertainty for the published 0.868 baseline itself remains Decision pending validation (Evidence D).

Source: Engineering Dry Lab Cycle 0 (wiki-documented integrity lesson).

Do not rank sensor/receptor channels from unstandardized PCA yet

Proposed
Evidence
Engineering Cycle 0 found the SNN PCA path lacked StandardScaler, so sensor-importance rankings tracked raw magnitude (e.g. S13 vs S6) rather than clean separability, disagreeing with standardized SVM / ANOVA-F diagnostics.
Interpretation
Without scale control, “which channels matter?” answers can be artifacts. That blocks a trustworthy answer to how many distinguishable receptor/sensor channels are useful for AeroSense panel design.
Design consequence
Queue adding StandardScaler before PCA and re-run LOSO / ranking comparisons before using channel importance to change receptor-panel strategy. Decision pending validation.

Source: Engineering · Dry Lab Cycle 0 (queued redesign; not closed).

Hold hardware precision requirements until re-measurement

Decision pending validation
Evidence
Engineering Cycle 1 explored ADC / state quantization as a precision budget for an eventual hardware e-nose, then found quantize_states was scheduled on the wrong Brian2 clock — prior bit-sweep conclusions are invalidated pending rerun.
Interpretation

Do not set Hardware bit-depth or analog-fidelity requirements from those invalidated numbers. Re-measure after the clock fix, then revisit Read-layer specs. Decision pending validation — awaiting re-measurement (not yet AeroSense fluorescence data).

Model ↔ Experiment Feedback Loop

AeroSense is Sense → Read → Decode → Act. The Model is the Decode layer: its job is eventually to read receptor-response patterns from the biological sensing layer — not to stop at an external metal-oxide benchmark.

KT Batch1–3 is a benchmark / surrogate development dataset used to build and stress-test the Decode pipeline while aging/batch structure is known. It is not final biological validation of AeroSense OR/GCaMP assays.

13 · Project integration

From Public Sensor Data to AeroSense

AeroSense’s product stack is Sense → Read → Decode → Act: Description, Hardware, Design. The evidence→decision cards and Model ↔ Experiment Feedback Loop above state what has — and has not — transferred from the KT surrogate into project choices.

The Model track currently develops the Decode idea on KT Batch1–3, a real metal-oxide aging corpus. That is honest surrogate engineering, not a substitute for decoding AeroSense receptor-response patterns.

Transfer to AeroSense assays will require a versioned fluorescence / receptor-response feature matrix (planned export path EXP-AS-07 and Results · Model results), the same leakage-safe split discipline, and an explicit statement of domain shift between metal-oxide voltages and GCaMP-derived features.

No closed fluorescence-Model feedback loop is claimed on this page: there is not yet dated evidence that this decoder changed receptor selection or hardware sampling based on AeroSense assay metrics.

Seed-to-seed variance of the v0 preset; whether any single-seed candidate (including Engineering’s gated w_kc_mbon=13 / 0.8870) is stable (Evidence D).
Next validation
Multi-seed / k-fold distribution for v0 before promoting alternatives.

KT metal-oxide benchmark ≠ AeroSense biological validation

Current evidence
Methods and results on this page use KT Batch1–3 as a surrogate aging corpus for Decode development.
What remains uncertain
Transfer to receptor-fluorescence feature distributions from AeroSense OR/GCaMP assays (domain shift, channel count, noise structure).
Next validation
Repeat the protocol on a versioned in-house matrix (planned path EXP-AS-07 / Results · Model).

Missing matched baselines, ablations, robustness, and per-class reports

Whether fly-inspired motifs outperform simple classifiers on the same features; which motifs drive gains; error structure across gas classes; tolerance to noise / offset / mixtures.
Next validation
Archive matched ML baselines, architecture ablations, robustness suite, and per-class reports under a pinned environment.
